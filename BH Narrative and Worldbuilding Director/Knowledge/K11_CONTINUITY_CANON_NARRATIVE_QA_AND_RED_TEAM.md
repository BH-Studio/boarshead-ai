# Continuity, Canon, Narrative QA, and Creative Red Team

## Canon classes
### HARD CANON
Explicitly approved and expensive to change: world rules, protagonist identity, historical events, major deaths, central relationships, ending facts, etc.

### SOFT CANON
Approved but intentionally tunable: minor dates, incidental names, local details, exact quest order, noncritical backstory.

### OPEN
Provisional ideas, alternatives, unknowns, unexplained mysteries, deferred decisions.

## Canon ledger fields
For important entries:
- ID/topic;
- statement;
- class: hard/soft/open;
- approval/source;
- effective point in timeline;
- who knows it;
- affected characters/factions/quests;
- contradictions/notes;
- change history.

## Knowledge boundaries
Track separately:
- **author truth**;
- **player knowledge**;
- **character knowledge**;
- **faction/public belief**;
- **misinformation/lie**.

Many apparent continuity errors are actually knowledge-boundary failures.

## Continuity dimensions
As needed track:
- chronology and ages;
- geography/travel time;
- character location;
- injuries/resources;
- relationship state;
- who is alive/present;
- faction control;
- quest completion;
- discoveries/knowledge;
- promises/debts;
- branch-specific truths;
- world-event aftermath;
- terminology/naming.

## Canon change control
When an approved fact changes, report:
`OLD CANON → PROPOSED NEW CANON → CREATIVE REASON → RIPPLE EFFECTS → REQUIRED REWRITES/RETCONS → RISKS → USER APPROVAL`.
Do not silently revise old facts to make a new idea fit.

## Creative Red Team mandate
After a preferred candidate exists, temporarily assume it is wrong and try to break it.

### Structural attacks
- inciting event does not actually force change;
- protagonist could leave without cost;
- midpoint changes nothing;
- climax solves a different problem than the story set up;
- return/integration is missing;
- twists depend on withheld author information rather than misinterpreted evidence.

### Character attacks
- protagonist is passive;
- motivation changes for plot convenience;
- villain acts stupidly to let plot happen;
- companion arc exists only in dialogue;
- redemption lacks accountability/cost;
- supposedly different characters share one voice.

### Interactive attacks
- choice labels misrepresent outcomes;
- branches erase choice history at rejoin;
- story assumes fixed mission order;
- player can know facts the protagonist inexplicably ignores;
- protagonist violates player-selected identity;
- branch complexity grows faster than narrative value.

### World attacks
- institution exists without material/social cause;
- lore contradicts everyday life;
- magic/technology solves problems arbitrarily;
- cultures are monocultures;
- geography/economy/travel do not support plot;
- hidden world rule appears only to solve climax.

### Emotional/theme attacks
- story tells a theme gameplay contradicts;
- emotional payoff lacks setup;
- relentless intensity causes numbness;
- twist destroys rather than enriches attachment;
- ending resolves plot but not relationship/theme.

### Authenticity/derivative attacks
- surface aesthetics borrowed without context;
- real group is coded as inherently monstrous/corrupt;
- trauma is used as instant depth;
- concept is too close to a single recognizable source;
- archetype has become cliché because no project-specific pressure transforms it.

Classify findings:
- `CRITICAL` — candidate should not be canonized as-is;
- `MAJOR` — substantial revision or explicit acceptance needed;
- `ACCEPTABLE RISK` — known tradeoff;
- `UNKNOWN / NEEDS VALIDATION` — evidence required;
- `NO ISSUE`.

## Narrative QA toolbox
### Causality chain
Rewrite macro outline with "because of that / therefore" links. Investigate long runs of "and then."

### Setup/payoff ledger
Track setup, expected payoff window, actual payoff, branch dependencies.

### Character pressure test
Put character in a dilemma between two established values. Does the chosen behavior make sense and reveal something?

### Blind voice test
Remove names from representative dialogue.

### Choice/consequence audit
For each major choice map intention, response, state, delayed consequence, acknowledgment, payoff.

### Branch matrix
Enumerate important state/route combinations and test continuity.

### Open-order test
For open-world content, run representative sequences in unusual orders.

### Emotional beat map
Chart intended emotional state and pressure across main arc/questline.

### Narrative playtest hypotheses
Examples:
- "Players will understand that this choice is relational, not a route split."
- "Players will suspect Character X but still trust them enough to continue."
- "The midpoint reveal will reinterpret earlier clues rather than feel arbitrary."
Define what feedback would disconfirm the hypothesis.

## Validation honesty
A good internal review is not player evidence. Mark untested claims `UNVALIDATED` until actual readthrough/playtest/target-player feedback exists.
