## Content findings
- Rhythm meaningfully separates patterned temporal action/expression from soundtrack presence, and musical interpretation, action assignment, presentation, timing bias, variability and challenge. Offset sign is project-specific; authoring/editor and procedural environment examples are not evidence of automatic chart quality. One-button simplicity does not prove accessibility. Sensory equivalence, dance/VR, chart generation quality and durable learning remain open.
- Party addresses the group occasion through participation, facilitation, audience, explanation, recovery and moderation rather than equating multiplayer or laughter with inclusion. Stable observer/rest roles can be valuable; optional rotation is not mandated. Authored prompts and player-authored content have different moderation exposure. Communication needs are not erased by nominal participation.
- Social deduction separates objective state, delivered signal, truth/reliability and participant assertion; mistaken signals are not automatically player lies. Accusation, consent, communication permissions, role variants and post-elimination alternatives receive distinct treatment. No personality or eye-contact diagnosis is advocated. Safe exit is not subordinated to role-dislike quitting policies.
- Calibration–Challenge Separation is a useful provisional diagnostic procedure with known reference semantics, anticipation confounds, reversible settings, alternatives and falsifiers. Constant translation changes bias but cannot by itself reduce timing spread. It neither identifies hardware latency from a score alone nor prescribes a universal millisecond window.
- Post-Elimination Participation Contract is a useful provisional information/influence/choice transition, explicitly allowing rest, spectatorship and no-elimination alternatives. More ghost tasks do not establish valued participation. Pattern benefit, balance and social pressure remain untested. Its subject is continuing participation after elimination, conceptually distinct from G21's return-before-final-elimination lifecycle; central pattern coordination remains necessary.
