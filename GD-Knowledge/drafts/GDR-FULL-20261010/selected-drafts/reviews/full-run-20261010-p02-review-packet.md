## Contradictions and unresolved interpretations

The following `P02-CON-*` labels are **packet-local issue keys**, not new global register allocations. They are proposed Coordination entries only; `REGISTERS.md` was not edited.

### P02-CON-01 — Stronger feedback versus conditional effects

**Propositions:** “stronger feedback always improves feel” versus context-specific measured benefits and costs. **Records:** TOP-02 C-02/C-03/C-07/C-08; sources 011 and 012. The amplification comparison in 011 and haptic-presence comparison in 012 differ in intervention, task and measures; neither supports a universal monotonic intensity rule. Conversely, they do not establish that minimal feedback is always preferable.

**Treatment:** compare semantics, task visibility, control and desired experience before intensity. Preserve non-significant outcomes as inconclusive regarding equivalence. **Needed:** targeted ablations with actual task/access contexts. **Owner:** Presentation-and-Interaction evidence review; Coordination for any shared-register entry. **Status:** universal unsupported; application tradeoff unresolved.

### P02-CON-02 — Redundancy versus competing attention

**Propositions:** alternative channels can preserve access when a route is unavailable; activating every channel/repetition can compete with the current task. **Records:** TOP-03 C-01/C-07/C-08/C-10; PAT-02 C-01–C-07; sources 005–010. These are not logically contradictory: selectable access, essential timing and automatic decoration are different decisions.

**Treatment:** specify facts and supported routes, then test simultaneous presentation separately from availability. No universal best channel count or emotional-equivalence claim. **Needed:** participant/context-specific access and attention comparison. **Status:** contextual tension retained, not empirically resolved.

### P02-CON-03 — Responsiveness, buffering and parity

**Propositions:** early input retention may prevent missed intentions; retaining or delaying input can also produce stale or late action. **Records:** TOP-01 C-01/C-04/C-05/C-08/C-09; sources 002/003/014. Source 003 uses buffering for a recent eligible request; source 014 uses deliberate delay. These are different mechanisms. Identical numerical settings across devices also do not establish functional parity.

**Treatment:** name timing stage, eligibility and cancellation; keep delay measurement separate from accepted-intent counts. **Needed:** an action-specific comparison across the actual devices/activation methods. **Status:** terminology separated; optimal local policy unknown.

### P02-CON-04 — Source 013 interruption-label conflict

**Observed inconsistency:** the publisher HTML Table 1 assigns its higher-accuracy rows to “Interrupted,” whereas the surrounding text says interruption lowered accuracy and gives the larger paired-haptic advantage to interrupted performance. The displayed lower-accuracy rows are labeled “Non-interrupted.” **Records:** TOP-03 C-05/C-06 and source 013, Methods interrupted-item definition, Results/Table 1.

**Treatment:** do not silently swap labels, claim which representation is correct or quote a resolved condition-specific magnitude. Retain the authors’ reported tests only with the caveat, and do not use the paper as decisive evidence for the P02 recipe. This may be a presentation/reporting error; its cause is unknown. **Needed:** correction/version history, author clarification or auditable data. **Status:** open; quantitative condition-specific use withheld.

### P02-CON-05 — Source 011 Model B population description

**Unreconciled reporting ambiguity:** §3.7 describes Model B as using amplified conditions; §4.3 labels Model B `n=1699`, the total analyzed sample including the standard condition. This inspection did not audit code or data to determine how the model was actually fitted. **Records:** TOP-02 C-08 and source 011, PDF pp. 8–9.

**Treatment:** do not “correct” the paper or infer invalid statistics. The draft’s clean amplification counterexample uses Model A, explicitly `n=678`. Outcome-specific feedback remains a reasoned design proposal, not a claim whose universal validity depends on interpreting Model B. **Needed:** analysis/code clarification before detailed Model B effect claims. **Status:** open ambiguity, not adjudicated methodological failure.
