## Framed research questions and synthesis decisions

**Roguelike:** Is the operative promise systemic exploration and adaptive planning under a declared run boundary, a historical membership claim, or a broad market label? Inspect traditional criteria without turning them into a standard. Separate variable spaces from strategic variation, discrete decisions from action duration, and character loss from all possible persistence. The author’s proposed working profile remains revisable.

**Roguelite:** What survives an attempt and what purpose does that retention serve? Distinguish numerical power, opportunities, narrative, challenge access, knowledge and ordinary settings. Do not attribute later success or return solely to progression when practice, seeds, pool changes, assistance and survivor selection also differ.

**Deckbuilder:** Is composition changed during play, and when does that change affect available actions or terminal value? Distinguish ownership, draw and discard zones, non-shuffling defaults, information rights, compulsory adaptation and optional composition control. Do not equate arrival probability with card utility or more options with more viable choice.

**Two extracted patterns:** the run-state pattern adds a typed transition across an attempt; the deck-cycle pattern adds a conditional mechanical composition decision. Both are hypotheses with prerequisites, exits, alternatives and explicit rejection criteria. Neither creates a new general consent or comparison-display canon. Coordination may merge or narrow them after overlap review.

## Author self-review — adversarial pass

**Reviewer identity and independence:** the same G16 authoring assistant performed this content/packaging self-review. No separate independent reviewer, independent agent, participant or empirical replication occurred. Persona language is not used to imply independence.

**Strongest recommendation challenged:** the candidates favor making state/cycle consequences explicit enough to support an informed choice. The strongest opposing case is that an intricate but wanted discovery/adaptation game can become a sequence of optimizer dialogs; a meaningful commitment or compulsory awkward acquisition can be the experience, not an error. A perfectly accurate explanation may also overwhelm the people it is meant to help. The drafts therefore recommend a truthful minimum when sufficient, distinguish account consequences from fictional unknowns, and require a comparison that can reject extra disclosure/control. No recommendation is elevated to a universal accessibility or ethical rule by repetition.

**Possible overreach in progression:** treating accumulated power only as a confound would bias research against players who value accumulation itself. The roguelite file keeps that preference as a substantive countercase and asks for separate measures of learning, progression pleasure, understanding and burden. No source here proves persistent growth is necessary, beneficial, harmful or inevitably exploitative.

**Possible overreach in deck arithmetic:** a correct target-arrival probability can be misused as a card-value score. The final drafts restrict the toy model to a fresh uniform pool and fixed draw, distinguish legal play/utility, and include resource and ownership-based scoring counterexamples. No simulator or player result was fabricated. Larger pools are not treated as automatically good or bad.

**Source challenge:** creator product descriptions and manuals dominate many genre claims; they are strong for the words/rules documented and weak for effects. The external experiment is a short platformer study, not direct metaprogression/deckbuilding validation. The mirrored Aeon PDF is not visually authenticated; its default no-shuffling language is qualified by explicit exception text. Wilson’s thesis results are excluded because only the catalog was accessed. All source IDs/dates/versions and independence relationships are retained.

**Boundary challenge:** the chosen roguelike/roguelite split is an analytical tool, not an approved universal taxonomy. NetHack exceptions, divergent labels and hybrid overlaps remain visible. No genre is defined by presumed age, disability, willingness to grind, platform or multiplayer mode. Noncombat and competitive/cooperative deckbuilding are present, but no population representativeness is claimed.

**Duplication challenge:** the existing safe-exit and opportunity-cost patterns were inspected as actual selected bytes. G16 narrows its contributions to run-state transformation and deck circulation, respectively. That does not decide canonical ownership: the reviewer may request a merge or retain the material as genre adaptations. The four additional ideas remain unallocated questions, not hidden extra pattern files.
