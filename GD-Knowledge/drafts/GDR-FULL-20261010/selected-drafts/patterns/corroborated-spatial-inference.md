# Corroborated spatial inference

> **Research candidate, not released guidance.** Version 0.1.0, candidate revision `initial-1`. Author self-review only; independent evidence review has not occurred. All worked designs and validation protocols below are illustrative or proposed. No W03 game build, prototype, participant session or playtest was executed. External creator accounts are not observations performed by this workspace.

## Purpose

Help a player form a **better-grounded, revisable account** of an event or relationship from evidence encountered in a place. The intended outcome is not automatically the author's preferred answer. It may be recognition that an early account is uncertain or that a new observation warrants a different question. [C-01]

This is a newly named W03 synthesis, not a named technique attributed to Jenkins, Smith/Worch or Fernández-Vara. Its causal efficacy remains a hypothesis. The recipe is supported as a reasoned extension of their conceptual and practitioner material, not by a matched experiment. [C-01; GDR-SRC-20261010-W03-001; GDR-SRC-20261010-W03-002; GDR-SRC-20261010-W03-003]

## Context and prerequisites

Use only when interpretation of evidence is part of the intended player experience. A purely evocative arrangement, an abstract play space or a direct operational instruction may not need this pattern. [C-01; C-07]

The authoring context must supply a question worth considering, recognizable evidence relations, and enough fictional context to distinguish at least some plausible accounts. Evidence should be reachable through relevant routes and usable access modes. There must be a decision about what can remain uncertain, what consequences a mistaken interpretation has, and whether the player can revise it. Required evidence cannot depend silently on specialized outside knowledge. These are proposed prerequisites, not released studio requirements. [C-02; C-04; C-05]

When visits can occur in different orders, observations need a usable history: what was encountered, what may have changed, and what comparison remains warranted. This is a player-facing continuity requirement, not an instruction to choose any engine component or persistence design. [C-06]

## Mechanism and evidence

| Proposed causal link | Supporting basis | Evidence limit and competing explanation |
|---|---|---|
| A relation is encountered and perceived. | Relational staging motivates the recipe; access guidance addresses unavailable cue channels. [C-01, C-05] | Salience or route placement, not evidential structure, may determine what is noticed. |
| The relation constrains possible accounts. | W03 applies trace-based interpretation to a stated question. [C-02, C-03] | Shared appearance or duplicated testimony may create false corroboration. |
| A further observation distinguishes accounts. | This is the pattern's hypothesized intervention, not an observed result. [C-01] | More information may merely make the intended answer more obvious, without better reasoning. |
| The player can revise an account or choose a grounded next action. | Separating observations, explanations and action consequences supplies a testable bridge. [C-03, C-04] | The player may infer something defensible that the author did not anticipate. |
| The process is worth the attention it costs. | No direct supporting outcome data inspected. [C-07] | A direct statement, unresolved mood or simpler scene may better serve the experience. |

Do not collapse these links into “environmental storytelling increases engagement.” Detecting an object, understanding its relation and valuing the resulting interpretation are different outcomes. The pattern is rejected or narrowed if the latter links fail even when detection succeeds. [C-01; C-07]

## Recipe

Proposed process only; no prototype was produced or tested in W03.

1. **Specify the question and its stakes.** Write the operational distinction, optional meanings and consequences of being wrong. Separate what must be knowable from what the player is free to interpret. Do not invent a puzzle where the purpose is atmosphere. [C-04]
2. **Separate observation from explanation.** For each candidate trace, record what the player can actually perceive, what might explain it and what additional assumptions each explanation requires. Keep author knowledge outside the player-observation column. [C-03]
3. **Identify discriminating evidence, not a numerical quota.** Select an observation that changes the plausibility of competing accounts. Another copy of the same claim is not automatically independent support. A direct observation may make corroboration unnecessary; in that case simplify or exit the pattern. [C-01, C-02]
4. **Stage the relation where it can be compared.** Place or otherwise present the relevant features so that their relationship is available from plausible approaches. Avoid adding unrelated detail to simulate evidential richness. Keep an alternative arrival order in the authoring check. [C-01, C-06]
5. **Provide equivalent grounds, not necessarily identical presentation.** An alternate description should convey the relevant relation while leaving the intended reasoning open. Where the goal is access to an explicit fact instead, say it directly. Coordinate sensory and linguistic design rather than imposing visual parity as the only goal. [C-05]
6. **Preserve uncertainty honestly.** Mark an in-world record as testimony when it is testimony. Make an intentionally unreliable source distinguishable from an unexplained inconsistency where the distinction matters. Do not claim player agreement proves creator intent. [C-02, C-03]
7. **Check interaction and continuity promises.** Ask whether the traces imply actions that the player cannot meaningfully attempt, whether a later state contradicts an earlier observation without explanation, and whether the key relation can be recovered after interruption. Describe the desired player experience; resolve mechanics and implementation elsewhere. [C-04, C-06]
8. **Compare against less elaborate alternatives.** Propose a matched unrelated-prop arrangement, a direct explanation or an optional rather than required inference. Evaluate evidential grounding and value, not merely answer accuracy. [C-07]
9. **Exit, simplify or retain conditionally.** Remove the requirement when important evidence cannot be accessed, the distinction depends on undisclosed world rules, the scene cannot sustain its observation history, or the cost of interpretation exceeds its purpose. Retain multiple accounts when ambiguity is intentional. Only a local test or explicit later studio decision can justify the tradeoff; this draft does neither. [C-04, C-06, C-07]

### Adjustable variables

| Variable | What can change | What must be protected |
|---|---|---|
| Evidential specificity | Broad association through a directly diagnostic relation. | Do not silently change the question or the stakes. |
| Independence of grounds | Another trace, another source of testimony, or a direct test. | Distinguish genuinely different grounds from repetition. |
| Spatial separation | Same comparison area through encounters across visits. | Needed relationships must remain recoverable. |
| Delivery channels | Visual relation, description, sound-supported observation or other appropriate access route. | Preserve the needed information, not a preferred sensory modality. |
| Reliability | Honest, mistaken, outdated, staged or ambiguous evidence. | Keep the reliability question fair to the intended design rather than arbitrary. |
| Cost of a mistaken account | No consequence, reversible detour or a deliberate commitment. | The player-facing grounds must match the consequence the design intends. |
| Interpretive closure | Settled operational fact through unresolved personal meaning. | Do not require closure merely to satisfy a clue checklist. |

These are local design variables, not numerical prescriptions. [C-01–C-07]

## Worked examples

### Illustrative: the interrupted kiln repair

**Invented example; no game build or test.** A player inspecting a community workshop wonders whether a kiln stopped before or after a support was replaced. The operational goal is to choose the more relevant maintenance record, not to identify a culprit. [C-01, C-04]

The visible observation is a heat stain on an older panel that does not continue onto an adjacent replacement support. An installation record gives a date but is copied from an uncertain notebook. A stored offcut matches the replacement material. The authoring record lists rival explanations: replacement after use, cleaning after use, or a different heating event. The evidence is not treated as universally diagnostic material science; the fictional context must establish any needed assumptions.

The initial scene puts the panel and support in a comparable view. The offcut is on an optional service route, so finding it cannot be essential to avoiding an unrecoverable failure. An observation description conveys the discontinuity of the stain without asserting a cause. A later record can qualify the date, but a copied version of the earlier notebook is not counted as new independent evidence. [C-02, C-05, C-06]

The proposed grounded outcome is “replacement after heating is plausible, but the notebook does not settle it.” The player may choose which archive to inspect next. A version that forces a single culpability conclusion would exceed the available evidence. A direct label explaining the replacement date would be a legitimate simpler design if reconstruction has no player-valued purpose. [C-03, C-04, C-07]

### Illustrative transfer and boundary

An arrangement of worn benches and repaired tools might support a question about repeated shared use without any crime, disaster or puzzle. Transfer requires the same mechanism—interpretable relations and a reason to consider them—not a similar-looking room. Conversely, an assortment deliberately left open to personal meaning has no competing causal accounts that need discrimination. In that case use an evocative arrangement and omit this pattern. [C-01, C-07]

## Tradeoffs and alternatives

Corroboration can create room for revision but also create redundant exposition. Spatial separation can reward revisitation but demand memory or repeated travel. Optional inspection preserves refusal but cannot be relied on as the sole route to a mandatory fact. Alternate descriptions can support access while making some relations more explicit than a visual presentation. None of these costs is eliminated by adding more props. [C-01, C-02, C-04–C-07]

Alternatives include direct exposition, a character's explicitly uncertain account, one unmistakable observation, player-authored notes, an optional interpretive scene, or no narrative explanation. Choose among them by the intended experience and locally evaluated costs, not by assuming spatial delivery is inherently more sophisticated. [C-07]

## Failure cases

| Warning sign | Candidate diagnosis | Possible correction |
|---|---|---|
| Players repeat the conclusion but cannot cite observations. | Answer cueing rather than grounded inference. | Remove the answer-like hint; compare with direct exposition to test whether reconstruction matters. |
| Players offer a defensible account that is punished. | The author has assumed unavailable evidence or world rules. | Supply a usable distinction, accept the account or reduce the consequence. |
| Players count several records as several independent witnesses. | Duplicated origin is hidden. | Clarify provenance or replace redundant testimony with another kind of observation. |
| An access mode conveys only object names or gives away the solution. | Information relation is missing or overinterpreted. | Describe the relation; evaluate the reasoning goal separately. |
| Returning invalidates earlier observations without explanation. | Observation history is unreliable in an unintended way. | Explain the change, preserve the relevant fact or remove the dependence on return. |
| Every object is treated as a required clue. | The design or surrounding incentives teach exhaustive inspection. | Reduce requirements and distinguish optional meaning from progression needs. |

These are proposed diagnostic hypotheses, not recorded player incidents. [C-02–C-07]

## Counterexamples and do not use conditions

Do not use this recipe to make a purely reflective space answer a single puzzle, to imply a culture or identity is evidence of wrongdoing, or to conceal a mandatory action behind an inaccessible detail. Do not present a sequence of plausible interpretations as verified author intent. The recommendation can also be inappropriate where uncertain commitment is explicitly the intended choice and resolving the uncertainty would remove that choice. [C-03, C-04, C-05, C-07]

The strongest opposing case is that **more corroboration destroys the desired ambiguity**. A small number of evocative traces may sustain personal interpretation better than a carefully discriminating cluster. This is not a defect to solve by maximizing evidence; it is a reason to select another technique. [C-07]

## Playtest

**Proposed / not performed.** Primary question: does the discriminating arrangement improve warranted interpretation and a valued next action, rather than merely recognition of the intended answer? [C-01, C-07]

Compare relational staging with unrelated but similarly visible placement. Keep the same relevant objects, vocabulary, traversal burden and access support where feasible. Use a direct-explanation alternative only as a distinct condition because it changes the activity. Avoid testing the same mystery twice with the same participant after the answer is known. Recruit for actual language, sensory access, navigation familiarity and interpretive context; define consent, privacy, recruitment and sample planning before any future test.

Allow unprompted exploration, then ask what was observed, what account was formed, what remains uncertain and what could change the account. Accept defensible alternatives. Measure observation/interpretation separation, unsupported certainty, accessible availability of the key relation, chosen next action, burden and player-valued interest. Coders should not be told that only the author's preferred answer counts.

Salience, prior knowledge, wording and inaccessible presentation can explain differences. Set local rejection criteria before testing. Reject or simplify the pattern if grounding does not improve, a simpler account serves the same goal with less burden, or any apparent benefit depends on excluding relevant access/language groups. Reject mandatory use if reasonable uncertainty is being penalized instead of intentionally explored. No percentage, timing threshold or successful test result is asserted. [C-04–C-07]
