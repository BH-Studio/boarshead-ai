# Extraction game

**Version 0.1.0 · initial-capture-1 · draft · coverage open.** This is a broader structural genre entry, not a synonym for extraction shooter. No live raid, inventory transaction, match recording, economic simulation or participant test was performed.

## Definition and contested membership

For this entry, an **extraction game** organizes repeated excursions around exposing or acquiring value and deciding whether, when and how to move it across a consequential return/retention boundary. Value might be equipment, cargo, a recruited character, a recorded discovery or another explicitly retained state. Returning matters because some gain or exposure resolves differently on success, failure or an alternative exit. This is a working analytic definition, not an industry-wide formal standard. [C-01]

A last-player-standing match with an evacuation animation is not necessarily extraction-centered. An adventure’s return journey is not sufficient without consequential excursion/retention decisions. A roguelite can overlap, but a mandatory ending that banks every reward automatically may leave a different choice structure. A survival game can overlap, but indefinite living in one world need not have the same excursion boundary. Examine the actual state transition rather than settle membership from a store tag. [C-01]

Shooting, military fiction, PvP, permanent character death, a shared market and seasonal wipes are **not logical necessities** of the proposed definition. Some implementations combine them; others can use cooperative salvage, evasion, negotiation or delivery. Lethal Company is included as a documented non-shooting-centered salvage/return comparison, not proof that every one of its mechanics matches an extraction-shooter template. [C-01, C-06]

Distinguish **retained in-game knowledge** from ordinary player learning: every repeatable game can teach the person something, so memory alone cannot make it an extraction game. A hypothetical research dossier whose safe return changes future options has a specific retention rule; remembering a route does not by itself supply one. Cargo extraction, character extraction and information delivery may have different conditions and beneficiaries. State them separately. [C-01, C-08]

## Custom research instructions

Start with a state table: prepared possessions; borrowed/protected items; carried unbanked gains; already retained gains; consumables; mission information; and entitlements. For each, record entry, loss, return, partial return, disconnect, teammate recovery and re-entry behavior. Do not use “lose everything” when exceptions exist, or “safe” without identifying safe from which failure. [C-02, C-04, C-08, C-12]

Investigate both shooting and nonshooting implementations, solo/team differences, contested and environmental exits, persistent and run-limited advantages, and optional versus mandatory high-stake areas. For a hybrid, keep combat execution questions in the shooter/tactical guidance and retention questions here. Do not assume the emotional goal is always tension; voluntary exploration, mastery and cooperative logistics can impose different requirements. [C-01, C-06, C-07, C-11]

Primary-source routes are versioned extraction/loss rules, official economy and patch explanations, identified scenarios and original research with methods appropriate to the claimed effect. Patch intent is not patch outcome. Survey sentiment is not a controlled estimate of churn, compulsion or fairness. Include economy failures, deliberately retained uncertainty, acceptable disengagement and cases where insurance or free equipment harms another intended property. [C-03, C-05, C-07, C-12, C-13]

Specify misleading comparisons to reject: persistence is not inherently depth; more painful loss is not inherently meaningful commitment; more returns are not necessarily healthier engagement; more exits are not necessarily more agency; a free kit is not proof of fair access; and a successful commercial extraction shooter does not establish that guns are a genre prerequisite. [C-01, C-07, C-09–C-13]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Candidate promises include bringing something valuable home, deciding how much exposure is enough, making preparation matter, recovering from setbacks and coordinating people with different immediate interests. These are research questions, not a universal player profile. Some intended audiences may welcome high-stake uncertainty; others may prefer predictable session boundaries, low material loss or cooperative contribution without adversarial encounters. [C-07, C-08, C-11]

Distinguish meaningful commitment from pressure to repair a loss before stopping. The first can involve an understood, voluntarily chosen trade; the second may feel compulsory even when activity rises. There is **no direct controlled extraction-loss evidence in this batch** resolving when one becomes the other. Respect an informed decision to decline a raid or stop after failure rather than treating every departure as a design defect. [C-07, C-13]

## Characteristic loops and structures

Analyze preparation → entry → acquisition or discovery → reassessment of exposure → return attempt or failure → retention accounting → optional re-entry. A player can skip, retreat, return partially, assist another’s return or decline the next excursion. The sequence is a useful state model, not a mandatory fixed phase count. [C-01, C-08]

At **encounter scale**, evaluate immediate threats, carrying limits and local opportunity cost. At **excursion scale**, ask what can still be banked and what alternatives remain. At **persistent scale**, examine wealth, unlocks, access to tools and whether a loss removes the means to participate again. Individual and squad retention may diverge: a teammate can benefit while another bears equipment or rescue risk. [C-08, C-10, C-11]

Official ARC and Hunt documentation demonstrates loss/retention and entry-equipment choices; these are not universal rules for the family. Embark’s later documented temporary restriction is especially important: a baseline system’s availability can depend on the mode/condition. Preserve dates and conditions instead of flattening both documents into a timeless rule. [C-02–C-04]

## Characteristic decisions and evidence-to-application bridges

### Entry preparation and exposed value

Define what the player is committing **before** irreversible entry, and distinguish owned, reserved, spendable, protected and carried value. Present known costs without pretending uncertain encounter outcomes are known probabilities. A player who understands a valuable loadout and still declines it has expressed a preference, not necessarily failed onboarding. [C-08, C-15]

Use preparation complexity only where it changes a meaningful option: cargo capacity versus mobility, a retrieval tool versus protection, or shared supplies versus private gains. If the map’s required tool is unknowable and missing it makes return impossible, the supposed “risk decision” may instead be hidden author knowledge. Consider a disclosed requirement, an obtainable substitute or a different task. These are hypotheses to test, not mandates that every uncertainty be removed. [C-08, C-11, C-15]

### Accumulation, loss and re-entry

A larger carried gain can make continuation costlier because more is exposed; that follows from the rule, not from a demonstrated psychological effect. Whether the player experiences excitement, caution, frustration or obligation remains an empirical question. Protected slots, partial banking, recoverable cargo and baseline equipment alter different parts of the risk structure; do not call all of them insurance. [C-02, C-07, C-10]

A [viable baseline re-entry loadout](../patterns/viable-baseline-re-entry-loadout.md) is proposed for cases where repeated loss otherwise removes access to consequential participation. “Viable” means it affords an achievable role under an explicitly defined baseline scenario, not equal expected profit or equal duel strength. Preserve a genuine stop choice. Avoid using a nominal free option to funnel players into an impractical role or a paid recovery offer. [C-10, C-11, C-13]

**Counterpressure:** high-value regions may need commitments that the baseline kit does not satisfy. That can justify a separate optional tier, but not a hidden removal of the only practical re-entry path. Embark’s 1.33.0 notice supplies a documented risk-tier restriction and stated intent; it supplies **no result proving the restriction worked or remained current**. [C-03, C-10]

### Contested exits and camping

Audit exit agency at several points: what could be learned before committing, which routes or timings were feasible, what changes when an opponent contests the exit, and whether retreat or another goal remains possible. A camper’s advantage is not automatically illegitimate; nor is every ambush meaningful counterplay. A nominal second exit may be unreachable, predictably controlled or inaccessible to a particular input configuration. Count feasible alternatives, not map icons. [C-08, C-09]

Do not equate a readable retention contract with permanently revealing all exits. Crytek’s reported survey offers a contrary case to an unconditional “always show every exit” recommendation, while its described criticisms show that information and geography can change other objectives. This limited report does not establish the ideal disclosure policy for a new game. [C-05, C-08, C-09]

Possible adaptations include discoverable return conditions, differently exposed routes, an earlier opportunity to bank part of the value, or a clear option to abandon cargo while retaining the character. Each changes stakes, pursuit and group incentives. Select one against a diagnosed failure; do not bundle them and attribute the outcome to “more agency.” [C-08, C-09, C-15]

[C-10, C-11] |
| Shared versus individual return | Record who owns carried value, receives retained gains, can authorize departure and bears the cost of a rescue. | Private gains can conflict with shared exit risk; a forced vote can also trap a player. F02 supplies the distinction between uncertain outcomes and unavailable essential information. Its recovery analysis shifts attention from “can a player queue again?” to “what viable state can they enter?” P02 extends access review to inventory, confirmation, carrying and extraction controls, while preserving the uncertainty of alternatives. F07 keeps voluntary participation and harm guardrails separate from activity counts. These are inspected **draft** transfers; missing F03 economy/reward and F04 combat/traversal conclusions are not filled from memory. [C-DEP]

[Viable baseline re-entry loadout](../patterns/viable-baseline-re-entry-loadout.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G04-PAT-02`, is the main proposed adaptation. [Provisional-versus-confirmed impact feedback](../patterns/provisional-versus-confirmed-impact-feedback.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G04-PAT-01`, concerns a different failure: mistaking an attempted effect for a confirmed state. A retention event may need truthful confirmation, but do not import a combat hit marker as a complete banking interface. [C-10, C-12]

The existing F07 **Legible commitment and safe exit** pattern concerns leaving participation with disclosed bounds and attention to others. “Safe exit” does **not** mean guaranteed tactical evacuation or a costless mid-raid refund. A cooperative restorative/extraction hybrid must explicitly resolve whether danger can interrupt the intended restorative period; a genre label or the audio pilot cannot decide that conflict. [C-07, C-08, C-DEP]

## Representative cases and worked nonshooting adaptation

| Inspected case | Role in this research | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| ARC Raiders guide plus dated 1.33.0 notice | Entry equipment, exposure and scoped risk-tier variation. | Current condition availability, experiment outcome and welfare effects. [C-02, C-03] |
| Hunt: Showdown official guide and survey report | Conditional extraction and a contrary information-disclosure case. | Reproducible match behavior, representative sampling and causal effects of hidden exits. [C-04, C-05] |
| Lethal Company developer description | Cooperative cargo return and ship/field role differentiation outside a shooting-centered template. | Exact loss/retention exceptions and current build; no claim of wholly nonviolent play. [C-06] |

**Illustrative worked excursion — tidal archive retrieval.** Players enter an abandoned tidal research site to collect fragile records. A carrier can move large containers; a remote operator interprets gate states; either player can request return. No shooting is required. A record counts as retained only after a confirmed dock transfer. A player can abandon cargo to use a narrower escape route, while a missed tidal window may cost exposed cargo. The specific fiction, roles and rules are invented here. [C-15]

Before entry, state which tools are borrowed and which cargo remains at risk. Offer a baseline lamp and carrier suitable for a known low-exposure route, not every high-value wing. During the excursion, preserve uncertainty about unscouted water flow but provide accessible gate-state facts. At return, distinguish cargo unloaded from records accepted. After failure, let the players review the accounting, stop, or select a functional baseline attempt without a paid recovery prompt. These are proposed adaptations; no ethical, economic or experiential efficacy is claimed. [C-08, C-10, C-12, C-14, C-15]

**Strong counterexample:** an intentionally finite expedition campaign may end when resources are exhausted. An automatic replenishing kit could destroy that chosen premise. Another game may make collective failure and reconstruction its voluntarily understood central challenge. The correct response is to state and evaluate that promise, not silently install an endless recovery loop. Conversely, removing all loss in a mode whose intended choice is how much cargo to expose can erase its central trade. [C-07, C-10, C-15]

## Accessibility, ethics, localization and production

Inspect the **whole excursion**: selecting a kit, comparing retained/carried value, using tools, reading exit conditions, transferring cargo, confirming results, recovering and stopping. Small inventory actions, repeated hold interactions and simultaneous movement/communication can be access barriers even when shooting is optional. Preserve essential state through supported modalities without changing hidden-entity knowledge. [C-14]

Localize ownership, protection, borrowing, forfeiture, pending return and confirmed retention precisely. Check voice/text options during field/remote coordination, including message expiration and reference frames. A translated “safe” label that conflates safe storage with a safe route changes the risk contract. Numerical UI/activation defaults require platform-specific review; no universal setting is supplied. [C-08, C-14]

Evaluate meaningful contribution, economic exposure, consent to group commitments, interruption and practical ability to stop separately from session length or return rate. Do not infer a health benefit from lower churn or diagnose compulsion from a single long session. Crytek’s engagement discussion mixes reports with a developer interpretation; G04 does not promote that interpretation into causal evidence or a prescription for slower progression. [C-07, C-11, C-13]

Production cost includes persistent-state recovery, exploit-resistant accounting, policy/support for mistaken losses, content authored for depleted players, and combinations of squad size, kit, route and input configuration. A free baseline creates balancing work; an unprotected economy creates support work. No patch claim or proposed recipe certifies this work complete. [C-10–C-12, C-14]

## Research questions and validation — proposed, not performed

**E-A: Commitment or pressure?** Use a consent-based fictional excursion with finite, non-purchased stakes and disclosed loss rules. Compare selected loss/recovery variants while keeping the task and legitimate information comparable. Measure rule understanding, chosen exposure, perceived control, regret, burden and ability to stop; do not optimize return rate alone. Interview people who decline or stop. Record prior experience, time constraints and squad obligations. Reject an engagement claim when its apparent gain depends on misunderstanding, distress or recovery pressure. The study needs its own ethical review; no population effect is assumed. [C-07, C-13, C-15]

**E-B: Does an exit create actionable counterplay?** Compare two authored exit arrangements with the same objective and exposed value. Record what the player could know before commitment, feasible alternatives, attempted responses and reasons for retreat. Count departure decisions as well as deaths. Control or record map familiarity, travel capability, camper information and squad composition. Reject a topology improvement when nominal choice increases but feasible action does not, or a configuration’s essential cue becomes unusable. [C-08, C-09, C-14, C-15]

**E-C: Does the baseline support participation without compulsory re-entry?** Test a defined depleted-state kit against ordinary baseline tasks, not only against the strongest geared opponents. Record achievable contributions, extra burden shifted to teammates, transfer/resale loopholes conceptually, understanding and the option to stop. A kit fails this claim when it can queue but cannot fulfill any intended role, or when using it is practically contingent on a purchase. No target retention or profit rate is presumed. [C-10–C-12, C-15]
