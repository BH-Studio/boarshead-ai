# Battle royale

**Research candidate, not released knowledge.** This entry helps choose elimination, spatial/opportunity pressure, uncertain acquisition, scouting and return. It does not define a universal player count, map size, damage curve, match length or optimal balance setting.

## Definition and contested boundaries

The working definition is a bounded competitive session in which participants or teams progressively lose eligibility until a last surviving unit wins. The useful distinction is **terminal eligibility**, not whether every temporary defeat is irreversible. This is an analytical inference, not a formal genre standard, and last-survivor victory alone does not make every elimination tournament structurally equivalent. [C-01]

A narrower shooter-centered use adds contested insertion, scavenged capabilities, shared terrain and an increasingly restrictive safe area. PUBG’s overview exemplifies that structure. A broader elimination-family use can include Tetris 99, whose pressure and attacks need no traversable shrinking map. This comparison challenges mandatory-shooter and mandatory-circle definitions without claiming universal agreement about puzzle battle royale membership. [C-02; C-03]

A product can contain several modes with different endings and return rules. Fortnite Reload explicitly combines battle royale with reboot opportunities; PUBG has changed Recall over documented versions. Distinguish individual downing, individual defeat, eligibility to return, actual return, squad defeat and match completion. A screenshot of a respawn feature cannot by itself settle the genre boundary. [C-04; C-05; C-06]

## Custom research instructions

Record the **unit of victory and final elimination** before counting entrants. Inspect exact mode, map, patch, platform and phase. Trace a participant through first commitment, insertion, capability acquisition, a route decision, an encounter, defeat, possible return, finality and departure. Separate “the account persists” from “the unit remains eligible in this match.” [C-07]

Research strict elimination, team rescue, automatic temporary return and non-shooter elimination as distinct structures. Challenge “more shrinking pressure improves pacing,” “random loot guarantees freshness,” “more survivors means greater tension,” and “reboots always reduce frustration.” Identify the choice that each uncertainty or constraint is supposed to support and the conditions under which it instead removes agency. [C-08; C-09; C-10]

Seek original patch rationales, versioned rules, map-aware observations and studies of both early eliminations and finalists. Include people who stop after elimination, people who remain to watch and those who return; a sample of finishers alone misses important experience. Distinguish desired spectator participation from a reward or social obligation to remain. No player-session dataset was inspected in this batch. [C-11; C-17]

## Player promises and variation

Candidate promises include improvising with an uncertain start, choosing where and when to engage, making consequential survival decisions, reversing an unfavorable situation, and experiencing a legible finale. They are hypotheses, not a universal audience profile. Some players seek strict finality; others prioritize continuing with friends. A puzzle-elimination audience need not share a shooter audience’s locomotion, aiming or spatial-scouting preferences. [C-07; C-12]

Separate **stakes** from **time burden**. A defeat can be meaningful without making its player wait through a long unwelcome spectator period. Conversely, watching and advising can be a chosen part of team play rather than wasted time. The design question is whether the participation contract makes that distinction usable. [C-11]

## Characteristic loops and mechanisms

### Insertion, acquisition and route uncertainty

The shooter-oriented loop can be modeled as insertion → acquire capability and information → choose a route or position → engage, avoid or investigate → respond to lost opportunities → contest a terminal outcome. It is an analysis of one family, not a mandatory sequence for every variant. A player may rationally avoid a fight or abandon valuable loot. [C-02; C-07]

Different uncertainties do different work. A variable landing opportunity changes initial exposure; random equipment changes available actions; unknown opponents change risk estimates; a future safe area changes the value of a route. Combining all of them can make a setback hard to interpret. Identify which uncertainty produces a desired decision rather than assume more randomness produces more replay value. [C-09]

**Proposed acquisition choice:** compare full scavenging with a basic capability floor, constrained loot sets, previewed categories or choice among equivalent roles. A capability floor may preserve counterplay after an unlucky start, but can erase the intended value of finding tools. Evaluate whether an unlucky player still has a chosen response, not whether all starts are statistically identical. No loot distribution or universal fairness threshold is supplied. [C-09]

### Pressure is a map-and-information problem, not a circle-speed rule

A restriction can make waiting costly and redirect encounters. Whether that produces interesting choices depends on reachable routes, terrain, current positions, available movement tools, information and simultaneous opponents. Equal nominal distance to safety does not imply equal traversability or opportunity. This is a proposed model for spatial review, not a measured equation. [C-08]

KRAFTON’s June 2026 Blue Zone letter reports different pacing problems in Normal and Ranked play and proposes mode-specific changes. It explicitly treats excessive pressure as capable of crowding out strategy. The article’s internal observations and goals are useful practitioner evidence; a reproducible outcome analysis is not supplied. Do not turn its settings into universal defaults or infer that all announced changes are verified in a current client. [C-10; GDR-SRC-20261010-G21-012]

Apply pressure only after checking options. Draw the public deadline, routes and known constraints. Ask whether a player who recognizes the deadline can choose among meaningfully different actions. Compare changing warning time, movement time, objective placement or available routes rather than changing every variable together. Stop tightening pressure when the experience becomes an unavoidable transfer from one hazard into another with no valued decision. A deliberately harsh survival mode may accept a different tradeoff, but must state it. [C-08; C-10]

### Scouting and cue access

Distinguish an observed enemy, a reported sighting, a probable route and a public event. PUBG’s overview describes care-package smoke as an observable signal; that signal does not establish who has reached the package. EA’s historical Apex announcement describes contextual pings and character-specific information. These are documented information opportunities, not proof that a particular cue creates fair counterplay. [C-02; C-13]

Transfer the reviewed P02 principle by preserving **legitimate facts and uncertainty** across channels. An alternative cue should not convert an old sighting into continuous enemy tracking, nor should concern about secrecy remove already-public deadline information from someone who cannot perceive its original channel. Test information age and certainty as well as detection. [C-14]

### Return and finality are separate design decisions

PUBG 23.2 documents a teammate-mediated Recall chain with recovery, registration, return transport and conditions that cancel return. Update 32.1 changes the timing and route presentation, with developer feedback about waiting and landing options. These are two versions in one production history, not independent evidence that rescue improves player experience. [C-05; C-06]

Reload documents another contract: return while a teammate remains eligible, followed by a late-match cutoff and a terminal squad outcome. This is a counterexample to the claim that battle royale requires every individual defeat to end participation immediately. It is not evidence that every strict-elimination audience should adopt reboots. [C-04]

Select the return dependency deliberately: self-executed recovery, teammate rescue, scheduled return while the team survives, or no return. For each, state what must remain true, who bears the risk, whether waiting has a credible bound, what capabilities return and when eligibility ends. A displayed timer is misleading if an unstated condition can invalidate it. A rescue opportunity can increase teamwork or produce pressure to take unwanted risks; neither effect is established by the inspected documents. [C-12]

### Version discipline is part of design reasoning

EA’s 2019 Apex announcement describes Jumpmaster choice and beacon return. The currently retrieved beginner guide explicitly scopes its tips to Core modes and describes assigned drop points and a timed return condition. The Core context was inspected; this is not silently reassigned to Wildcard. Exact patch/date reconciliation and current client behavior remain unverified. Keep the records separate rather than combine them into an invented present-day ruleset. [C-13; C-15; C-16]

Likewise, PUBG 23.2’s broad “no cap” wording must be read alongside its phase/flight restrictions. A mechanism can permit repeated attempts without permitting indefinite returns. Preserve the qualification rather than choose whichever sentence best supports a pattern. [C-05]

## Conditional application process

1. **Define the terminal contract.** Specify winning unit, defeat states, return conditions, final cutoff and spectator/exit options. Confirm that a player can tell participation from eligibility. [C-07; C-12]
2. **Choose the intended uncertainty.** Separate loot, route, opponent, future-objective and social uncertainty. Remove combinations that provide only an explanation gap rather than a valued decision. [C-09]
3. **Audit reachability and knowledge together.** Review terrain and capabilities under the proposed pressure. Verify accessible public facts without revealing hidden positions. [C-08; C-14]
4. **Walk through early, middle and late setbacks.** Include a disconnected ally, a pending rescue, a missed flight, a lost last teammate and a return into a changed area. Do not assume a technically completed respawn is useful participation. [C-12]
5. **Evaluate the complete session.** Separate active decisions, downed time, waiting, voluntary spectating and requeue commitment. Compare a simpler version, including strict elimination when that is the intended experience. All proposed tests remain unperformed. [C-11; C-18]

## Pattern selection and hybrid conflicts

[Public pressure, private position](../patterns/public-pressure-private-position.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G21-PAT-01`, proposes a public constraint forecast while retaining hidden positions. Use only where the deadline is meant to be public and there are meaningful responses. A puzzle-elimination format may need an entirely different pressure model. [C-20]

[Bounded return before final elimination](../patterns/bounded-return-before-final-elimination.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G21-PAT-02`, makes recovery eligibility, return and finality explicit. Do not use it when strict individual finality is the chosen promise or return cannot provide useful participation. It is an unvalidated recipe, not a retention technique. [C-12; C-20]

An MMO hybrid can attach persistent loss to match elimination, but that changes the acquisition and session contract. A MOBA hybrid adds predetermined team roles that can conflict with improvisation from random equipment. A survival-crafting hybrid can make long preparation disappear in a short terminal encounter. Each requires an explicit choice about whose investment persists and which skills the match rewards; genre tags do not resolve the conflict. [C-20]

## Representative and negative cases

**Documentary range:** PUBG provides a spatial scavenging case, Tetris 99 a non-shooter pressure/targeting comparison, Reload a recoverable team-elimination case, and Apex documentation a warning about version-dependent insertion and recovery. Selection emphasizes structural contrast, not commercial success or a representative audience sample. No runtime or recorded gameplay was observed. [C-02–C-06; C-13; C-15]

**Illustrative worked example — two crossing routes.** A fictional match announces that a flood will close a crossing. A squad can take a short exposed bridge, a longer sheltered route or abandon a supply cache and move earlier. The announcement contains the closure and known geography, not enemy positions. A teammate’s last sighting remains dated. If neither route is reachable with the least mobile allowed capability, changing the caption cannot repair the underlying choice. Revise geography, timing or the promised movement options. This is synthetic design material, not a shipped scene. [C-08; C-14]

**Illustrative worked example — rescue cutoff.** A defeated player is eligible while a teammate survives and an extraction departure remains open. The teammate can risk a rescue interaction; successful registration is not yet actual return. If the team loses eligibility before departure, the interface explains final elimination rather than continuing a false countdown. A returning player receives enough disclosed capability to make a chosen next decision, but not an assumed invulnerability or free attack advantage. Compare this with immediate strict elimination and a simpler automatic-return contract. [C-12]

**Strong negative case:** making every defeat recoverable may destroy the intended satisfaction of securing an elimination and dilute careful survival play. A faster return can also trap the returning player in repeat defeats. The recipe must be rejected when no bounded configuration preserves the audience’s chosen stakes and usable re-entry; popularity of a mode does not refute that counterexample. [C-12; C-20]

## Accessibility, localization, ethics and production

Separate sensory detection, spatial inference, motor execution and strategic judgment. An accessible deadline does not imply the player can execute the required traversal. Cross-input assists, occlusion, hearing alternatives, small-screen maps and network delay need task-specific review before any implementation; this research supplies no universal parity test or timing threshold. [C-14; C-19]

Clarify local meanings of downed, eliminated, recalled, rebooted, spectating and out of the match. Use state descriptions as well as color; preserve the meaning of public versus private information. A team may wish to keep talking after defeat, while another player may need to leave. Avoid presenting optional rescue or spectating as an obligation enforced by shame or opaque lost rewards. Moderation, privacy and respectful departure require separate owner review. [C-11; C-12; C-14]

Population requirements, queues, server cost and map capacity depend on the actual technical and session design; a published entrant count is not a feasibility proof. Return systems create additional transition, disconnect, UI, localization and edge-case work. A strict-elimination version can be a legitimate simpler alternative, provided its commitment is honestly described. [C-17; C-19]

## Proposed validation — no tests performed

Participant studies, live telemetry and game prototypes require separate approval. The following are test specifications, not results or sample-size guarantees. [C-18]

| Question | Comparison and population | Measures, confounds and rejection rule |
|---|---|---|
| Does a public pressure forecast preserve agency? | Matched scenarios with different warning/movement allocations, including different allowed movement capabilities and map familiarity. | Correct constraint prediction, reachable options, chosen routes, perceived pressure and false enemy certainty. Hold terrain/opponents fixed where possible. Reject a change that mainly creates unavoidable outcomes or information leakage. |
| Does acquisition uncertainty support improvisation? | Full random start versus locally specified capability floor, with equivalent opponent/context scenarios. | Chosen responses after poor acquisition, explanation of failure, variety and preference. Separate aim skill from item knowledge. Reject a floor if it removes the intended discovery value without improving the chosen participation goal. |
| Does return restore useful participation rather than waiting pressure? | Strict elimination, conditional rescue and a bounded automatic alternative in consent-based scenarios. Include early defeats and people who prefer to leave. | Understanding of eligibility, waiting/active time, repeated immediate defeats, teammate pressure and valued decisions after return. Control remaining match length. Reject a benefit claim if more time logged is the only favorable result. |
| Is the finale legible? | Scenarios crossing the last return cutoff and final team defeat, with localized state terms and access alternatives. | Explain who can still win, why return ended and what leaving does. Reject a presentation that produces false promises even when its timer is accurate. |
