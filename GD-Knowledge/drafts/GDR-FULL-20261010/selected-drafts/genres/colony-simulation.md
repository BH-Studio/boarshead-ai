# Colony simulation

**Draft 0.1.0 · GDR-CAND-20261010-G13-GEN-01 · coverage-open.** This entry supports choices about delegated collective life, not a requirement to build a highly detailed person simulator. The sources are creator documents and historical production accounts; no executable game was observed and no player-effect study was inspected. The [review packet](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g13-review-packet.md) distinguishes these sources from inspected, parent-selected shared drafts.

## Definition and boundaries

For this draft, **colony simulation** means play organized around the continuing operation of a situated community whose agents perform at least some consequential activity indirectly, under player-shaped tasks, priorities, policies or environments. Individual histories, needs and relationships can matter, but simulating every individual at maximum detail is not a membership test. This is a proposed analytic boundary, not an industry standard or an approved taxonomy change. [C-01]

A management game can aggregate demand without inviting interpretation of particular inhabitants. A city builder can foreground spatial networks and civic scale. A colony simulation foregrounds the connection between collective operation and inhabitants whose circumstances constrain delegated work. These are differences in decision emphasis, not exclusive labels; the G12 parent entries have not been inspected here. Life simulation shifts attention toward inhabiting or arranging individual lives rather than making collective viability the dominant organizing problem. Sandbox describes who supplies goals and how freely systems can be recombined; it can coexist with colony management. Directly gathering resources as an avatar is not sufficient to establish colony simulation unless delegated community operation also matters. [C-01]

Keep three layers separate: a person represented by the fiction, the state variables used to compute behavior, and the player's interpretation of that person. A job queue is not a psychological model. An aggregate satisfaction value is not evidence that each inhabitant's interests were met. [C-01, C-04]

## Custom research instructions

1. **Specify the delegation contract.** For one ordinary and one urgent job, identify task creation, eligibility, priority, reservation, route availability, interruption, cancellation and retry. Ask whether a refusal expresses a character preference, a safety rule, an unavailable resource or a software defect. Do not group these under “bad AI.” [C-03, C-06]
2. **Examine representation at changing scale.** Compare individually tracked agents, representative individuals backed by aggregate population, and wholly aggregate work pools. Inspect what survives a level-of-detail transition: injuries, assignments, obligations, relationships and recorded events. Compare a simpler coherent model against a more detailed one, not only against a deliberately poor baseline. [C-06]
3. **Trace needs and resources across time.** Follow a disturbance through production, transport, stock availability and agent availability. Record delays, buffers and recovery opportunities. Do not infer a universal starvation, stress or staffing threshold from one game. [C-02, C-06]
4. **Separate incident generation from social interpretation.** Identify authored events, stochastic selection, endogenous interactions and any retrospective explanation. Ask which actor could know the alleged cause; a plausible story generated after the fact is not necessarily a faithful account of the simulation. [C-05, C-07]
5. **Audit responsibility.** Inspect what the author chose to represent, incentivize and omit; what control and knowledge the player actually had; and what the fiction attributes to inhabitants. Reject claims that human behavior is naturally reducible to the selected needs, productivity or trait variables. [C-04, C-08]
6. **Extend beyond famous examples.** The present range is RimWorld, Oxygen Not Included and Dwarf Fortress creator material. Future inspection should include a low-agent-count design, an aggregate/individual hybrid, a nonviolent or low-scarcity setting and actual unsuccessful interventions. The present set is neither a market census nor evidence about a single “colony-sim audience.” [C-01, C-11]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Candidate promises include stewardship of a continuing community, engineering reliable interdependencies, interpreting interpersonal incidents, and constructing a place worth preserving. These are **design hypotheses**, not measured audience segments. A player might value an inefficient memorial, an intentionally fragile settlement or an eventual collapse. Competent rule prediction and maximizing survival are therefore different evaluation targets. Ask participants what outcome they were pursuing before treating their decisions as mistakes. [C-01, C-11]

RimWorld's product description presents needs, relationships and directed incidents together; Klei foregrounds oxygen, warmth and sustenance; Bay 12 describes persistent history alongside thoughts, institutions and care. These documented emphases motivate comparisons between social interpretation and resource engineering. They do not establish that any feature caused attachment, emergent narrative quality or commercial success. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-001; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-002; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-003]

## Characteristic loops and structures

A useful proposed core loop is **set an intention → agents evaluate their constraints → work or non-work changes the settlement → the player interprets the result → revise the intention or accept the consequence**. The important feedback may be why a task did not execute, not a reward for the task that did. At session scale, a design can organize around restoring a viable routine, completing a construction project or resolving an incident. At long horizons, succession, migration, institutional change or deliberately ending a settlement can matter as much as growth. These are candidate structures, not mandatory progression stages. [C-06]

Distinguish an external incident selector from the ordinary simulator. Ludeon's own account says its storyteller selects events to shape stories. That supports creator intent; it does not prove that a player's experience is causally coherent, fair or emotionally effective. Internal work rules still need their own explanation. [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-001]

### Evidence-to-application bridge

| Proposed causal link | What supports it | What remains unestablished |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple constraints can prevent a delegated intention from executing | Klei reports a rejected priority redesign; the particular blocker categories in this draft are analytic examples, not reproduced findings | Which constraints confuse a particular audience, and how often |
| Distinguishing those constraints can support a correct diagnosis | Original reasoning: different causes require different repairs; reviewed interface guidance distinguishes missing, stale and misinterpreted facts | Whether the proposed explanation improves unprompted prediction or merely adds reading |
| A correct diagnosis can enable an intended intervention | Logical only when the relevant intervention is available and the player wants it | Whether the repair remains useful under resource shortages, conflicting needs and time pressure |
| A traceable event can contribute to a story the player values | Creator intent and an authoring hypothesis | Attachment, empathy, enjoyment and perceived fairness are separate, unmeasured outcomes |

The full recommendation—**make important delegation exceptions and cascade links inspectable before increasing simulation detail**—is a low-confidence application hypothesis, not a demonstrated genre law. [C-03, C-06, C-07]

## Practical choices and conditional application

| Decision | Choose it when | Cost, failure risk and simpler alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Individual agents versus aggregate labor | Particular identities, schedules or histories change decisions | Individual detail adds state and explanation obligations. Aggregate ordinary work and retain explicit exceptions when most distinctions are irrelevant; disclose the abstraction. |
| Policy priorities versus direct orders | Repeated delegation is part of the intended challenge | A larger priority scale can mask eligibility or route failures. A short queue with explicit blockers may be preferable. |
| Needs as pressures versus maintenance taxes | Meeting or declining needs creates a meaningful choice | Constant replenishment can displace other intentions. Use fewer differentiated pressures or optional automation rather than multiplying meters. |
| Cascades versus isolated incidents | Players can investigate interacting causes or deliberately accept systemic risk | Dense coupling can make recovery depend on inaccessible knowledge. Reduce coupling, add buffers or offer a diagnostic scenario before adding warnings. |
| Adjustable incident authority | Some changes to inhabitants or settlement history should remain player-protected | Per-event confirmation can destroy continuity. Offer understandable scopes rather than approval of every ordinary action. |
| Detailed explanation versus discovery | The player needs actionable truth to manage consequences | Exposing every hidden variable can remove valued uncertainty. Reveal the known blocker, its uncertainty and available intervention without claiming omniscience. |

These are design alternatives derived in C-06–C-10, not validated presets. Start by stating the intended player responsibility and one concrete failure the design should make understandable. Sketch a dependency chain and a successful contrasting case. Identify which facts belong to an individual, a household or a collective. Select the smallest representation that preserves the intended distinction. Then plan a comparison against that simpler representation. Do not add a personality system, event director or diagnostic dashboard merely because a representative title has one. [C-06, C-07]

## Representative cases and negative cases

**Klei's rejected priority change — firsthand negative case.** The Occupational Upgrade announcement reports that preview testing led the team to restore the existing priority system. It is useful evidence that even a plausible delegation redesign can be rejected in production. It is not a comparative player dataset, and the rejected algorithm and test conditions are unspecified here. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-005]

**Dwarf Fortress personality prose — presentation can distort the model.** In the 13 September 2013 development log, the author discusses individual departures from cultural values and cautions that highlighting extreme personality facets can produce overblown descriptions. This motivates testing the prose as well as the underlying state; it does not show how players actually judged those dwarves. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-004]

**Illustrative worked example: a flooded pump workshop.** This is an invented design exercise, not a shipped-game observation. A settlement has a pump repair intention, but the assigned worker cannot reach the replacement parts. Harvesting waits because another route shares the flooded access. Food stock then falls; a tired worker pauses work, making the problem look like a priority failure. [C-06, C-07]

The initial interface shows only low food and an unfinished pump. A proposed revision lets the player inspect: the repair's blocked material route; the affected harvest task; and the worker's separate rest condition. The repair is not “increase every priority.” Possible interventions include reopening access, moving the parts, using another worker, or abandoning the workshop. The player might knowingly preserve a memorial instead of rebuilding at the most efficient location. Evaluate whether they understood that tradeoff, not whether they chose the author's preferred outcome. [C-07, C-11]

For a less complex alternative, the design could use a single workshop service state and a plainly stated repair cost. That may support the same stewardship choice without agent routing. Conversely, a player seeking a failure chronicle may prefer sparse prospective information and richer retrospective records. Both challenge the strongest recommendation: more explanation is not always the right experience. [C-06, C-07]

## Pattern selection and adaptations

[Scoped autonomous changes](../patterns/scoped-autonomous-changes.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G13-PAT-01`, is a candidate for protecting high-impact world changes while allowing ordinary delegation. In a colony, distinguish permission to admit residents or relocate a community from permission to perform routine work. Permission is not a guarantee that an event is feasible or will occur. Avoid individual approvals for every meal, conversation or errand. [C-09]

[Reversible experiment branch](../patterns/reversible-experiment-branch.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G13-PAT-02`, is a candidate for testing a coupled layout away from the valued settlement. The copied state must include relevant stocks, schedules and time behavior; otherwise the test teaches a different system. Do not offer rollback over other players' work or pretend it erases acquired knowledge. Neither pattern is approved for implementation. [C-10]

## Accessibility, ethics, localization and production implications

Provide an accessible route to the same critical task facts, not an assumption that color, sound or tiny spatial markers will suffice. Preserve uncertainty when translating an incident into another modality: “cause unknown” should not become a definitive accusation in narration. The inspected `GDR-CAND-20261010-P02-PAT-02`, review-correction-1, requires factual equivalence rather than identical emotion; its overlap with P01 remains unresolved. [C-08]

Agent counts multiply authored text combinations, concurrent alerts and state transitions, not just computation. Proposed evaluation should include long names, grammatical agreement in incident reports, plural/collective subjects and the distinction between a character's statement and authoritative narration. A low-detail aggregate model may be cheaper to maintain; its savings are not quantified here. [C-08]

Where the fiction represents disability, cultural difference, distress or dependency, audit whether one convenient negative modifier becomes a person's entire identity. Allow the design to represent care and interdependence without declaring them inefficient mistakes. This is a proposed ethical review lens, not a finding about player mental health or a requirement that every game model real-world society comprehensively. No clinical, educational or therapeutic benefit is claimed. [C-04, C-08]

## Research questions and validation

**All tests below are proposed and NOT PERFORMED.** Recruit only under separate authorization. Include intended novices, experienced optimizers and story-focused players, as well as relevant language/access routes. Justify samples and local meaningful-benefit/harm bounds before collection; no numerical universal thresholds are supplied. [C-11]

| Question / comparison | Procedure and measures | Confounds and rejection rule |
|---|---|---|
| Does exception information improve delegation understanding? | Compare ordinary status with a cause-specific trace under unchanged rules. Present one eligible and one ineligible task; vary the blocking condition. Record prediction before intervention, explanation or equivalent nonverbal response, and chosen goal. | Control familiarity, extra reading time, information availability and moderator help. Reject advantage limited to repeating the demonstrated answer; retain access aids in both conditions. |
| Does individual detail change useful decisions? | Compare an individual model with an aggregate alternative that preserves total resources and the relevant exception. Ask for a forecast and a justified plan. | Identity salience, visual richness and simulator differences can confound detail. Prefer the simpler model when additional state does not support the intended distinction at acceptable burden. |
| Can players reconstruct a cascade without false blame? | Present matched symptom-only, causal-trace and optional-history versions of a synthetic incident. Measure correct versus confidently false causal accounts, recovery options found and interpretation. | Seed events and simulation timing must match. Reject explanations that invent certainty, overwhelm access routes or worsen the intended discovery experience. Do not infer empathy from accurate causal recall. |

### Coverage checkpoint

Researched: delegation contracts, individual/aggregate boundaries, needs/resource coupling, authored incidents, social interpretation, responsibility and recovery alternatives. Unresolved: current executable algorithms, large-colony performance, multiplayer delegation, locale-specific agent prose, actual perception of fairness/attachment, and simpler shipped counterexamples beyond the inspected descriptions. Missing dependencies and child proposals are explicit in the packet. Next discriminator: whether an accessible cause-specific trace changes **prediction of a blocked versus feasible task**, not merely completion rate. [C-11]
