# Soulslike

> **Bounded research candidate, not released knowledge.** The working genre boundary and recommendations remain contestable. This entry does not certify a game as a soulslike, establish a required difficulty level, or rank players by completion. No game build, frame trace, prototype or participant test was run. Named-game evidence is limited to the specified documentation, scholarship and reported studies.

## Decision and scope

Use this entry to decide how action commitments, encounters, exploration, setbacks, persistent advancement and community knowledge interact in a game drawing on the Souls lineage. Treat the label as a **family resemblance and design conversation**, not a rule requiring a particular camera, combat resource, death penalty, visual tone, fixed difficulty or exclusion of assistance. A marketed label and an analyst's classification should be recorded separately. [C-01; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-008; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-009; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-010; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-011]

The practical questions are what the player commits to, what information makes another attempt different, what survives a setback, what a return path contributes, how builds remain understandable, and which kinds of help support or change the chosen activity. Exact animation windows, comprehensive action-RPG economies, network combat, full accessibility certification and clinical outcomes are outside this bounded work. [C-07–C-14, C-17]

## Definition, lineage and contested boundaries

A useful comparison describes the **combination** of commitments and feedback, encounters and recovery, persistent knowledge and progression, and authored versus community-provided interpretation. Resemblance on one axis is not decisive: a difficult action game need not use the same exploration or loss structure, and a resource-recovery game need not center action execution. These are analytical distinctions, not necessary-and-sufficient membership criteria. [C-01]

The official Elden Ring guide and Sekiro manual make different loss and combat rules visible. Sekiro is retained as a **contested neighboring/lineage case**, not silently certified as a member. Another Crab's Treasure supplies creator self-identification rather than a supposedly neutral taxonomy. Evidence about those sources supports examining variation; it does not authorize collapsing every action RPG, metroidvania or challenging platformer into this file. [C-01–C-04]

Distinguish lineage from “soulslike difficulty.” Difficulty is a relation between a task and a player in a context, not a fixed moral property of a genre. A shared fixed challenge may be a valued design choice, while an alternate configuration may intentionally offer a different experience. Neither a universal assist requirement nor a ban on assistance follows from the label. The proposal is to state the chosen activity and consequences honestly, then evaluate the relevant users and configurations. [C-01, C-06, C-12, C-13]

## Custom research instructions

For each case, record **version, mode, assistance, input, camera situation, encounter state, build and recovery route** before attributing an outcome to “difficulty.” Separate authored rules from learned community conventions: an accepted speedrun route, a self-imposed challenge and an in-game prerequisite are not interchangeable. Unknown build or mode information must remain unknown, not filled from memory. [C-07, C-11, C-14]

Inspect a range rather than only celebrated boss victories: early entry, ordinary encounters, navigation, a repeated return, a changed enemy relation, a resource-poor state, an unsuccessful build choice, optional assistance, departure and later re-entry. Include games and activities outside the proposed boundary as negative cases. Creator intent can explain a choice but cannot establish that players learned, enjoyed or experienced it as fair. [C-06–C-14]

Challenge comparisons that confound the encounter with its approach route, a numerical build increase with learning, stronger damage with a more readable cue, or more attempts with stronger motivation. Investigate dropouts and people who never obtained usable access, not just experienced reviewers. Ask what failure leaves knowable and actionable, not whether people “earned” continuation. [C-06–C-10, C-15–C-17]

Primary routes should include historical and current manuals, patch notes, reproducible gameplay records, developer explanations and original participant or review-corpus research. For recordings, require platform/build when available, a scene and timestamp; none were used here as direct gameplay observations. Do not adopt a source's broad definitions, sales forecasts or moral language simply because it is scholarly. [C-01, C-06, C-17]

## Player promises and actual evidence limits

Possible promises include learning to read an opponent, making a consequential commitment, recognizing a route, developing a build, finding meaning with others, or overcoming a personally selected challenge. These are **candidate goals**, not claims about every player's reasons. A player may seek exploration, story, performance, experimentation, sociability or a stable challenge comparison in different proportions. Ask rather than infer preference from victory, time played or help used. [C-06, C-11, C-12]

**Empirical evidence — a bounded questionnaire.** Guzsvinecz reports 100 Hungarian respondents recruited through university-connected Facebook groups and sharing in January 2024: 87 men and 13 women, with 61 in its genre-playing group and 39 in the other group. Among 20 previously exposed members of that other group, 13 reported stopping because of Souls mechanics. The genre-playing group rated difficulty options least important among the surveyed mechanics. This is a convenience/snowball, self-report sample, not a population estimate, a disabled-player needs assessment or a causal comparison of options. The contrasting groups do not establish one universal preference. [C-06, C-15; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-014, §§5,7.1,7.3.1–7.3.2,8.1]

**Empirical evidence — a selected textual corpus.** Sun and colleagues' v1 preprint reports analysis of 600 Steam reviews, selected for more than 50 hours of play, helpfulness and descriptive content. It interprets themes of rhythm, self-attribution and absorption; §6.2 acknowledges survivorship bias. Raw reviews and coding reproducibility were not audited here. This is neither observed within-person improvement nor evidence that punishment produces therapeutic benefit. Its moral and causal rhetoric is not adopted. The displayed submission date and identifier also need bibliographic reconciliation. [C-06, C-16, C-17; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-015, §§3.1–3.3,4,6.2]

These studies do not directly contradict assistance examples: one reports selected attitudes, another selected narratives, while product documentation describes available features. None compares the same participants under the same changed rule. Treat their differences as evidence about scope and selection before claiming a design dispute has been resolved. [C-06, C-17]

## Characteristic decisions and mechanisms

### Action commitment and encounter interpretation

Analyze a commitment as a transition: available information and actions before input; the action accepted; what can change or be canceled during execution; recovery; and the feedback that becomes available afterward. Timing, position, resource state, animation visibility and control configuration can each change the result. The useful question is which relationship the player could learn and act on—not whether the action is sufficiently “weighty.” This state model is a proposed analytical aid, not a measured universal combat model. [C-07]

Avoid explaining every failed attempt as impatience or “greed.” A player may have formed a wrong rule, acted on a correct rule too late, lacked a usable cue, encountered camera occlusion, lacked a viable build, or preferred a risky choice. Multiple explanations can coexist. A purported learning intervention should distinguish them rather than convert all failure into a character judgment. [C-07, C-13, C-17]

The Sekiro manual's posture/deflection relation offers a documented combat contrast to a generic stamina-and-dodge description. It does not establish that a specific response window is accessible or that the rhythm explanation works for every user. Do not infer exact frame counts, input buffering or latency tolerances from prose descriptions. [C-03, C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-009, §§Deflection and Posture/Guard]

### Death, persistence and the next attempt

The Elden Ring guide documents restoring resources and reviving most defeated enemies at rest, an alternate revival location near a Stake of Marika, and retrieval of carried runes with loss if another death intervenes. Sekiro's manual instead describes halving current sen and experience toward the next skill point, with permanent loss and a possible Unseen Aid exception. These are historical documented architectures, not a required common penalty. [C-02, C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-008, §Game Environment; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-009, §§Loss upon Death/Unseen Aid]

For a new design, maintain a **persistence ledger** rather than a single “punishment severity” value. Specify character progression, carried resources, equipped tools, world changes, route access, encounter state, previously acquired information and remaining obligations. A player retains knowledge outside the save file, but that does not mean every death supplies useful knowledge. If the actionable relationship was never visible, repeating it may provide little diagnostic value. [C-08, C-14]

| Recovery component | Genre-specific question | Plausible alternative, not a universal recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Encounter attempt | Does the next attempt allow testing a changed decision? | Focused retry, alternate approach, retreat or optional practice |
| Return route | Is navigation, attrition or route mastery still part of the desired challenge? | Preserve meaningful return choices; shorten already-solved transit when it only substitutes for the chosen encounter |
| Lost resources | Is retrieval an elective risk, a forced debt, or a blocker to another attempt? | Partial recovery, banking, different stakes, or an explicit alternate mode |
| Persistent world change | What remains trustworthy after rest, defeat or reload? | Clearly declared reset domains rather than an unqualified “everything resets” |
| Interruption | Can the player stop without misleading the group or losing an undisclosed commitment? | Context-appropriate save/exit policy; do not assume solo pause and shared time are equivalent |

A shorter retry can preserve attention to the encounter, but it can also remove the expedition or attrition decision that made the encounter meaningful. Conversely, retaining a route solely because it is a convention is not evidence of value. Compare the actual intended activities, with encounter demand, route demand and recovery stakes manipulated separately where feasible. [C-08, C-09]

### Build commitment, exploration and help

A build choice becomes problematic when the player commits scarce resources without a usable account of the consequences, or cannot try a viable alternative after discovering a mismatch. It is not automatically a “trap” merely because another build would be stronger. Distinguish numerical efficiency, role preference, interaction access, unknown future requirements and unintended dead ends. Compare a reversible trial, staged commitment, better consequence information, accessible alternatives or a specific refund rather than declaring every investment reversible. [C-10]

Lies of P's Overture launch notes describe difficulty options, individual Quartz revision and separated Dash/Dodge controls, with a warning that control changes reset settings. That is evidence of a particular revision and its configuration cost. It does not show that all build costs can be refunded, that these options were present in every older build, or that usability improved for every player. [C-05, C-10, C-13; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-012, cited sections]

Welsh's interpretive essay discusses how guides, efficient routes, build knowledge and speedrunning change engagement with Dark Souls. Use this to ask whether community knowledge helps someone reach a chosen activity or preempts an exploration they valued. It does not justify describing guide users as less authentic or treating uninformed discovery as the only legitimate form of play. [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-013, main-text guide/build discussion]

### Assistance and the activity it preserves

Aggro Crab calls Another Crab's Treasure a soulslike adventure; its official PlayStation product copy describes assistance options. These are linked production claims, not independent experiments. They are nevertheless a useful negative case against defining assistance out of the genre by assertion. The detailed options were not verified from a playable build or the unavailable patch-note body. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-010; GDR-SRC-20261010-G15-011, §PLAY AT YOUR OWN PACE]

For a proposed assist, name the barrier and the activity to preserve. A damage reduction may allow more observation but may not address inaccessible timing information. A clearer cue can preserve the decision without changing resource loss; an automatic response may intentionally change execution into a different activity. Offer truthful descriptions and evaluate the chosen goal. Do not promise identical difficulty, emotion or community comparability when the rules differ. [C-12, C-13]

## Application process and detailed examples

### Diagnose, choose, compare, revise

First name the target experience without the genre label: for example, choosing when to commit to an attack after reading position and intent. Then specify what is observable, what is uncertain, the action and recovery states, and what a failed attempt changes. Examine competing causes before selecting an intervention. Preserve an unchanged condition and at least one plausible alternative. A later authorized evaluation should separate comprehension, execution, chosen risk, affect and burden. Revise or stop when the intervention fixes the wrong cause, removes a desired activity or leaves a supported player state without an honest continuation. [C-07–C-14]

### Illustrative worked case: the lantern guardian

**Synthetic design exercise; no observed game or test result.** An imaginary encounter has two readable windups. A sweeping attack makes close retreat appropriate; a thrust makes lateral movement appropriate. Both carry a distinct shape cue and a usable alternative information route. The timing and damage values are intentionally unspecified until a prototype and target context justify them. [C-07, C-13, C-14]

A supported attempt may demonstrate the sweep. A transfer case changes the opponent's appearance and position while retaining the sweep/thrust relation; importantly, the case includes a thrust so that “always retreat” and “classify the tell, then choose” make different predictions. Ask for the intended response before feedback. A separate optional practice condition permits more observation without consuming the expedition's rare material. This is not proof that the design teaches, and sustained access cues are not removed to create a supposed mastery test. [C-14]

Now examine recovery. If the guardian is reached through an attrition corridor, a nearby retry point changes both encounter exposure and the corridor's planning role. Compare retaining the corridor, offering an earned connector, and separating practice from the committed expedition. Ask whether the corridor still presents choices the player values. Do not claim that the nearest checkpoint is inherently fairest, nor that repetition proves respect for the player. [C-08, C-09, C-17]

### Illustrative build and assistance countercase

Suppose an imaginary build invests limited tokens in a slow attack that works well at range but is awkward in close spaces. A preview encounter may reveal the interaction before commitment; a limited revision may allow a different tool. Yet a full refund can erase a deliberately chosen allocation problem, and a preview can reveal an intended surprise. The decision is what consequences must be understandable now, what may remain uncertain, and which alternatives preserve the chosen experience. [C-10]

An option that increases survivability could support reading the encounter but still leave the input sequence inaccessible. An automatic dodge could enable exploration while removing the execution goal. Neither is inherently a failure if truthfully offered for a different desired activity; neither establishes identical mastery. Evaluate each against its stated purpose and keep the player free to reject it. [C-12, C-13]

### Hybrid conflicts and strongest counterexamples

A survival-crafting hybrid can impose collection, repair, travel, loss retrieval and encounter practice after one setback. The combination may support expedition stakes, but cannot be presumed better because each component appears in a genre convention. Refer to [Survival crafting](survival-crafting.md) for the material-side analysis and separate the costs before deciding which to retain. [C-18]

A metroidvania hybrid may require returning with a capability; a soulslike-style recovery objective may instead direct return before acquiring it. These demands can conflict. A cooperative hybrid can make another player's assistance part of the desired activity, while a self-imposed solo challenge intentionally excludes it. Retain these as explicit configurations, not measures of legitimate participation. [C-11, C-18]

The strongest opposing case to shorter retries and greater reversibility is a deliberately stable, costly expedition whose preparation and imperfect commitments are the actual attraction. Removing those costs could flatten the experience. The strongest opposing case to preserving them is a player who already understands the route and only wants a usable opportunity to learn the encounter. This packet provides a way to discriminate those situations, not a universal winning side. [C-09, C-10, C-12]

## Accessibility, ethics, localization and production

**Access.** Inspect readable state under effects, camera occlusion and multiple enemies; alternative cue meaning and timing; remapping and sustained/repeated input; cognitive support for resuming an interrupted objective; and configuration persistence after updates. Lower damage is not a substitute for seeing, interpreting or operating the relevant interaction. A sensory alternative must not reveal the name of a concealed attacker unless that knowledge is intentionally allowed. Exact timing, motion, text and contrast requirements require appropriately scoped guidance and local testing; no numerical universal is issued here. [C-13]

**Ethics and community practice.** Do not infer enjoyment, competence outside the task, resilience, therapeutic improvement or moral worth from completing an encounter. Self-attributions in selected reviews are not a license for the design to blame the user. Avoid public labeling of assistance that pressures disclosure of personal access needs. A community can value comparable rules without imposing those rules on every private activity; policies and moderation need their own evidence and authorization. [C-06, C-11, C-12, C-17]

**Localization.** Test attack-state terms, equipment traits, ambiguous lore, hint strength and update notes in the actual languages. A literal translation can change whether two cues appear to refer to the same rule; a more explanatory translation can disclose a mystery prematurely. Record which ambiguity belongs to the fiction and which is merely an unusable instruction. No cultural group is assumed to prefer one difficulty or learning style. [C-11, C-13, C-14]

**Production.** Review state consistency through checkpoints, respec-like changes, cutscenes, assistance, controller changes and version migration. Test cues under the actual camera and supported devices rather than assuming an authored animation is always visible. Keep network and multiplayer effects separate from local commitment design. These are conceptual test obligations, not an engine plan, staffing estimate, implemented feature or QA result. [C-07–C-14]

## Proposed validation and discriminating questions

**All tests are proposed, NOT PERFORMED.** Participant numbers, analysis and acceptable margins must be chosen for the actual research question, with informed consent and stopping options. Include differing prior knowledge, desired activities, access needs and prior non-completion; do not recruit only helpful long-play reviewers. [C-06, C-14]

| Test | Comparison / procedure | Measures and disconfirmation |
|---|---|---|
| T-SO-1: learned relation versus memorized action | Supported attempt, then a changed case in which a competing rule predicts another action. Collect prediction before feedback; hold usable access information constant. | Distinguish perception, rule explanation, chosen risk and execution. Reject the teaching claim if improvement is surface-specific or cue availability—not the intended rule—explains it. |
| T-SO-2: retry versus expedition | Compare unchanged return, earned connector and focused retry while accounting for encounter difficulty, supplies and ordering. | Record route choices, approach burden, encounter learning, preference and interruption cost separately. Reject universal shortening if the desired attrition activity disappears; reject unchanged repetition if it only blocks the chosen encounter without valued decisions. |
| T-SO-3: build information and revision | Compare understandable commitment information, a local trial and a limited revision option; preserve relevant mystery and resource constraints. | Ask what the build makes possible and what cannot be undone. More switching is not automatically better agency. Reject a change that misrepresents irreversible consequences or trivializes the intended allocation problem. |
| T-SO-4: target-specific assistance | Compare a cue/access change with a consequence change against the stated barrier, rather than bundling both. | Evaluate comprehension, operation, activity retained and desired challenge. Reject an accessibility claim when completion improves but the user still cannot access the chosen interaction. |
| T-SO-5: informed and uninformed exploration | Compare player-chosen help levels while recording prior guide exposure and preference. | Examine actionable understanding, surprise, satisfaction and unwanted disclosure. Do not treat guide use, non-use or completion as a moral endpoint. |
