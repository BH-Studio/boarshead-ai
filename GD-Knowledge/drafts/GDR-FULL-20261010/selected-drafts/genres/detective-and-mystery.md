# Detective and mystery

**Research candidate, not released guidance.** `GDR-CAND-20261010-G10-GEN-02`, version `0.1.0`, revision `initial-draft-1`. This is a complete bounded genre draft with open coverage, not a finding that all mystery players seek the same experience. It authorizes neither canonical story changes nor implementation. No game was played and no participants were tested for G10.

## Definition and contested boundaries

This entry uses **mystery** for play organized around an unresolved question about events, causes, identities, meanings or relationships. **Detective play** is the narrower activity of investigating such questions through acquiring, interpreting and testing evidence. Crime, police authority, a single culprit, a final accusation and one recoverable explanation are not required by this working definition. A mysterious atmosphere alone does not establish an investigative loop. These are proposed retrieval boundaries, not formal standards or a market survey. [C-01]

Keep five functions distinct. **Clue availability** concerns what evidence exists and can be encountered. **Interpretation** concerns what an observation or statement might mean. **Hypothesis testing** concerns using an observation or action to discriminate alternatives. **Narrative revelation** concerns what the work eventually discloses, whether or not the player inferred it. **Evidentiary sufficiency** concerns what supports a conclusion under the declared task, including any remaining alternatives. More revelation can finish a story without demonstrating that the preceding evidence supported the answer. [C-02]

A **clue** is evidence relevant to an investigative question, not necessarily a collectible or highlighted object. An **observation** is something encountered; **testimony** is a source’s claim about something; a **hypothesis** is an explanation under consideration. A proposition can be true in the fictional world but not knowable to the player, knowable but not yet noticed, believed but false, or entered correctly without being understood. The answer checker adds another layer: it may accept or reject an input for reasons separate from the fictional truth. These analytic distinctions adapt, rather than replace, reviewed W03 evidence and G03 knowledge/authorization guidance. [C-02; reviewed dependency ledger]

**Neighboring labels.** [Puzzle](puzzle.md) owns the broader treatment of constrained reasoning, manipulation and solution contracts. Adventure supplies broader world interaction; point-and-click does not define what counts as evidence. A social-deduction game may place uncertainty in other players’ strategic claims rather than in a recoverable authored past, so the relevant social-deduction entry is a separate dependency. An archaeological interpretation, an exploration of family history or a scientific fictional investigation can be a useful mystery context without an accusation mechanic. This entry proposes applicability, not a claim that a publisher used the same genre label. [C-01]

## Custom research instructions

Begin with the question the player is supposed to resolve and the question the story may leave unresolved. Name whether the experience values reconstructing a fixed event, understanding motives, discovering unfamiliar institutions, making a decision with incomplete evidence, or collaboratively authoring an explanation. Do not declare one of these the authentic form of detective play. [C-01, C-03]

Inspect the route from each required conclusion back to accessible evidence. Record the assumptions needed to connect them, the evidence source’s reliability and independence, discovery-order constraints, and a serious alternative explanation. Challenge the author’s interpretation: could a reasonable player who saw the same evidence infer something else? Could someone reach the answer through an unintended but valid route? Could someone simply cycle input choices? These are different conditions and need different responses. [C-02, C-08, C-09]

Investigate the answer interface as part of the evidence system. A journal category, grammatical slot, completion percentage or confirmation sound may disclose information before the fictional investigation earns it. Conversely, a rejected phrase can reflect a parser limitation rather than a mistaken explanation. Inspect the exact build and input where possible; creator descriptions alone do not establish runtime completeness. [C-05, C-06, C-10]

For procedural mysteries, ask when truth is generated, what can subsequently change, which traces are preserved, and whether every admitted case supports its declared completion condition. Do not infer case validity from a rich simulation or a large city. For authored mysteries, test dependency failure, overlooked alternatives and localization changes rather than assuming deliberate authorship guarantees a fair inference. Seek unsuccessful deductions and abandoned paths, not only creator-selected successful examples. [C-07, C-13]

Later expansion needs original player-reasoning studies, directly inspected noncriminal and culturally varied mysteries, cooperative investigations, and actual procedural-case traces. Existing sources are mainly creators’ accounts and product documentation. They do not establish that solving fictional cases improves general reasoning, investigative skill or real-world evidential judgment. [C-16]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Possible promises include earning an explanation, exercising curiosity, building a coherent account, discovering why a character’s account was incomplete, expressing a judgment under uncertainty, or participating in a story without solving every detail. These are proposed audience questions, not a validated typology. A person can welcome uncertainty about motive while wanting clear feedback about how to enter an answer. Another may want a fully adjudicated logic problem. [C-03]

Do not infer preference from completion alone. A completed case may reflect deduction, exhaustive guessing, external help, remembered spoilers or a companion’s answer. Failure to accuse may reflect principled doubt rather than confusion. An accurate explanation of what remains uncertain can be a better outcome for one design than a forced declaration of guilt. Measure the promised experience and the quality of the relevant reasoning separately. [C-02, C-03, C-16]

## Characteristic loops and structures

### An investigative loop with separate state layers

A proposed loop is **encounter a question → seek evidence → preserve provenance → form alternatives → choose a discriminating inquiry → revise beliefs → decide what can be concluded or done**. Narrative revelation may occur during any part of it. A case may end in a supported explanation, a practical resolution without full knowledge, or an intentionally unresolved question. This is a design model, not an empirical sequence every player follows. [C-02, C-18]

Keep the following records conceptually separate during authoring. They are design records, not a required engine architecture or approved data schema. [C-02, C-13]

| Record | Example of what it contains | Common invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Fictional events and rules | What happened, when, and which causal relationships apply. | Changing the past solely because the player guessed differently while still promising a fixed truth. |
| Available evidence | A trace, testimony or record, its origin and accessibility conditions. | Treating an inaccessible clue as if it made an inference fair. |
| Player encounters | What this playthrough actually exposed or recorded. | Assuming proximity means attention or comprehension. |
| Player hypothesis | A selected or written explanation, including uncertainty. | Treating a notebook entry as knowledge the player has demonstrated. |
| Adjudication and commitment | What a checker recognizes and what an accusation commits. | Equating accepted wording, NPC belief, reward and factual truth. |

### Truth contracts and procedural truth

**Fixed authored truth** permits a stable past with different discovery orders. **Generated-then-fixed truth** permits a different past per case, provided its evidence and consequences remain coherent with that case. **Evolving simulation** permits new events while preserving the time-indexed meaning of earlier traces. **Explicitly co-authored truth** can let players help create the explanation, but should not be presented as discovering a uniquely predetermined past. These are proposed contractual distinctions, not claims about how every named game implements its world. [C-13]

For a generated case, a useful proposed audit records the seed where meaningful, generator and rules versions, initial truth state, subsequent event history relevant to the question, evidence-generation rules, accessible discriminators and the acceptance condition. If a simulation destroys the only necessary record, the design needs a supported recovery, an honestly unresolved outcome, or a different completion contract. Replacing the past invisibly is not the same repair. No generator or case validator was run in this research. [C-13]

A long narrative can combine small answered questions with larger unresolved ones. Requiring full certainty about every motive can destroy ambiguity; allowing uncertainty about every practical action can make the investigation inert. Identify which questions are optional interpretation, which unlock action, and which require a conclusive answer. This adapts W02’s distinction between an outcome, its accepted conditions and the party recognizing it. [C-02, C-08, C-09]

## Mechanisms, decisions and evidence-to-application bridges

### Clue availability is not interpretation

Reviewed W03 distinguishes reaching evidence, perceiving its relations, proposing an explanation and using it to discriminate alternatives. For detective play, audit that chain at the level of the required conclusion. A brighter clue cannot repair a missing causal premise; another copy of the same report does not provide independent corroboration. The proposed remedy is to identify the failing link before adding content. This is a conditional inference, not a demonstrated increase in comprehension. [C-02, C-08]

An **evidence board** should distinguish the source’s words from the protagonist’s inference and the player’s current hypothesis. Automatically connecting two facts can be a useful requested explanation, but may perform the central deduction uninvited. Automatically storing an encountered fact can support memory without necessarily deciding its relevance; even that depends on which facts are collected and labeled. Retain the uncertainty and provenance of a recorded statement. [C-08, C-11, C-12]

### Feedback and the answer-oracle problem

Pope’s 2017 development account describes confirmation in groups of three fates and acceptance of several fate formulations. Those are historical design choices, not universal recommended quantities or proof that all retail builds behave identically. The same page’s reader reply identifies a potential weakness: two known answers can be held constant while a third is varied. That is an analytical objection, not an observed G10 exploit. [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-G10-004]

Klavins describes a different tradeoff: more informative partial validation reduced reported frustration but also reduced difficulty and length. The interview is a firsthand iteration account, not a controlled comparison or a general rule to reveal a particular number of wrong slots. Together, the accounts justify investigating the information disclosed by checking, not copying either numerical choice. [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-G10-013]

The structural bridge is straightforward: an answer checker that responds differently to competing inputs can become an evidence source. Making checks coarser changes that source but does not necessarily eliminate it. A fully negative result can also be costly to interpret. Separate “input understood,” “claim contradicts known evidence,” “insufficient support,” “accepted by this institution,” and “the story establishes this as true.” Not every game should expose all five, but the design should know which it is communicating. [C-09, C-10]

| Validation option | Appropriate question | Main tradeoff and alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate per-field correctness | Is learning the vocabulary or entering a known answer the current task? | May turn a deduction into slot enumeration. Use input acknowledgment without truth feedback when uncertainty matters. [C-10] |
| Partial or bundled validation | Can a player recover from an incorrect account without isolating every answer? | Can still leak answer information and can conceal the location of a legitimate error. Evaluate repeated-query behavior. [C-05, C-06, C-10] |
| Evidence-based contradiction | Does a claim conflict with a specified observation under the task’s rules? | The selected counterevidence may itself be a hint; offer it under the intended help policy. [C-10] |
| Final commitment with consequences | Is acting under uncertainty part of the promise? | An inaccessible clue or accidental submission can create an unintended irreversible cost. Preview the commitment and preserve the agreed recovery. [C-09, C-11] |
| No omniscient correctness feedback | Is the goal interpretation or a practical decision rather than a fully checked solution? | Some players may be unsure what the game considers complete. Explain closure without claiming factual certainty. [C-03, C-09] |

### Red herrings, reliability and alternate explanations

Separate **true but irrelevant information**, **mistaken testimony**, **deliberate fabrication inside the fiction**, **an alternative explanation still consistent with evidence**, and **an authoring/UI defect**. A wrong assumption is not automatically a designed red herring. The Golden Idol interview describes a case where an intended clue invited misleading elimination, illustrating why the actual inference route needs review. It does not establish a universal amount of misdirection. [C-06, C-08]

A conditional recipe is to state what makes the tempting interpretation plausible, what could count against it, and whether finding that discriminator is possible under the declared case contract. If an incorrect accusation has a serious cost, review whether the contrary evidence was accessible and interpretable before commitment. If ambiguity is intentional, preserve it instead of retroactively labeling every nonpreferred account a player mistake. A single decisive observation may suffice; a large clue count may still leave the issue unresolved. [C-08, C-09]

Do not convert “most plausible so far” into “established truth.” In an open fictional model, unconsidered alternatives remain possible; a tightly bounded puzzle may deliberately exclude them through explicit rules. The design’s intended level of closure determines what the checker may responsibly claim. The [new acceptance pattern](../patterns/evidence-bounded-solution-acceptance.md) addresses explicit answer classes, not the truth of arbitrary free-form accusations. [C-09, C-14]

### Expression, linguistic form and recognition

A structured response interface can reduce ambiguous input but also tell the player which categories exist, how many entities participate or what kind of event to seek. A free-text interface can permit expression while rejecting correct synonyms or accepting vague nonsense. The relevant design question is not “more freedom” in the abstract; it is which distinctions the task needs and which forms the system can recognize reliably. [C-05, C-06, C-10]

Normalize equivalent expressions separately from deciding that distinct histories are equivalent. Naming an immediate physical cause and an ultimate cause may be supported alternative descriptions in a particular game, but that does not authorize substituting another perpetrator. Where the design cannot bound recognition, use a limited vocabulary, a reviewable statement format or an explicitly interpretive outcome rather than claiming unrestricted natural-language understanding. No language model or parser was implemented or validated here. [C-09, C-10]

### Interpretation can matter without gating progression

inkle’s explanation of *Heaven’s Vault* distinguishes discovery of sites from correctness of translation, while the press description emphasizes translation’s role in the character’s understanding. These accounts can coexist: a belief or interpretation can matter without being the permission check for reaching the next place. They are one creator context, not independent gameplay validation. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-G10-007; GDR-SRC-20261010-G10-008]

For a mystery emphasizing exploration, consider allowing progress while keeping an uncertain interpretation revisable. For a formal deduction challenge, conclusive adjudication may be central. For a character drama, an NPC’s response can show the consequence of a belief without certifying that belief. Do not resolve this difference by declaring one genre rule superior. [C-03, C-09]

### Procedural cases need explicit failure handling

The *Shadows of Doubt* closed-alpha report identifies UI, guidance and simulation-response problems despite an ambitious systemic setting. This is a negative production account from a historical build; it does not establish present defects or a representative failure rate. Its value here is as a counterexample to assuming that a generated world automatically makes investigation understandable. [C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-G10-012]

For a prospective system, compare the actual trace a player can inspect with the generator’s hidden truth. A solved internal constraint set is insufficient if witnesses cannot be reached, clues are erased without replacement, or a supported hypothesis cannot be expressed. When a necessary discriminator is unavailable, use a declared alternate route, an unresolved disposition or a bounded fallback case. Selecting a particular runtime architecture remains outside this genre entry; the relevant F05 reviewed dependency is not established in this packet. [C-13]

## Worked example: the missing observatory lens

**Illustrative invented case; not a shipped-game observation, legal procedure or performed playtest.** An observatory’s lens is absent after a scheduled inspection. The player’s question is initially “was this an authorized transfer or an unauthorized removal?” The story may leave the mover’s motive unresolved. Two staff members had access; access alone establishes neither action nor intent. [C-17, C-18]

The authored record distinguishes the actual event from the evidence presented. A maintenance notice says the lens was due for cleaning. A signed transfer record lists a destination but lacks the receiving signature. A crate is visible in a secured receiving area. An automatically stamped door log uses a clock that is known, through an inspectable calibration notice, to differ from the observatory’s wall clock. The case’s designed evidence must make clear which comparisons are legitimate; a timestamp is not a trustworthy chronology merely because it looks precise. [C-02, C-08, C-18]

Possible accounts include an authorized transfer with incomplete paperwork, unauthorized removal disguised as cleaning, and a record referring to another lens. The player can compare the crate’s serial plate with the source inventory and ask the receiving office about the authorization. Merely counting three suggestive clues should not close the case. The serial comparison discriminates which object moved; authorization evidence addresses permission; neither alone establishes motive. These are separable conclusions. [C-08, C-09, C-18]

A player may encounter the crate first, infer transfer and later find the notice. Unless the task specifically requires obtaining authorization to enter an area, the design should not reject the same supported account solely because clues were read in an unpreferred order. Conversely, knowing a location is not the same as holding permission to enter it. This applies the inspected G03 and W02 distinctions without importing a universal no-gates rule. [C-02, C-09]

The notebook can preserve quotations and a record of which clock generated a timestamp, while leaving the chronology as a player hypothesis. A requested help response might say “check whether these times use the same clock”; that is a relation hint, not mere transcription. An access description may instead read the calibration notice verbatim in an appropriate modality without labeling its relevance. The boundary depends on what information the player has legitimately encountered. [C-11, C-12]

Before a final report, show the commitment: reporting “authorized transfer established” requires the agreed evidence about the object and permission; reporting “unauthorized removal” has different consequences. If the receiving office is unavailable and the remaining evidence supports both accounts, the honest disposition can be “object located; authorization unresolved.” The system should not reward naming a convenient culprit while pretending the available evidence proved it. A later scene may reveal the truth, but that later revelation does not retroactively make the earlier accusation supported. [C-09, C-13, C-15, C-17]

The strongest counterexample to full closure is a story deliberately exploring uncertain motives. Once the player has located the lens and addressed the practical loss, additional evidence forcing a definitive moral explanation could weaken the chosen theme. The strongest counterexample to accepting every consistent account is a case with a genuinely available discriminator: stopping early is a provisional hypothesis, not automatically an equally valid complete solution. [C-03, C-08, C-09]

## Accessibility, localization, ethics and production

**Evidence access.** Provide the facts a player is entitled to receive through appropriate routes without forcing a particular conclusion. A transcript should distinguish a witness’s statement from established truth. An alternative description can preserve that a sound occurred nearby without asserting who caused it. An evidence board’s automatic labels, alt text and speaker names need the same spoiler review as visible dialogue. Information equivalence does not prove equivalent difficulty or emotion. [C-12]

**Cognitive and interruption access.** Separate a recap of encountered facts from a recap that resolves their relationship. Permit revisiting testimony and identifying its source. On return from a long break, a player may remember the explanation but not the input interface, or remember the people but not the chronology. Do not infer the needed help solely from elapsed time or a count of collected clues. [C-11, C-12]

**Localization.** Check whether grammatical agreement, honorifics, pronouns, alphabetic ordering, voice casting or culturally specific assumptions reveal or erase distinctions. A sentence-slot interface must be reviewed as a whole answer contract rather than as isolated strings. A translation can preserve literal meaning while changing which answers sound grammatical. This batch supplies a risk analysis, not measured cross-language equivalence. [C-10, C-12]

**Ethics.** Do not treat accent, ethnicity, disability, emotional flatness, social class or nonconforming behavior as a generic proof of deception or guilt. A character may be unreliable for a reason established in the fiction; that is different from asking the player to infer criminality from an identity marker. For stories involving real people or sensitive histories, obtain separate rights and ethical review. These are proposed design safeguards, not a real-world investigative or legal standard. [C-17]

**Production.** Maintain separate review attention for world consistency, clue availability, interpretation, language recognition, input recovery and adjudication. More possible answers increase validation work; more clues increase writing and localization work without necessarily adding independent support. Test all deliberately supported discovery orders, including interrupted saves and partial reports, before claiming recoverability. Where resources cannot support an open response space, a narrower honestly described contract is preferable to a false promise of unrestricted reasoning. [C-10, C-13, C-18]

## Research questions and proposed validation

**Not performed.** These are prospective tests for a separately approved study or game prototype. Participant numbers, local decision thresholds, content warnings and access arrangements must be justified before data collection. This entry supplies neither a power claim nor an outcome. [C-16]

| Question and comparison | Procedure and relevant participants | Measures, confounds and falsification |
|---|---|---|
| Does partial validation aid inference or mostly aid enumeration? | Compare matched fresh cases with local, bundled and evidence-based feedback; include participants with different prior mystery familiarity. | Capture hypothesis explanations and query sequences, not only completion. Reject a reasoning-benefit claim if advantage disappears when the checker is absent or relies on cycling inputs. Repeated exposure and outside spoilers are confounds. |
| Is the crucial evidence sufficient for the intended conclusion? | Ask fresh readers/players to state alternatives before commitment; separately review access to each relevant clue. | A coherent unintended explanation is evidence of ambiguity to investigate, not automatically an incorrect response. Revise a unique-answer claim when no available discriminator separates a serious rival. |
| Does a red herring support meaningful revision rather than arbitrary reversal? | Compare the misleading route with a version retaining the same facts but removing the tempting implication. | Record the basis of revisions and whether disconfirming evidence was reachable. Reject necessity of the red herring if it only adds uninformative search or depends on an unstated stereotype. |
| Does a clue recap preserve the desired inference work? | Offer fact-only and relation-bearing help to participants who request the corresponding kind; avoid naïve help-user/nonuser comparisons. | Record unwanted spoilers, recovery from interruption and remaining ability to explain alternatives. Self-selection and reading preference matter. Adapt the recap when it silently answers a question the player wished to solve. |
| Are alternative representations evidentially comparable? | Compare witness text/audio, spatial descriptions and notebook representations with relevant access users and native-language reviewers. | Audit identity, reliability, timing and causal claims disclosed by each route. Reject a parity claim when one route omits a needed discriminator or supplies an unearned conclusion. |
| Do generated cases retain a defensible completion route? | Separately audit versioned case traces, then study a justified sample including difficult and disrupted states. | Distinguish generator contradiction, missing evidence, interface failure and player inference. Reject a blanket validity claim upon a valid counterexample; this is not a proposed universal reliability percentage. |

A solved case is not a test of general reasoning transfer. A later case must specify what relationship remains invariant; a different fictional law, testimony reliability rule or answer convention changes the task. Any claim about durable or real-world transfer needs an appropriate design beyond this bounded research. [C-16]
