# Survival

**Draft 0.1.0; heading coverage remains open.** This entry helps decide which pressures create useful choices about remaining viable, leaving shelter, spending limited means and recovering after a setback. Neither scarcity, environmental hardship nor permanent death is treated as a necessary cause of engagement, immersion or genre membership. No real-world survival or health advice is provided. [C-01, C-06]

## Definition and boundaries

**Working definition — inference:** survival foregrounds maintaining a vulnerable character or group’s capacity to continue under interacting environmental, temporal or resource constraints. The important question is what the player can do about vulnerability—not how many meters are displayed or whether an environment looks harsh. Limited means need competing uses or consequential tradeoffs to organize a decision. A small number by itself does not establish meaningful scarcity. [C-01, C-06]

| Neighbor / facet | Working distinction | Conditional application |
|---|---|---|
| Survival crafting | Transformation, tools, repair and capacity-building can be central. | Do not import a crafting progression tree as a requirement of survival. G15’s selected survival-crafting bytes were not retrieved. |
| Survival horror | Horror is an intended experience; maintaining viability is a decision structure. | Reuse the [inspected G01 boundary](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g14-review-packet.md#dep-survival-horror); do not assume hunger or terror is compulsory. |
| Management / colony simulation | Allocation can concern many people and long horizons. | Distinguish aggregate planning from the chosen survival viewpoint; do not transfer unseen G12/G13 conclusions. |
| Adventure / exploration | Discovery can remain valuable when some pressures are reduced. | State which viability problem remains and which activity a lower-pressure mode emphasizes. |
| Stealth / combat | Avoidance or confrontation may consume limited means. | Neither is required for this working definition; resource decisions can be entirely noncombat. |
| Open world, solo, cooperative, persistent world | Facets affecting information, time and ownership. | Shared stores, offline time and world persistence require explicit policy, not automatic genre assumptions. |

These are proposed routing distinctions, not mutually exclusive market categories or an approved taxonomy revision. [C-01]

## Custom research instructions

Follow an **outward-and-return decision**, not just a resource display. Record the initial viable state, reason to leave, time passing, consumption, renewal opportunities, uncertainty, shelter behavior and what remains after success or failure. Identify which costs are avoidable, chosen, informationally hidden or unavoidable. Separate scarcity in the model from the player’s understanding of it. [C-06, C-07]

Seek examples that challenge “more hardship is better”: a mode removing one pressure while retaining another; a viable noncombat loop; a renewable resource that becomes routine rather than interesting; and a recovery choice that preserves investment without promising the same emotional stakes. Inspect actual versions and modes. Current documentation is concentrated in two game families, not a representative sample of survival players. [C-02–C-05, C-13]

Future source routes should include dated official rules, build-specific expedition traces, economy/depletion observations over relevant horizons, developer accounts of removed upkeep, and studies of actual decisions. Do not turn a promotional statement about immersion or authentic wilderness into evidence of a player effect. Investigate culturally different relationships to land, community and subsistence rather than assuming solitary extraction is the default. [C-13, C-14]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Candidate promises include prudent preparation, improvising when conditions change, learning a place, caring for a group, constructing dependable refuge, or exploring with adjustable pressure. Some players may value relentless uncertainty; others may want recovery and planning without repeated maintenance. These are **hypotheses for audience research**, not established prevalence or a claim that removing costs preserves exactly the same experience. [C-08]

A player who understands a risk and returns early has not necessarily failed the design. Another who spends a reserve for an expressive goal may also understand it. Distinguish knowledge, chosen risk, desired experience and eventual outcome. Do not judge comprehension from whether a player hoards, consumes or selects a low-pressure mode. [C-07, C-11]

## Characteristic loops and structures

An analytical loop is **assess viability → prepare → pursue a need or chosen goal → respond to changed conditions → replenish, relocate or recover**. A session might be a short trip, establishing refuge or moving to a new resource region. A long horizon might concern depletion, renewal, expanding capacity or sustaining a community. These are proposed structures, not requirements for perpetual escalation or unlimited growth. [C-06]

The Long Dark’s official overview describes linked needs and selectable experiences. More importantly for a universal-loss claim, its undated permadeath description is qualified by the dated **v2.28, Build 136607** release note, which documents optional Cheat Death with penalties. This is a documented historical option—not a claim about every current mode or platform. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-G14-005] [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-G14-006]

Playdigious’s specific Subnautica modes FAQ distinguishes pressures: its “free” entry removes hunger and thirst while retaining oxygen. Its “extreme” and “creative” entries describe different consequence/resource contracts. The labels are retained as displayed; this is mobile-publisher documentation, not a verified PC rule set. A separate overview groups Freedom/Creative with wording that also removes oxygen pressure; that documentation ambiguity is preserved rather than silently reconciled. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-G14-007] [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-G14-011]

### Model a resource ecology, not merely a meter

The following decomposition is this draft’s analytical proposal. It concerns fictional game systems, not a realistic model of human physiology or ecosystems. [C-06]

| Dimension | Question that changes a decision | Failure to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Stock | How much is available now and who can use it? | Displayed ownership is mistaken for accessible capacity or uncommitted stock. |
| Flow and delay | How quickly is it consumed, renewed or transformed? | The same safe-looking stock has different viability under a changed time cost. |
| Location | Where can it be replenished, stored or recovered? | Return assumptions rely on a shelter or cache that no longer works. |
| Dependency | What other resource, permission or tool makes replenishment possible? | The recovery action requires the resource that failure already removed. |
| Persistence | What continues during travel, rest, menu use, absence or another player’s action? | The game surprises players with an uncommunicated time or ownership contract. |
| Optionality | Can the player decline, substitute, postpone or change the goal? | One compulsory chore repeats without a consequential choice. |

Resource abundance can still leave interesting logistical, route or timing decisions. Conversely, severe scarcity can collapse a decision into one obvious action or an unrecoverable state. These follow from the specified model; they do not establish how a particular audience will feel. [C-06, C-08]

### Conditional application process

1. **Identify the intended vulnerability.** Is the desired challenge preparation, navigation, adaptation, social care, combat or sustained upkeep? Name what should remain demanding when assistance is enabled. [C-08, C-10]
2. **Draw the usable loop.** Include replenishment, access requirements, time, likely return routes and recoverable state—not only acquisition and spending. Flag dependencies that can make recovery circular. [C-06]
3. **Create alternative choices before intensifying scarcity.** Options might include a shorter trip, a different objective, another shelter, an exchange, a substitute tool or accepting a disclosed consequence. These are illustrative candidates, not mandatory features. [C-07]
4. **Test what shelter promises.** Specify whether it blocks particular threats, suspends consumption, preserves belongings, supports saving, or only reduces exposure. “Safe” should not silently mean several incompatible things. [C-09]
5. **Compare pressure and consequence changes separately.** Slower depletion changes decisions differently from losing less progress, pausing, or removing a sensory barrier. Offer coherent modes without claiming experiential identity. [C-10]
6. **Review the loop after familiarity develops.** If routine upkeep displaces the intended decisions, reduce or automate it, introduce a genuinely different problem, or accept a calmer maintenance activity when that is the audience’s goal. Do not assume novelty must be manufactured indefinitely. [C-08, C-13]

## Pattern selection and adaptations

[Expedition return reserve](../patterns/expedition-return-reserve.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G14-PAT-02`, is a new **hypothesis-led** technique for exposing a discretionary-trip versus return-capacity tradeoff. Its generic comparison/stock aspects substantially overlap the inspected [Visible opportunity-cost comparison — GDR-CAND-20261010-F02-PAT-01](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g14-review-packet.md#dep-opportunity). Standalone extraction is provisional; a genre adaptation may be preferable. It applies when returning or reaching another viable destination is part of the actual goal. It is inappropriate as an obligatory rule for a one-way journey, consequence-free exploration or a game built around deliberately exhausting resources. [C-07]

Reuse [Configurable threat consequences — GDR-CAND-20261010-G01-PAT-01](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g14-review-packet.md#dep-threat) for separating encounter presentation, harm, progress consequences and continuing activity. That pattern does not prove that an optional penalty preserves fear. Reuse [Supported attempt and transfer check — GDR-CAND-20261010-F01-PAT-01](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g14-review-packet.md#dep-transfer) for resource prediction; do not duplicate its learning recipe. [C-10, C-11]

**Hybrid conflicts:** stealth may save a tool but spend travel time; construction may provide expressive goals while adding maintenance that undermines short expeditions; horror may seek uncertain safety while a declared refuge promises dependable recovery. Resolve these at the specific feature or mode. A threatening tone does not authorize breaking a previously stated save or refuge contract. These are local design hypotheses, not settled cross-genre priorities. [C-08–C-10]

## Representative cases and negative cases

The two official documentation families support **variation in rule contracts**, not observed enjoyment, realistic physiology, actual accessibility or a comprehensive genre survey. The dated Long Dark recovery option is a negative case for mandatory permanent loss. Subnautica’s granular mode descriptions are a negative case for treating every resource pressure as inseparable. Neither proves all pressure can be removed while preserving the same activity. [C-02–C-05]

### Illustrative worked example: a noncombat survey expedition

In a fictional observatory, an explorer uses a charge-powered environmental suit to survey unstable terrain. There are no opponents or combat actions. The task is to visit a marker and return with observations. A shelter may provide a recharge only while its fictional generator remains active. The player can inspect its operating state; no claim about actual protective equipment is implied. [C-06, C-07, C-09]

At one junction, the player has **12 fictional charge units**. A required observation costs 2, the current return route 6, and the player has chosen to protect 2 as contingency. Only 2 remain uncommitted under that policy, so a detour costing 4 does not preserve it. If a verified alternative destination reduces the return requirement to 2, that same detour fits with 2 remaining beyond the protected reserve. These are invented arithmetic examples, not recommended balance values or proof of safe return. [C-07, C-11]

The interface can express this through accessible ranges, route annotations or a planning summary rather than compulsory mental subtraction. An unknown route hazard remains unknown; the tool must not label the budget a guarantee. A player can intentionally spend the reserve, change the destination or cancel the survey. Ask whether the player understands the consequences before interpreting the choice. [C-07, C-11]

**Counterexample:** a one-way migration toward a new settlement has no meaningful return trip. The relevant budget concerns reaching the next viable destination, and enforcing a return reserve would misrepresent the objective. In a creative exploration mode without depletion, remove this technique rather than retaining a meaningless warning. A quiet mode can be worthwhile on its own terms without being claimed equivalent to pressured survival. [C-07, C-08]

## Accessibility and production implications

**Access proposal:** distinguish arithmetic, memory, continuous attention, navigation, sensory cues and precision inputs from the intended resource choice. Consider persistent plans, configurable reminders and safe interruption where compatible with the stated mode. Do not conceal exact information already available to sighted or hearing players from alternative routes merely to preserve “survival difficulty.” No universal font size, alert timing or mandatory audio level is prescribed. [C-10, C-11]

**Production inference:** validate depletion, renewals, inventory ownership, time advancement, caches, save/reload and recovery together. Test losing a critical tool, arriving at a disabled shelter, a blocked route and resuming after an interruption. In cooperative play, another player’s withdrawal can invalidate an individual reserve unless shared ownership rules account for it. The required cases follow from the chosen model; no implementation architecture or measured cost saving is asserted. [C-06, C-09, C-13]

**Ethical and localization hypotheses:** avoid presenting hunger, poverty, displacement or subsistence practices as evidence of personal worth. Review whose land and labor resource systems presume to be available. Distinguish “estimated,” “protected,” “required” and “guaranteed” in translation; collapsing these terms changes the decision. Allow evaluation participants to decline distressing themes or bodily-need mechanics without treating that as performance failure. These proposals need local audience and subject-matter review. [C-14]

## Research questions and validation

**Proposed only; no participant or gameplay test was performed.** Does an explicit return-capacity model improve comprehension of a chosen expedition policy without turning discovery into compulsory conservative play? Compare a stock-only display with a usable stock-plus-route estimate, keeping actual consumption, route geometry and available alternatives the same. Do not change both warning information and difficulty without labeling the intervention. [C-11]

Include matched trials with the same stock and detour but different return requirements, and a trial in which the destination legitimately no longer replenishes. Ask the rule-based feasibility question before feedback; then separately ask whether the participant wishes to take the detour. Counterbalance order, retain accommodations, and distinguish new-state prediction from an explicitly changed rule. Success on one example is not evidence of learned transfer. [C-11]

Recruit by relevant game familiarity and supported sensory, motor, numerical, memory and language needs. Record resource and route predictions, explanation of remaining uncertainty, chosen risk, recovery feasibility, attention spent on upkeep, and desired exploration experience. Confounds include route familiarity, imposed risk preference, arithmetic load, assist visibility and emotional attachment to lost progress. No physiological or clinical outcome is inferred. [C-11, C-13]

**Local decision rule to preregister:** reject or revise the reserve display when it creates systematic false certainty, when participants read it as compulsory behavior despite understanding alternatives, or when it displaces the desired exploration without sufficient benefit. Evaluate the simpler reduced-upkeep alternative too. Specify practical tolerances and recruitment before testing; no general sample guarantee, reserve percentage or acceptable death rate follows from this evidence. [C-11, C-13]
