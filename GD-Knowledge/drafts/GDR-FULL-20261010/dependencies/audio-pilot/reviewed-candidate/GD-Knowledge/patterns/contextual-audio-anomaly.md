# Contextual audio anomaly

**Draft, hypothesis-led pattern candidate; not validated, accepted or released.** Version 0.1.0. Identity and source IDs are snapshot-scoped proposals. Evidence checking means inspection of the cited public passages on 2026-10-10, not a prototype, a played game sequence, participant results or approval. The same sources occur in the owning [Audio tension and ambience topic — GDK-TOP-0001](../topics/audio-tension-and-ambience.md); that reuse is not corroboration.

## Purpose

**Intended outcome:** make a change in an environment worth anticipating or investigating while the player still understands the task and controls sensory comfort. The candidate mechanism is a recognized relationship becoming unsettled, not simply making an event louder or adding another layer. The proposed additional value over a comparable isolated cue is **unestablished**. [C-01]

Use this as a way to formulate a testable option, not an instruction that all exploration should become tense. The topic owns definitions and alternatives; this file owns one conditional recipe. [C-03, C-09]

## Context and prerequisites

Consider the pattern only when a scene has an unresolved, potentially consequential situation; a player could perceive a relevant ordinary relationship; and the subsequent change permits a meaningful response such as checking, waiting, proceeding or withdrawing. Sound need not identify the exact cause, but required action information cannot depend on a sensory channel the player cannot access. These are proposed prerequisites, not empirically established necessary conditions for all tension. [C-01, C-03, C-04]

Before using it, write down the intended experience, minimum task facts, existing player knowledge, legitimate cue meanings, acceptable uncertainty, settings/access options, and the simplest competing option. Context may already be established by earlier play or knowledge of the environment; creating additional ambience is not inherently required. Recognition must be checked rather than inferred from time spent in the scene. [C-03, C-05]

**Platform/input assumptions:** none for the abstract recipe. Any later application must identify device, listening environment, display/caption legibility, input, and relevant hearing/attention needs. The linked proposed formative study is deliberately adult, seated and non-VR; it cannot validate every platform or player population. [C-05]

## Mechanism and evidence

**Hypothesis:** recognized relationship → noticeable deviation → potentially consequential interpretation → unresolved anticipation with useful choices. Detection and interpretation can succeed while anticipation fails. [C-01, C-03]

The perceptual precedent is an artificial sound-scene experiment, not an emotional game experiment. Creator accounts demonstrate reported approaches, not effectiveness. Official access guidance constrains information design. Film findings provide boundary challenges and repeated-exposure cautions. Their full records and exact limitations are below. No source directly validates the complete chain or the matched contextual-versus-isolated gameplay comparison. [C-01, C-03, C-04, C-06, C-07]

**Do not turn context, recognition and deviation into three mandatory authored layers.** They are candidate functions that can be combined, supplied by existing knowledge, changed, or rejected. An anomaly relative to one player’s knowledge can be an ordinary sound to another; a legible warning may be preferable to both. [C-03, C-06]

## Recipe

All steps, branches and parameters below are **proposed applications** of the hypothesis, not performed work or studio-approved implementation requirements. No prescribed duration, sound level, asset count or probability has empirical authority here. [C-05, C-09]

### 1. Specify the information and experience contract

Name the unsettled question and what the player must know now. Separate “something occurred beyond this door” from the optional unknown “what caused it?” Define available actions and ensure the scene does not require guessing an inaccessible critical fact.

**Exit:** when the scene’s purpose is comfort, precision or rapid response and the extra uncertainty would undermine it, select no added technique or an overt accessible warning instead. [C-04, C-05, C-06]

### 2. Identify the least costly recognizable relationship

Use an existing sound-to-source relationship, recurring activity, environmental state, or learned cue meaning. An audible baseline need not be dense, realistic in every detail, or newly introduced. Prefer one relationship the player can actually notice over an elaborate bed whose intended meaning cannot be described.

**Adjustable variables:** source recognizability, regularity, relevance to the task, prior exposure, and the amount of competing information. **Branch:** when no relevant recognition can be established without disrupting the task, test an isolated cue or an explicit warning; do not extend setup indefinitely. [C-01, C-03, C-05]

### 3. Introduce one interpretable deviation

Change an aspect that matters to the proposed relationship: an unexpected continuation, an unfamiliar event from an established location, or a known event occurring under an inconsistent state. For this pattern’s initial test, retain a perceivable event; omission is a separate comparison choice, not a second extracted pattern.

**Adjustable variables:** identity, timing, location, duration, spectral character, onset contrast and uncertainty about cause. **Control:** do not attribute an effect to context when loudness, threat proximity or visual staging also changed. **Branch:** if the target cannot be noticed comfortably, repair legibility or drop it; increasing intensity is not the automatic solution. [C-01, C-02, C-05]

### 4. Protect meaningful player response

Allow checking, pausing where appropriate, continuing or leaving according to the game’s actual contract. Avoid turning the event into an unavoidable punishment for not hearing or not interpreting a subtle cue. Record whether the sound is decorative, probabilistically informative, or a required warning; never let its category change silently for the player or the accessibility presentation.

**Exit:** if a required response is urgent, use clearer information rather than forcing an emotional ambiguity requirement onto the task. [C-04, C-05, C-06]

### 5. Author the accessible information at the same time

Specify an appropriate caption or alternative representation of the event and its relevant direction, without inventing knowledge the sound itself does not disclose. A caption might identify a scrape, not an unseen attacker. Support chosen audio-category levels and mono use; ensure essential information is not lost when decorative ambience is reduced. A text equivalent is an access design to evaluate, not proof of identical emotion. [C-04, C-05]

**Branch:** when a non-audio signal would necessarily disclose more detail than intended, reconsider what ambiguity is legitimate or revise the event. Do not withhold critical information to preserve a hearing-only mystery. [C-04]

### 6. Bound repetition, reliability and resolution

Document first exposure, return visits, saved/reloaded states, repeated interactions and meaningful exceptions. Preserve enough consistency that a player can understand the cue’s role. Variation may change presentation, but should not secretly change a critical signal’s meaning.

**Branch:** when repetition turns uncertainty into competent prediction, decide whether that is desirable learning. Do not assume fear must be restored or that randomization prevents habituation. If the intended effect depends on first exposure, label that limit. [C-05, C-07, C-08]

### 7. Compare and keep, narrow or remove

After separate authorization, compare A (context + deviation), B (matched isolated deviation), and C (overt warning) under the proposed playtest below. Keep the technique only as far as the actual evidence warrants. Remove or simplify it when it adds no useful aftermath anticipation, confuses required action, undermines comfort/agency, or cannot be made accessible within scope. [C-01, C-05, C-06, C-09]

## Worked examples

### Illustrative example — inspection alcove

**Not a shipped-game observation, audio asset, prototype, or test result.** A player is inspecting a maintenance indicator beside a closed service door. Ordinary cycles of a nearby mechanism end cleanly. After an interval of normal activity, one cycle has an extra scrape from beyond the door. Nothing has yet established whether the cause is harmless or threatening. [C-05]

The contract is to make a change noticeable and investigable, not to communicate a confirmed enemy. The player can finish checking the indicator, listen or inspect further, wait, or leave. A configurable caption such as “[scrape beyond service door]” preserves the event and location without declaring its cause. The caption text is illustrative and would need evaluation for timing, reading burden and access; it is not a validated solution. [C-04, C-05]

| Authoring decision | Candidate choice | Failure branch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary relationship | A recurring cycle has a recognizable ending. | No reliable recognition: simplify or abandon contextual preparation. |
| Deviation | One additional scrape; do not also move the threat or increase intensity in the A/B comparison. | Onset impact alone explains reports: revise or reject the mechanism claim. |
| Ambiguity | Cause unresolved; event and available actions understandable. | Player interprets a mandatory task instruction incorrectly: clarify. |
| Return visit | The event’s informational role remains consistent, with repeat exposure logged. | Player learns a useful cue: consider accepting mastery, not endlessly escalating surprise. |
| Access | Caption/visual facts and chosen volume settings support the same task decision. | A required fact disappears without sound: block advancement of the candidate. |

**B alternative:** the same scrape occurs at the same task point without first establishing the recurring cycle relationship; match exposure and event-time acoustics as far as practicable. **C alternative:** a recognizable explicit alert communicates the same minimum disturbance fact. Neither comparator was created or tested. [C-05]

### Contextualized creator accounts

The SOMA account is relevant to grounding an event in a coherent place; the Rogue Incursion account is relevant to limiting density rather than assuming more ambience is better. They are two production contexts, both horror-selected, and do not establish a transferable recipe or measured emotional result. No game build or recording was directly observed in this pilot. [C-03, C-08; SRC-0003, SRC-0006]

## Tradeoffs and alternatives

The hypothesized benefit is richer interpretation of a changed relationship, not guaranteed stronger tension. The costs include preparation time, reliance on prior knowledge, state/cue authoring and tests across settings/exposure. An isolated cue can be more economical; an explicit warning can be more appropriate when information matters; reduced audio can preserve attention; a missing expected event can be tested when omission itself is the real decision. None is established as universally preferable. [C-01, C-02, C-06, C-08]

**Illustrative contextual adaptations:** horror-oriented ambiguity must still respect the information contract; exploration-oriented curiosity may warrant a discoverable ordinary explanation; a comfort-oriented scene may deliberately avoid an anomaly. A hybrid should resolve the scene’s priorities explicitly rather than average incompatible promises. These are not genre findings or future genre-file contents. `related_genres` remains empty. [C-05, C-09]

## Failure cases

| Observable sign in later evaluation | Interpretation to investigate | Candidate repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The player notices nothing unusual. | Baseline not recognized, target masked, or irrelevant expectation. | Verify recognizability and legibility; replace with a simpler cue if necessary. |
| The cue is noticed but only surprises/startles. | The change lacks consequential future meaning. | Measure the aftermath separately; do not relabel the startle as anticipation. |
| Participants wait aimlessly or search the wrong object. | Required task facts became ambiguous. | Clarify action possibilities or use an overt warning. |
| A decorative event is treated as a guaranteed attack signal. | Learned reliability differs from author intent. | Revise cue roles/consistency; do not add arbitrary deceptive events by default. |
| Captions disclose an attacker that hearing-only play could not identify. | Alternative modality changes the information, not just its presentation. | Revisit the event’s information contract and wording. |
| Essential information disappears under preferred settings. | Critical sound was wrongly treated as decorative, or direction/caption access fails. | Provide the necessary facts through usable alternatives and verify them. |
| Sensory discomfort or loss of control is reported. | The desired effect or its intensity is unwelcome. | Stop or reduce/omit the technique; offer meaningful control. |
| Repeat exposure lowers tension while task success improves. | Learning, not necessarily design failure. | Narrow the intended use to first exposure or accept the changed role. |

These are **proposed observable failure signs**, not events found in a completed pilot. Their mechanisms remain to be tested. [C-02, C-04, C-05, C-07]

## Counterexamples and do not use conditions

**Strong opposing case:** the player already knows a familiar warning and can act on it; authoring a new ambient preparation may add time and clutter without adding anticipation. The principal sources never compared that case to this recipe. The burden of evidence is on the extra preparation, not on the simpler option. [C-01, C-06]

**Empirical boundary:** the film experiment in SRC-0004 does not depend on a demonstrated three-stage ambient recipe; it cautions against that unrestricted necessity claim. It is not a clean isolated-cue game experiment, and it cannot establish the best replacement. [C-06]

**Do not use** when ambiguous interpretation conflicts with a required timely action, the desired experience is undisturbed comfort, no relevant baseline is recognizable, meaningful response is unavailable, or accessible information/comfortable settings cannot be maintained. A scene can fail this pattern’s purpose even when arousal increases. These are conditional design inferences, not universal claims that those scenes cannot evoke tension by other means. [C-02, C-04, C-05, C-06]

## Playtest

**PROPOSED — NOT RUN.** Full protocol owner: [topic’s Playtest and validation section](../topics/audio-tension-and-ambience.md#playtest-and-validation). This summary identifies the same protocol, not a second study. No recruitment, prototype, participant data, technical measurements or playtests occurred. [C-05, C-09]

**Compare** A (recognized context + deviation) against B (identical target without establishing that predictive relationship), plus C (an explicit warning of the same minimum state facts). Match task, scene, threat behavior and consequences. Hold A/B waveform, event position/onset, duration and event-time mix comparable; document remaining history, acoustic, spatial, familiarity and attentional differences. C compares strategies and necessarily changes semantic explicitness. [C-05]

**Proposed participants/order:** 18 adult formative sessions, three per six A/B/C orders; separate six-session access-focused review by people who rely on non-audio information. These numbers cover order variants and provide a resource boundary, not statistical power or representativeness. Use matched cue families and repeat exposures; measure actual baseline recognition and prior familiarity. Record device, environment, chosen settings, input and access requirements; do not claim that a small subgroup represents a population. [C-05]

**Separate outcomes:** aftermath anticipation and unease; immediate surprise and reflex-like startle; confusion; fear/perceived threat; comfort; agency; required task-fact comprehension and action reasons. Proposed local 0–6 scales and a 2–12-second aftermath window are not validated standards. Behavioral checking or pausing is not by itself a fear measure. [C-05]

**Falsification/narrowing rules:** the topic’s proposed one-point median A–B advantage on aftermath anticipation is a formative advancement threshold only. No added value favors simplification, without proving equivalence. A failed recognition check or a difference explained only by loudness/startle rejects the claimed mechanism. First-exposure-only benefit narrows applicability. Critical information failure, a proposed one-point median comfort/agency loss, or distress blocks advancement of that version; any distress/withdrawal stops the session. Report individuals and uncertainty, not a success narrative assembled from selected measures. Freeze the exact protocol before any later execution. [C-05]

Obtain informed consent; permit adjustment, pauses and withdrawal without penalty. Check required facts without sound and with actual access users. Preservation of information is distinct from emotional equivalence. No part of these instructions authorizes execution now. [C-04, C-05, C-09]
