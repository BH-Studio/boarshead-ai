# Immersive simulation

**Draft 0.1.0; evidence and coverage remain open.** This entry supports deciding which interactions a game promises, how players can infer their limits, and which consequences recognize a chosen method. It does not certify genre membership, validate immersion, or prescribe a first-person camera. The local definition and recommendations below are explicitly inference or hypothesis, not an official standard. [C-01, C-05]

## Definition and boundaries

**Working definition:** a design family in which player-directed methods, interacting rules and consequential world responses are substantial organizing activities. “Simulation” here concerns selected relationships that can be used and combined, not exhaustive physical realism. An authored objective can coexist with latitude in how it is achieved. This routing definition is proposed for Library Coordination; it is not an approved taxonomy amendment. [C-01]

Spector’s Deus Ex retrospective describes mixed genre influences, multiple methods and authored goals; its local principles put entertainment ahead of literal realism when those conflict. The launch account for Weird West explicitly uses the label for a top-down game. These establish particular practitioners’ usage, not a consensus definition or measured player benefit. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-G14-001] [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-G14-008]

| Neighbor or facet | Boundary used in this draft | Consequence for research |
|---|---|---|
| Simulation | A model can emphasize fidelity, practice or management without making improvised problem-solving central. | Compare usable relationships, not counts of simulated variables. G12’s selected Simulation bytes were not retrieved. |
| Sandbox | Open goals or construction do not necessarily promise broad interaction across systems. | Ask whether the game grants open ends, open methods, both, or neither; G13’s selected sandbox bytes were not retrieved. |
| Stealth | Managing detection may be one method or the central activity. | Preserve the specific evidence and recovery contract in the [stealth entry](stealth.md); do not require every obstacle to be bypassable unseen. |
| Role-playing / adventure / puzzle | Character differentiation, interpretation and authored solutions can overlap. | A method-specific puzzle can legitimately reject other methods when that restriction is communicated. |
| First person, top-down, open world, multiplayer | Facets rather than membership tests here. | Camera changes what can be observed; multiplayer changes who can alter a shared state. Neither substitutes for analyzing the promised interaction. |

The table is an analytical distinction, not a claim that these families are disjoint. An entry may describe a game mode or sequence more accurately than a whole product label. [C-01]

## Custom research instructions

Trace a **permission and consequence chain**, not a montage of impressive solutions. For a future observed case, record the objective, attempted verb, target properties, relevant rule, actual response, state persistence, feedback and any constraint violated. Record an interaction that fails as carefully as one that works. Neither a developer’s intention nor a clip proves the same affordance exists throughout a game. [C-05, C-11]

Seek three kinds of contrary evidence: a simpler authored solution that better supports the intended task; a supposedly systemic interaction that is inaccessible or unintelligible; and an exception that improves the experience rather than merely exposing an unfinished system. Current case selection is weighted toward English-language developers in overlapping design traditions. It does not establish prevalence across cultures, audiences or production scales. [C-04, C-11]

Research source routes should include build-specific action traces, creator descriptions of rejected mechanics, information-policy documentation and participant predictions before feedback. Inspect multiplayer state ownership, assist settings and mods separately when relevant. A future player study should distinguish “I predicted it,” “I could do it,” “the game acknowledged it” and “I valued doing it.” Do not use any one answer as a surrogate for the others. [C-05, C-09]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Candidate promises include improvising from learned relationships, expressing a preferred method, discovering consequences, and pursuing a plan without following a single prescribed sequence. These are **hypotheses about possible audiences**, not measured expectations of everyone who uses the label. Some players may prefer dependable tools and clear objectives; others may prefer discovering hidden relationships. They can disagree while understanding the same rules. [C-06]

Freedom also includes declining violence, abandoning a plan or accepting an imperfect outcome where the design supports those choices. It does not entail unlimited resources, universal persuasion, equal rewards, or exemption from previously stated commitments. An “anything works” description is inappropriate when only specially tagged objects or one scripted solution are accepted. Equally, a game need not promise “anything works” to offer worthwhile agency. [C-05, C-06]

## Characteristic loops and structures

A proposed local loop is **observe → infer a usable relationship → attempt or combine methods → inspect consequences → revise the model or plan**. A session can center on an objective with multiple methods; a longer horizon can center on changing access, relationships, capacities and world state. These are analysis tools, not a universal sequence or established psychological mechanism. [C-05]

Three distinctions make the loop actionable. **Available method** means the necessary state and resources actually exist, not merely that a designer can imagine it. **Consistent affordance** means a learned relation transfers within its declared scope; it does not require identical behavior across visibly different materials, actors or permissions. **Consequence** includes recognition and altered opportunities, not only damage, currency or a branching ending. [C-05]

The strongest counterweight to maximal simulation in this corpus is the Mark of the Ninja retrospective: its authors describe an initially unfocused prototype rich in simulation that did not deliver the desired play. That supports investigating focus and production tradeoffs; it does not prove simulation is generally undesirable or that one simplification caused the finished game’s quality. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-G14-003]

### Conditional application process

1. **State the promise at the obstacle.** Write the required outcome and any method constraint separately. Determine which actor accepts it and which downstream changes follow. Reuse the reviewed W02 objective contract rather than inventing a second recognition system. [C-07]
2. **Choose a small interaction surface.** List the properties players can discover and verbs that operate on them. Select relationships relevant to this activity. Record deliberately unsupported combinations and how their limits can be discovered without irreversible punishment. This is a prototype proposal, not a fixed production quota. [C-05]
3. **Test combinations and exceptions.** Include reordered actions, reused objects, altered access, interrupted attempts and an unavailable dependency. A missed solution may be an uncommunicated rule, an implementation defect, or a deliberate task restriction; diagnose before adding more branches. [C-05, C-08]
4. **Expose the decision, not necessarily the answer.** Make actionable facts usable through supported modalities. Do not reveal hidden identity simply because a sound has become text. Do not conceal an already-public rule in order to manufacture uncertainty. [C-08]
5. **Measure prediction, feasibility, preference and aftermath separately.** Include trials with genuinely different correct predictions. Retain accommodations; remove only optional answer-leading help when appropriate. [C-09]
6. **Stop expanding where the promise cannot be supported.** Narrow the verb/material scope, explicitly author the encounter, or remove the claimed freedom. A smaller dependable system is an alternative to accumulating exceptions that nobody can reliably explain. Whether it is preferable needs local testing. [C-05, C-11]

## Pattern selection and adaptations

[Multi-route objective contract — GDR-CAND-20261010-W02-PAT-02](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g14-review-packet.md#dep-objective) is the primary existing pattern: separate achieved state, satisfied conditions, acceptance and consequences. Do not create duplicate “many solutions” guidance. [Supported attempt and transfer check — GDR-CAND-20261010-F01-PAT-01](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g14-review-packet.md#dep-transfer) applies to learning a relationship, not to proving that a preferred method is optimal. [C-07, C-09]

Use [Evidence-bounded search and reacquisition](../patterns/evidence-bounded-search-and-reacquisition.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G14-PAT-01`, only when actor knowledge is itself a manipulable relationship. It is unnecessary for an environment without searching agents. [Fact-preserving cue alternatives — GDR-CAND-20261010-P02-PAT-02](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g14-review-packet.md#dep-cues) concerns information access, not approval of an exact implementation or identical emotion. [C-08]

**Hybrid conflicts:** a stealth achievement may restrict a method that the broader objective accepts; a survival resource cost may make a technically possible route practically unavailable; a tightly authored mystery may intentionally protect an inference from direct manipulation. State those boundaries. Do not silently trade a promised nonviolent completion for a forced combat recovery sequence. These are design hypotheses requiring the game’s explicit commitments, not categorical prohibitions. [C-06, C-10]

## Representative cases and negative cases

The inspected Deus Ex and Weird West accounts serve as **definition and intention examples**, not gameplay observations. Mark of the Ninja supplies a negative production account for “more interaction simulation is always better.” Leonard’s technical account also includes deliberately privileged sensing behavior, so it is a counterexample to treating plausible agents as literally restricted to physiological perception. [C-02–C-04, C-12]

### Illustrative worked example: protect an archive during a flood

This invented scenario is not a shipped-game observation. The stated objective is **keep the records dry until transport arrives**. The available tools divert water through connected channels, move portable crates, or request temporary storage from a caretaker. The caretaker’s permission is a separate social rule; moving records without it can meet the physical objective while violating a declared commitment. [C-05, C-07]

A prototype would track water level, outlet connectivity, storage permission and record condition. It would not need fluid turbulence or a complete social simulation. A portable crate blocks a narrow channel but not a wide drain: the distinction must be inspectable rather than a hidden quest exception. Diverting water into a occupied shelter should not count as satisfying an explicitly stated “without flooding occupied rooms” condition. If that condition was not part of the objective, the consequence can still matter, but it should not be retroactively presented as a rule the player knowingly broke. [C-05, C-07]

A narrower authored alternative gives the player one repair procedure and makes sequencing under time pressure the task. That may be the better design when repairing a particular mechanism is the promise. Another alternative removes time pressure and makes the space a safe experiment. Neither needs to mimic the other’s audience or emotional effect. [C-06, C-10]

## Accessibility and production implications

**Access hypothesis:** make object properties, target selection, action confirmation and persistent consequences available through the configurations the product supports. Offer inspectable history or reminders where remembering every prior experiment is incidental to the desired challenge. Distinguish a planning aid from an automatic solution; offer confirmation for irreversible actions without forcing every interaction through an obstructive dialog. Actual access performance still requires representative testing. [C-08, C-09]

**Production inference:** every meaningful cross-system relationship creates cases involving ordering, persistence, invalid states and feedback. Budget authoring and verification around the chosen promise, not around a marketing count of systems. Localization must preserve whether an utterance expresses possibility, permission, uncertainty or a binding objective; losing that distinction can change the task. If agents can mislead, distinguish their claim from a trustworthy system acknowledgement. [C-05, C-11]

**Ethical hypothesis:** avoid equating social persuasion with universal consent or treating all people as manipulable resources. Test whether supposedly nonviolent alternatives actually respect the promised boundaries. A refusal or consequence can be intentional, but it must not be disguised as evidence that a player lacks competence or moral worth. [C-10]

## Research questions and validation

**Proposed; no participant, prototype or gameplay test was performed.** The principal question is whether a bounded, explicitly scoped interaction set supports transferable prediction and valued improvisation better than a broader, exception-heavy set in the intended activity. [C-09, C-11]

Prepare counterbalanced illustrative tasks with matched objectives, available time and accessible information. Separate a **same-rule transfer** condition with changed objects from an explicitly **changed-rule** condition. Include both valid and invalid uses, not two cosmetic scenes with the same answer. Ask for the predicted physical result and objective status before feedback; then ask which action the participant prefers. Allow equivalent nonverbal responses and keep accommodations available. [C-09]

Recruit by familiarity with systemic games and relevant sensory, motor, memory and language needs; do not infer those from demographic stereotypes. Record correct predictions, unsupported-rule assumptions, recovery after unexpected outcomes, chosen methods and stated value of those methods. Distinguish novelty, practice, interface prompts and arithmetic demands as confounds. A commercial game’s success is not a control condition. [C-09, C-11]

**Decision rule proposed for local preregistration:** adapt or reject the broader interaction set when its extra relationships mainly increase unrecoverable misunderstandings or verification burden without improving the targeted experience. Do not call a small null result proof of equivalence. Set acceptable error, burden and recruitment targets with the actual team before collecting results; no universal numerical threshold is justified here. [C-11]
