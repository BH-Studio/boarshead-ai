## Author self-review — not independent review

**Reviewer:** the same authoring assistant, adversarial pass during this G21 response. No independent reviewer, agent, participant study or external audit is asserted. Changes made before initial delivery retain the same candidate IDs and `initial-authoring-1` revision.

**Strongest recommendation challenged:** make public constraints and return conditions legible while preserving chosen alternatives. The strongest objection is that the proposed legibility can erase a genre’s desired knowledge work, and recovery can destroy the satisfaction of finality. “More choice” and “more time participating” are not adequate rebuttals. The pattern prerequisites were therefore restricted to already-public constraints and deliberately recoverable activities, with explicit omission conditions and comparisons to simpler alternatives.

**MMO challenge:** offering optional/asynchronous roles may dissolve the reliable mutual dependence that a selected group wants. The draft retains mandatory collaboration as a legitimate scoped alternative rather than treating independence as a universal welfare improvement. Persistence is not itself a sufficient justification for building a costly MMO.

**MOBA challenge:** structured communication can be too shallow for expert coordination, while shared progression may redistribute rather than eliminate blame. The draft presents optional rich communication and genuinely different resource models; it does not claim ping systems solve toxicity. Poor play and silence are not treated as proof of malicious intent. The Tribunal study’s negative prediction and selection limitations remain explicit.

**Battle royale challenge:** technically successful re-entry can create immediate repeat defeat, coercive rescue and diminished stakes. The draft evaluates useful decisions, chosen spectating and teammate burden instead of simply counting return events. A strict-elimination design can legitimately reject PAT-02.

**Source challenge:** official documents are strong evidence of what creators describe, but weak evidence of player effects. The two empirical studies are historical and observational. No pattern is elevated above low confidence in efficacy. The EA guide was checked in context and explicitly says Core, preventing a tempting but unsupported reassignment of its new rules to another mode.

**Ownership challenge:** both patterns touch shared domain concepts. Their distinctive proposed contribution is respectively joint constraint/opportunity/information authoring and explicit temporary/terminal recovery states. They may still duplicate or fit better as extensions of unseen F06/W03 work. Canonical novelty and ownership remain unresolved; no new permanent concept is asserted.

**Accessibility challenge:** better disclosure cannot repair an infeasible movement requirement or a burdensome social contract. F01’s discriminating examples, the separation of prediction from preference, and P01’s scoped numerical correction remain intact. No unsupported threshold, universal balance rule or taxonomy stereotype survived as an adopted conclusion.

**Residual author limits:** no current game build, contemporary causal BR evidence, independent rerun of the papers, live moderation evidence or complete latest shared dependency selection was available here. These limit efficacy and coverage claims even though the author drafts are complete as artifacts.
