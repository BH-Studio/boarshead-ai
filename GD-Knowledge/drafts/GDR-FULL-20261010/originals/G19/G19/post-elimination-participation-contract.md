# Post-elimination participation contract

**Draft research candidate, not released knowledge.** Scope: G19 only. Definitions and application recipes are reversible research proposals, not approved studio decisions. No game implementation, participant test, independent review or publication occurred in this batch.

## Purpose

**Post-elimination participation contract** addresses the interval after someone loses an active role while other people continue the shared activity. Its purpose is to make the remaining options intelligible: what the person knows, may say or do, can still influence, and may decline. The intended benefit is **valued and voluntary participation without accidental changes to the information game**. That benefit remains a hypothesis, not a result established by the existence of a ghost, audience or support role. [E-01, E-05]

“Elimination” includes losing an active seat, ability or phase role; it need not mean death in the fiction. The pattern applies to some [party](../genres/party.md), [social-deduction](../genres/social-deduction.md) and round-based hybrids. It does not prescribe keeping everyone busy until the round ends. A break or a no-elimination structure can be the correct outcome of applying the pattern. [E-01, E-03]

## Context and prerequisites

Use it only when a continuing activity creates a meaningful transition out of active play, and the design can state the rights of active players, former players, observers and moderators. Identify what the transitioning person already knows, what new information a spectator view exposes, which channels they can use, and the remaining time/commitment as locally understood. Do not promise an exact wait when it is unknown. [E-01, E-04]

Prerequisites are a comprehensible rules boundary, a feasible communication/access route, a voluntary rest/exit option, and production responsibility for transition, recovery and moderation. A new post-elimination ability changes gameplay; it requires configuration-specific evaluation rather than being treated as an engagement-only interface feature. [E-01, E-04–E-06]

Exit this pattern without adding a continuing role when the round ends for everyone together, the group prefers a break, no desired accessible contribution is available, or managing the information boundary exceeds available support. Redesigning the round or using a no-elimination variant is a substantive alternative, not a failure to meet a participation quota. [E-03, E-05]

## Mechanism and evidence

The inspected rules give contrasting feasibility examples: Clocktower retains selected rights after death; the versioned Influencer description deliberately permits constrained ghost communication; One Night’s publisher describes no elimination. None is evidence that one structure causes belonging or is appropriate to every group. [E-02, E-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-G19-011; GDR-SRC-20261010-G19-015; GDR-SRC-20261010-G19-016]

| Proposed causal link | Evidence class / support | Limit or contrary pathway |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit transition → a participant can know the remaining rights | Inference from stated rules and an accessible explanation. [E-01] | A displayed explanation may still be missed or misunderstood; comprehension must be checked. |
| Matched knowledge/channel permissions → fewer unintended information routes | Logical information-boundary inference, adapted from reviewed P02-PAT-02. [E-04] | Does not prevent every social leak or prove enforcement; deliberate bounded disclosure can be a valid rule. |
| Bounded continuing influence → valued agency | Hypothesis, with documentary examples of feasibility. [E-02, E-05] | Influence may be irrelevant, burdensome or competitively distorting. |
| Genuine rest/exit choice → less coerced continuation | Ethical/design hypothesis and scope distinction, not a measured result. [E-05, E-06] | A nominal button or verbal offer can be socially costly; available is not equivalent to freely chosen. |
| More activity → greater belonging | **Not supported as a general link.** [E-07] | Activity can rise while preferred contribution and perceived inclusion fall. |

This pattern specializes a phase transition with changes in **knowledge, influence and participation choice**. It does not replace the existing cue-alternatives pattern or a genre-neutral safe-exit topic. Coordination may still decide its scope belongs under another domain or overlaps with pending work. No duplicate-clearing receipt is asserted.

## Recipe

1. **Describe the loss precisely.** Identify which action, ability, seat or right ends and what continues. Separate personal scoring, team outcome, nomination, voting, talking and observing rather than applying one blanket “out” state. Ask whether a continuing phase is needed at all. [E-01, E-03]
2. **List knowledge before and after the transition.** Include private role information, previously observed events, hidden truth revealed by spectator tools, and public round history. Mark what remains uncertain. If a spectator view exposes additional truth, do not silently retain all outgoing channels. [E-04]
3. **Choose permitted influence as a rules decision.** Options include none, continued discussion, a limited decision resource, a constrained message or another declared role-specific action. State allowed recipients and timing. A blanket “ghosts never communicate” rule is inappropriate when the chosen rules intentionally allow bounded communication. [E-02, E-04]
4. **Offer a real choice among feasible roles and rest.** Explain what each option can actually contribute; do not call a decorative task equivalent to strategic influence. Allow decline without a public justification requirement. When only observation/rest is feasible, describe it honestly rather than inventing a meaningless chore. [E-01, E-05, E-06]
5. **Make the new state accessible without revealing more than allowed.** Explain remaining controls, information and exit through the participant’s usable route. Distinguish a message sent from a message successfully understood; do not turn an intentionally ambiguous signal into certain hidden truth. Reuse the inspected information-preserving cue guidance. [E-01, E-04]
6. **Define the end and re-entry.** State whether the role ends at a reveal, round boundary, timeout or voluntary withdrawal; whether a return is possible; and what a returning participant may know. Reconnection must not grant a fresh limited resource or erase a safety choice without an explicit rule. These are design checks, not implementation claims. [E-01, E-04, E-06]
7. **Audit both intentional and unintended disclosure.** Later authorized checks should exercise ordinary chat, private messages, overlays, spectators, alternative cues and shared-room speech appropriate to the platform. Distinguish a forbidden leak from a message that the rules intentionally authorize. If the contract cannot be sustained, reduce spectator information, change allowed influence, or omit the continuing role. [E-04]
8. **Evaluate agency, burden and the game itself.** Compare the continuing-role option with rest and a feasible no-elimination structure. Inspect desired contribution, rule comprehension, information leakage, strategic effect and preference separately. Retain the simpler option when the proposed role has no participant-valued benefit. [E-03, E-05, E-07]

## Adjustable variables

| Variable | Local options | Constraint to document |
|---|---|---|
| Transition trigger | Elimination, seat rotation, failed phase, lost ability | What ends and what remains; no ambiguous all-purpose “dead” flag. |
| Knowledge change | Preserve prior information; reveal public facts; reveal more | Permitted recipients and uncertainty for every newly visible fact. |
| Remaining influence | Discussion, constrained action, limited resource, none | Rule-specific scope and ending; no universal vote/message allocation. |
| Optionality | Continue, observe, rest, leave | Actual social and interface cost of declining; no forced personal explanation. |
| Communication | Private/shared, live/structured, persistent/transient | Access, expression, timing, moderation and disclosure implications. |
| Re-entry | Next round, selected phase, no mid-round return | State reset, previous knowledge, remaining influence and access preferences. |
| Success | Chosen contribution and tolerable burden under local goals | Not inferred from activity count, elapsed time or host approval. |

These are design variables under [E-01, E-04–E-07], not approved defaults.

## Worked examples

**Illustrative hidden-role prototype.** A participant loses the ability to initiate actions but keeps the information learned during play. The proposed configuration permits a limited contribution before the shared outcome. The design exposes only the same authorized information in the accessible private view, shows whether the contribution remains, and permits declining it. If a full spectator view reveals secret identities, taking that view changes available outgoing channels. An alternative configuration intentionally allows a constrained message instead; that permission is documented and the resulting information game is evaluated anew. No named game’s complete rules are asserted by this illustration. [E-01, E-04, E-05]

**Illustrative public-information party round.** A person loses an answering seat before the final round. Available choices are voting on already-public alternatives, observing, or taking a break; the design does not label a new repetitive task “meaningful” merely because it consumes input. Re-entry is offered at the announced next round. A participant who preferred the break is counted as exercising agency, not as a retention failure. Another participant who wanted an authoring seat is not presumed satisfied by the vote button. [E-01, E-05, E-07]

**Documented counter-design.** The One Night product summary supplies a no-elimination alternative at the structural level. Since its detailed rulebook was not read here, this pattern does not derive specific vote, reveal or tie procedures from that summary. The transferable question is whether eliminating the waiting interval is simpler than designing a continuing role. [E-03]

All prototype examples are illustrative; no implementation or player test occurred. Official rule examples are documentary observations only.

## Tradeoffs and alternatives

Continuing roles can preserve a connection to the shared outcome while adding explanation, decisions, private-state handling, moderation and testing. They may change alliances or make earlier elimination strategically desirable. A purely observational option can be preferable for a person who enjoys watching, yet inadequate for someone seeking influence. A break can preserve voluntary participation without preserving continuous activity. [E-01, E-05]

Alternatives include shorter independently resolved rounds, a common team outcome without individual elimination, simultaneous finale/reveal, or a deliberately paced spectator interval. None is universally better. Choose by the intended strategic structure and participants’ preferred roles rather than copying a feature from a successful title. A continuing role should not conceal a fundamentally unsuitable session model. [E-03, E-05, E-07]

## Failure cases and do-not-use conditions

| Failure / contrary case | Observable warning | Revision or stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Busywork is presented as restored agency | Inputs increase but participants say the activity is irrelevant or obligatory | Remove the task, offer rest or redesign real influence. [E-05, E-07] |
| Omniscient observer information crosses an ordinary player channel | A former player can assert hidden truth not granted by the rules | Change knowledge/channel permissions or omit that spectator route. [E-04] |
| A legitimate bounded message is wrongly suppressed as a leak | Moderation blocks an explicitly allowed role action | Correct the contract and tests to the actual role/version. [E-02, E-04] |
| A voluntary option is socially coerced | Decliners face public questioning, ridicule or penalty not part of an agreed game choice | Revise the social/interface exit design; do not report consent from continued presence. [E-06] |
| A new role changes the game’s objective unintentionally | Players prefer elimination or a single actor dominates the ending | Treat this as a balance/rules issue, not merely a UI inconvenience. [E-01, E-05] |
| Rules/help cues disclose secret state | An accessibility or reconnection message confirms hidden identities or refreshes limited influence | Stop the affected route; preserve access while removing the unintended state disclosure. [E-04] |
| The activity already ends together or a rest interval is preferred | There is no unwanted wait or unmet contribution desire | Do not add a continuing role to satisfy a pattern checklist. [E-03, E-05] |

## Falsifying proposed playtest

**Not performed.** A later approved comparison should include the declared continuing-role contract, an optional observer/rest condition and a feasible no-elimination or shorter-round alternative. Keep configuration and social context documented. Because variants can change game probabilities and strategy, do not present this automatically as a clean interface-only experiment. [E-03, E-05]

Check rule comprehension and information boundaries first using appropriate authorized staff scenarios; this is not a substitute for participant evaluation. Then gather consented private reports of desired role, actual influence, burden, welcome, freedom to stop and preferred continuation, alongside observed legal contributions and unintended disclosures. Analyze groups and repeated rounds with their dependencies; do not count every message as an independent test of belonging. [E-04, E-07]

Confounds include skill, role desirability, friendship hierarchy, previous elimination experiences, moderator choices, access route, ending duration and knowledge acquired from earlier rounds. A participant may perform the new role well while not wanting it. Reject the broad recommendation when added actions produce no valued contribution, create compulsory waiting, expose unintended secrets or damage the selected strategic experience. A bounded outcome may still justify a narrower pattern for a particular context; no universal participant count, duration or message quota is supplied. [E-05–E-07]
