# Sandbox

**Draft 0.1.0 · GDR-CAND-20261010-G13-GEN-03 · coverage-open.** This entry concerns player-defined projects and the supported space of possible actions. It does not equate sandbox with a large map, limitless mechanics, lack of rules, unrestricted multiplayer conduct or a guarantee of creativity.

## Definition and contested boundaries

For this draft, **sandbox** describes play in which forming, revising and pursuing player-defined goals through a reusable set of affordances is a central promise. Authored missions, constraints and progression can coexist with that promise. The useful design question is not whether the game contains an objective, but whether players can meaningfully redirect activity and combine supported actions toward projects not exhausted by one prescribed sequence. This is a provisional definition, not a formal genre standard. [C-01]

A nonlinear route to the same mandatory endpoint is not by itself equivalent to player-defined projects. An open-world map describes spatial organization rather than goal authority. A level editor may supply a sandbox mode without making its host game wholly sandbox. “Immersive simulation” can emphasize systemic solutions inside authored situations; shared affordances alone do not make the two labels synonyms, and the relevant genre entry was not inspected. Survival, colony and life simulation introduce particular stakes, agents and pressures into a sandbox rather than supplying automatic reasons to remove those constraints. [C-01]

Town-building appearance is also insufficient to classify a game as city management. Townscaper's official description frames it as a building toy with a constrained placement grammar and automatic transformations. That is a useful boundary case for the separately scoped G12 city-builder work, not permission to merge roster entries. [C-03]

## Custom research instructions

1. **Inventory actual afforded transformations.** Identify what can be placed, removed, combined, configured or interpreted and what cannot. Record dependencies, permissions and persistence. A feature list or claim to “do anything” is not an affordance audit. [C-01, C-07]
2. **Distinguish discoverable actions from predictable outcomes.** A small verb set can produce surprising transformations; a large simulation can offer precise local feedback. Inspect when players can find an action, forecast its effect and identify why it failed. Do not infer legibility from simplicity alone. [C-03, C-07]
3. **Examine goal support without commandeering goals.** Compare an empty start, examples, optional project invitations, player-authored bookmarks and directed tutorial tasks. Check whether suggestions become de facto mandatory through rewards or locked tools. [C-08]
4. **Specify the consequence boundary.** Distinguish local undo, world snapshot, isolated experiment, mode switch, restart and content export. Ask what each restores, what it leaves changed and whose work it can affect. “Recoverable” is meaningless without a stated scope. [C-05, C-06, C-09]
5. **Compare constraints as design material.** Resource budgets, physical rules, limited palettes and social permissions can give projects form. Contrast a constrained expressive toy with a wide system, rather than assuming that more verbs, acreage or simulation variables mean greater usable freedom. [C-02–C-04, C-07]
6. **Challenge showcasing bias.** A striking creation demonstrates what somebody could produce under unknown effort and expertise, not what a new player can discover. Inspect mundane starts, failed builds, abandoned goals and users who do not want to publish. No such player sample was obtained in this batch. [C-08, C-11]

Primary routes should include versioned creator documentation, actual observed sessions with declared builds, recovery specifications, player-created artifacts with provenance and original studies that distinguish expressive outcomes from guided task completion. Present research is document-led; the famous examples are neither representative usage data nor causal proof. [C-11]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Candidate promises include composing a place, experimenting with a system, inventing a challenge, rehearsing an idea, maintaining a world or collaborating on a creation. A player might value aesthetic authorship while declining economic optimization; another might deliberately preserve scarcity as the source of a self-imposed challenge. Private experimentation and unfinished work can be legitimate endpoints. These are hypotheses about possible use, not measured audience categories. [C-01, C-08]

Minecraft's introductory guide contrasts Creative and Survival constraints while describing player-directed goals. No Man's Sky's Waypoint documentation describes presets and adjustable economy, survival and combat settings, with restrictions for some modes. These published designs show that goal freedom and constraint configuration can be varied separately; they do not establish that removing costs improves everyone's experience. [C-02, C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-009; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-011]

## Characteristic loops and structures

A proposed core loop is **form or borrow an intention → try a supported transformation → inspect the result → keep, revise or abandon it → form a new intention**. At session scale, returning to an unfinished project can matter more than receiving a fresh task. At longer horizons, a world, collection of designs or changing skill may provide continuity without an authored finale. None of these requires an endless engagement target. [C-07, C-08]

Goal formation still requires structure: available actions, constraints, examples, feedback and persistence determine what can be attempted. Lack of mandatory objectives therefore does not imply absence of authored design. Townscaper's narrow interaction description is a contrary case to any universal demand for deep agent or economic simulation. Its automatic output is also a warning against assuming that a limited interface exposes every rule. [C-03, C-07]

### Mechanism and evidence bridge

| Proposed link | Evidence and reasoning | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Discoverable affordances make an intended action possible | Creator documents specify concrete transformations; possibility is not usability | A command can exist and still be inaccessible or misunderstood |
| Combinable actions support projects beyond an authored example | Original inference from reusable transformations | No inspected measure of expressive range, novelty or player creativity |
| Predictable recovery can lower the cost of trying an alternative | Documentation supports some state-copy operations; a developer account exposes undo failures | Reduced perceived risk and more useful experimentation remain hypotheses |
| Optional examples can help goal formation without replacing it | Original design proposal and reviewed learning distinctions | The example may become a copied answer, create obligation or spoil discovery |
| More successful experimentation improves enjoyment or learning | Not established | Do not infer psychological or educational benefit from action count or task completion |

The strongest recommendation is **to define a learnable action vocabulary and an honest consequence/recovery boundary before expanding world size or simulation breadth**. This is conditional synthesis, not a validated development sequence or a requirement to explain every secret. [C-07–C-09]

## Practical choices and alternatives

| Decision | Conditional choice | Tradeoff and simpler alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Breadth versus composability | Add an action when it changes the kinds of projects users can pursue, not merely the menu count | More combinations increase authoring and verification demands. A few reusable transformations may support a richer local space than many disconnected features. |
| Empty start versus optional examples | Offer examples when people cannot identify a wanted first experiment; make refusal and replacement meaningful | Examples can overdetermine goals. A readable palette and one reversible action may suffice. |
| Resource constraint versus free construction | Keep costs when obtaining and budgeting are part of the self-chosen challenge | Removing costs can enable composition while destroying an earned-world promise. Offer a clearly scoped mode or separate scenario, not a concealed rule change. |
| Local undo versus whole-state branch | Use local undo for actions whose reversal boundary is genuinely local; use a branch when consequences are coupled | Full snapshots are expensive and can overwrite valued history. Preview or paper planning can be preferable to either. |
| Visibility versus mystery | Explain permissions and failed actions; preserve unknown combinations when discovering them is part of play | Full rule disclosure can remove exploration. An unexplained failure should not masquerade as a secret when it is a defect. |
| Solo versus shared creation | Define which participants can alter which state and how disputed changes are handled | One player's undo can erase another's contribution. Multiplayer requires authority and moderation decisions beyond a sandbox label. |

These are original choices within C-07–C-10. Diagnose the intended project and the current obstacle first. Identify the smallest working action set. Describe its irreversible effects and where state persists. Select one optional goal aid or recovery mechanism only when it addresses a demonstrated problem. Plan a comparison in which richer tools do not automatically receive more tutorial time or more attractive content. [C-07, C-08, C-11]

## Representative and negative cases

**Townscaper — narrow tools can still invite self-directed composition.** The official opening describes placing colored blocks on an irregular grid and automatic transformation into building forms. It advertises no imposed goal. That documents an intentionally bounded toy, not unlimited authorship, a fully transparent algorithm or measured relaxation. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-010]

**Factorio — an undo command can under-deliver its apparent promise.** In Friday Facts #412, Wube describes historical blueprint undo omissions and problems remembering which distant action would be reversed. The account motivates inspecting affected state and location. It reports internal testing but provides no participant or effect data; its proposed 2.0 improvements are not a current-build reproduction in this batch. [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-012]

**Minecraft experimental copies — a bounded technical example.** The inspected testing guide describes copying an existing world when enabling experimental features in its Windows Bedrock route, alongside warnings about unstable versions. This supplies an example of a protected original/copy distinction in a testing workflow. It does not establish a general, frictionless gameplay rollback or validate a creativity effect. [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-G13-014]

**Illustrative worked example: the small harbor kit.** This is an invented design exercise. The player can place a wall segment, rotate it and connect a walkway; structural combinations change the silhouette. An optional card suggests enclosing a courtyard, but the tools remain available when that card is dismissed. The player instead makes a narrow observation pier. A comparison should accept that as a self-authored project rather than failure to build the courtyard. [C-07, C-08]

For the first uncertain combination, a preview shows the affected pieces. The player can try it in a separate branch and either retain that branch or return to the original. The branch does not secretly remove structural rules; any altered resource cost is disclosed. A simpler alternative allows local undo for the placement alone, provided connected pieces and settings are truly restored. If neither method can honor the stated boundary, the design should use preview and explicit consequence rather than a misleading “safe experiment” promise. [C-09]

**Illustrative counterexample: the costly expedition settlement.** Here a self-chosen project matters precisely because materials and dangerous travel were required. Mandatory free experimentation in the live world could undermine that promise. An optional non-progressing planning space, or no branch at all, may be more appropriate. This counterexample prevents the recommendation from turning freedom into compulsory consequence removal. [C-04, C-09]

## Pattern selection and adaptations

[Reversible experiment branch](../patterns/reversible-experiment-branch.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G13-PAT-02`, is useful only where there is a valued reference state and an isolatable uncertain change. It differs from switching on free resources or undoing a single command. Carry over the relevant rules, declare non-restored state and do not silently merge a trial into a shared world. [C-09]

[Scoped autonomous changes](../patterns/scoped-autonomous-changes.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G13-PAT-01`, applies when agents or procedural systems alter player-authored work in the background. A static composition toy may not need it. A living-world sandbox may protect selected structures while allowing visitors or environmental processes elsewhere; such protection is a proposed rule, not an observed feature of the examples. [C-12]

For a sandbox/colony hybrid, protect experimentation without making resource implications disappear unintentionally. For a sandbox/life hybrid, distinguish editing a room from editing a relationship history. In a shared world, one person's experimentation permission does not grant authority over another's work or private information. [C-01, C-09, C-10]

## Accessibility, ethics, localization and production implications

World editing may require repeated precise selection, spatial interpretation, sustained input and remembering modes. Proposed mitigations include alternate selection routes, controllable repetition, persistent mode labels and meaningful action summaries, but no universal accessibility effectiveness is claimed. Test combinations rather than assigning one generic preset to a diagnosis. [C-10]

User-created names, long item labels and symbolic systems require separate localization decisions. A visual pattern that is legible to its designer may not be legible to a new participant; where language supplies critical information, support alternative presentation without revealing extra facts. Creative freedom does not authorize appropriation of copyrighted source assets or publication of private player artifacts. Rights checks and sharing permissions remain outside this research authorization. [C-10]

Persistent combinatorial systems bring authoring, storage, compatibility and debugging obligations. A safe branch must cover the relevant simulation state, not only a picture of the construction. Larger state spaces also increase the number of combinations that remain untested; no coverage percentage or performance estimate is asserted. Limit the supported space honestly rather than marketing every technically possible combination as maintained behavior. [C-09, C-10]

## Research questions and validation

**All proposed tests are NOT PERFORMED.** Require separate authorization and a locally justified participant/sample plan. Include users with different construction experience, access routes, languages and desired projects. Do not require public sharing of personal creations. [C-11]

| Question / comparison | Procedure and measures | Confounds and rejection rule |
|---|---|---|
| Do optional examples support goal formation without replacing it? | Compare palette-only, optional project invitation and mandatory tutorial with matched tool access. Ask people to state or change their own intention; observe first meaningful action, departures from the example and perceived obligation. | Visual appeal, tool unlocking and exposure differ easily. Reject an optional aid that becomes a de facto mandatory goal or offers no advantage over a minimal action example. |
| Is the action grammar learned rather than copied? | Use contrasting combinations with different correct outcomes while preserving the same rule. Ask for predictions, then allow a self-chosen creation. | Correct imitation is not transfer. Retain access aids and record prior knowledge; reject claims based on one familiar configuration. |
| Is recovery understood and faithful? | Compare preview/local undo/branch in a synthetic state containing an off-screen dependency and a prior setting. Ask what will be retained before trying recovery. Inspect every declared protected state afterward. | A reassuring label can raise confidence without correct recovery. Any protected-state violation defeats the safety promise; assess usable recovery separately from willingness to experiment. |
| Does simplification preserve useful freedom? | Compare a small composable set with a broader set, holding artwork and introduction effort as constant as feasible. Evaluate self-authored intentions supported and obstructed, not only feature usage. | No universal creativity score is supplied. Prefer narrower scope when breadth adds burden without supporting the intended range; retain a broader system when it enables otherwise impossible valued projects. |

### Coverage checkpoint

Researched: goal authority, affordances, optional structure, discoverability, constrained expression, recovery semantics and hybrid conflicts. Unresolved: direct build observation, modding/programming sandboxes, multiplayer governance, long-term creative practice, educational outcomes, console/touch editing detail, recovery under actual persistence failures and representative novice experience. Next discriminator: does the proposed recovery path restore the stated boundary **and** permit a new self-chosen experiment without teaching a false rule? [C-11]
