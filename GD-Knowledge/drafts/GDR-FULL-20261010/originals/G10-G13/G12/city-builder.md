# City builder

**Draft, coverage open.** This entry helps design settlement-scale building, service and policy decisions. It does not recommend a real-world urban policy or certify an ideal city form. A depicted city is not automatically an economic simulation, and an efficient traffic metric is not automatically public benefit. [C-01, C-06, C-09]

## Definition and boundaries

For this file, **city builder** means an experience in which constructing or transforming a settlement’s spatial organization is a central activity, with consequential relationships among places, services, resources or modeled inhabitants. Some boundary cases foreground expressive construction rather than persistent obligations. Describe that difference instead of deciding membership solely from a top-down camera, buildings or a store tag. [C-01]

[Management](management.md) concerns allocation and coordination; the city-builder specialization adds **where** a decision operates, who can reach its benefits, what networks connect it and what ongoing settlement obligations it creates. [Simulation](simulation.md) supplies the model/interface/fidelity distinction. Colony simulation may emphasize a smaller community or individual agents, but its actual genre file was not available: no size threshold or replacement guidance is invented. A survival-city hybrid adds pressure without making crisis universal. A construction toy can be adjacent while omitting economic and political consequences. [C-01, C-07]

The advertised goals of Terra Nil and Townscaper make useful negative cases: restoration followed by departure, and expressive building without goals, respectively. They challenge automatic assumptions about infinite expansion and compulsory management. They do not prove a single settled definition or what all players prefer. [C-04, C-05]

## Custom research instructions

Start from a **spatial consequence**, not a list of building categories. Identify the demand, access path, service capacity, transport or utility network, funding source, construction delay, operating burden and affected groups. Determine whether a rule is local, district-wide, network-wide or globally pooled. Inspect exceptions such as disconnected but apparently close buildings, idle capacity that cannot reach demand, or expansion that improves one area by degrading another. [C-06, C-08]

For economic research, distinguish growth from replacement and maintenance; resource throughput from reserves; construction affordability from sustainable operation; aggregate prosperity from its distribution. For political research, identify who counts as a constituency, which preferences are authored, what can change through play, and how decisions constrain or empower those groups. Do not treat a single satisfaction score as an empirical welfare model. [C-08, C-09]

Seek original development explanations, model rules, patch-specific changes, inspectable built scenarios and independent player evidence. Deliberately include metropolitan networks, constrained settlements, restoration endpoints and expressive building, while recording which are central and which are boundary cases. The present corpus does not represent all cultural planning traditions, historical city forms or player populations. [C-01–C-05, C-14]

Challenge **one ideal urban form**, **realism as an automatic benefit**, **unlimited growth as necessary success**, **more roads as an automatic remedy**, and **traffic flow as synonymous with livability**. A layout that optimizes one authored cost function does not thereby validate a real planning doctrine. Future direct observations must record map, scenario, platform, build, settings and exact intervention; this batch inspected documents, not game executions. [C-06–C-09]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Possible promises include shaping a place, understanding interdependent services, sustaining a community, negotiating competing interests or bringing a damaged landscape to a chosen endpoint. These are candidate motivations, not audience statistics. A player interested in skyline expression may want solvable or optional upkeep; another may want maintenance to remain the principal challenge after construction. A political-management player may prefer incompatible demands that an optimization-oriented player would consider inefficient. Make the intended conflict explicit before tuning it away. [C-07, C-09]

One game can support more than one promise through scenarios or modes, but their evidence and outcomes should not be pooled indiscriminately. A forgiving construction mode can preserve spatial expression while invalidating a comparison about budget management. An endless mode can serve persistence without becoming the necessary end condition for every settlement game. [C-07, C-13]

## Characteristic loops and structures

An analytical loop is **read a place and its needs → choose a location or policy → commit construction and operating resources → observe spatial and delayed effects → maintain, adapt or retire**. Its distinctive tension lies in interacting scales: a locally useful building may depend on network access, district permissions and a citywide budget. A session can focus on a service problem rather than a growth milestone. A long-term structure can involve renewal, adaptation to changing needs, restoration or an explicit conclusion. [C-06–C-08]

### Networks are not only geometry

The Cities: Skylines II traffic diary describes several path-cost dimensions and separates flow, volume and road-condition views. **Application bridge:** choose information that distinguishes the hypothesized problem before prescribing a layout. A short route may not be the chosen route under a particular model; high traffic volume and poor flow are not interchangeable diagnoses. These documented distinctions do not establish an ideal network or real-world travel behavior. [C-02, C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-G12-008]

### Construction creates obligations

The economy-revision account shows that funding safeguards and service costs are authored choices. **Application bridge:** when construction is supposed to entail stewardship, connect its visible upfront cost to later maintenance, staffing, service reach and replacement. When expression is the goal, those burdens may instead be reduced or omitted explicitly. Do not secretly require perpetual expansion to finance maintenance while calling stable operation a viable objective. Whether stability is feasible depends on the actual model. [C-03, C-08; GDR-SRC-20261010-G12-009]

### Competing constituencies are not one hidden optimum

The Frostpunk 2 production account identifies factions and Council interaction as part of its city-building experience. **Application bridge:** where policy conflict is central, show how a choice affects different authored interests rather than presenting an unexplained aggregate approval score as truth. Interests may change; groups need not be homogeneous; an aggregate score can still be useful if its purpose and limits are clear. This is a design inference, not evidence that a faction model teaches politics or represents actual communities accurately. [C-09, C-10; GDR-SRC-20261010-G12-010]

## Conditional application process

**Identify the settlement promise and endpoint.** Choose whether the task emphasizes construction, service provision, survival, negotiation, restoration or expression. Describe what counts as success when population is stable or falling, and whether continued growth is optional, necessary or deliberately impossible. Do not silently change that contract after players have accumulated obligations. [C-07, C-08]

**Trace one decision across scales.** For a proposed service, identify its location, catchment or access rule, throughput, supporting network, operating cost, construction delay and affected constituencies. Specify what is modeled and what is assumed away. Where the model uses distance or age-based preferences, label those as authored model rules, not universal facts about people. [C-06, C-09]

**Compare alternatives that address different causes.** A route change, policy adjustment, repair, operating-budget change or demand reduction may be an alternative to new construction in the invented system. Include doing nothing when the apparent problem is an expected temporary condition. The comparison should preserve a meaningful choice, not conceal a designer-selected ideal answer behind cosmetic options. [C-06, C-08]

**Make uncertainty and authority legible.** Distinguish delivered service from capacity purchased, demand from visible complaints, and an estimate from a guaranteed outcome. Show whether a service policy operates locally or globally. Forecasts can use categories or ranges when justified; unjustified exactness does not become trustworthy by appearing in an overlay. [C-06, C-11]

**Evaluate maintenance and distribution, not only opening day.** Later scenario tests should include depleted reserves, disconnected districts, changed demand, service outages and no-growth periods. Check who benefits and loses as well as totals. A technically valid simulation can still make the intended alternatives impossible or unintelligible. [C-08, C-09, C-13]

### Illustrative worked example: two districts and one service budget

**Synthetic, not a shipped-game observation or real planning advice.** Two districts need a fictional public service. District East has unused service capacity but a broken connection; District West has a working connection and insufficient operating capacity. The budget can fund a repair, an operating increase or a new building, but not all three. Building nearest the visible queue is not necessarily the best response under these stipulated rules. The player’s preferred distribution remains a separate decision from correctly identifying each constraint. [C-06, C-09]

A focused overlay shows connection state, currently delivered service, committed repair work and the operating obligation of each option. A second view presents effects separately by district. The player can prioritize rapid restoration in East, improve West’s sustained capacity or accept a temporary deficit to preserve a reserve. The interface should not hide tradeoffs in a single unexplained green/red judgment. This is a proposed use of [pending-work reconciliation](../patterns/pending-work-reconciliation.md), not a claim that more overlays are always better. [C-08, C-11]

A delayed test case changes the repair’s completion time so that its benefit arrives after the deadline. The correct feasibility prediction changes, although the player may still prefer it for long-term reasons. After the immediate problem, a stable-population scenario tests whether the remaining network can be maintained. Failure would reveal a mismatch with a stated stewardship promise, not prove that all city builders need a no-growth mode. [C-07, C-13]

**Counterexamples:** for an expressive town-building toy, demand overlays and tax obligations can impose an unwanted game. For a crisis settlement, a comfortable steady state may contradict an intentionally finite challenge. For political play, resolving every conflict with sufficient construction can undermine the intended conflict over values. Retain the declared promise rather than combining all these structures indiscriminately. [C-05, C-07, C-09]

## Pattern selection and adaptations

[Pending-work reconciliation](../patterns/pending-work-reconciliation.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G12-PAT-01`, should distinguish promised infrastructure from usable service, with location and access included. [Bounded delegation contract](../patterns/bounded-delegation-contract.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G12-PAT-02`, should state district scope, allowed actions and exception handling when service management is delegated. Neither pattern is a proven player-benefit mechanism. [C-11]

Hybrid conflict must remain visible: automated road or service choices can remove spatial authorship; an attractive aggregate metric can obscure political disagreement; a survival timer can make thoughtful construction inaccessible; and maintenance obligations can either support stewardship or defeat expressive play. Offer alternatives matched to the intended promise rather than recommending all techniques together. [C-07, C-09, C-12]

## Accessibility, ethics, localization and production

The Frostpunk 2 gamepad account documents a production approach using radials and time-freezing inspection. It demonstrates a described alternative to simply reproducing a pointer interface; it does not prove equivalent access for all players. Proposed evaluation should cover finding a place, comparing options, selecting scope, confirming an action and understanding its consequences with the intended devices and access supports. [C-10, C-12]

Keep **interface accessibility**, **modeled resident access to services** and **real-world accessibility claims** distinct. A game can depict an accessible district while its own interface is inaccessible, or offer a usable interface while reducing residents to misleading averages. Evaluate translated place names, policy descriptions, units, number formats, status words and list/map correspondence together. Do not use icon color or rapid camera motion as the only route to essential state. [C-09, C-12]

Production review should account for interacting service rules, pathfinding/load assumptions, save migration, map/UI synchronization, translation and scenario coverage. A network model’s speed claim in a development diary is not a benchmark for a different production. Restrict detail when it adds unmaintainable obligations without improving the intended decisions; verify the resulting simplification rather than calling it realism. [C-06, C-12]

## Research questions and validation

**Proposed only; no G12 builds, playtests or participants.** Establish project-specific margins and sample requirements before execution. Include participants with varied spatial reasoning, city-builder experience, access needs and cultural expectations; this is a recruitment consideration, not a claim that those groups share preferences. [C-13]

| Comparison | Procedure and measures | Confounds and decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Global totals versus focused spatial/commitment views | Use matched disconnected-capacity, overloaded-service and late-construction cases. Ask for diagnosis, feasibility and preference separately. | Prior map knowledge and visual density matter. Reject universal overlay expansion if players identify fewer useful causes or spend attention on irrelevant data. |
| Growth-focused milestones versus a declared stewardship endpoint | Hold the model’s promised objectives explicit; test maintenance and changing demand over a no-growth period. | Different goals are different experiences, not interchangeable treatments. Reject a claim of viable stewardship if the authored economy requires expansion that the mode forbids. |
| Aggregate approval versus inspectable constituency consequences | Ask players to predict who is affected and explain tradeoffs before and after a policy. | Text length, prior political beliefs and authored group stereotypes can confound results. Reject a welfare/learning claim based only on higher aggregate score or confident explanations. |
| Pointer-first versus purpose-built controller/alternative-input workflow | Test complete building, policy and correction tasks with retained access options. | Device familiarity and menu differences matter. Revise when a route hides required information or creates unrecoverable scope errors, even if average completion is faster. |
