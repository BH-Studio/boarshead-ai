# Tactical RPG

**Research candidate, not released guidance.** The working definition and recommendations below are conditional synthesis. Coverage remains open, especially for build-level verification, no-level examples, nonviolent play and original player-effect evidence. The [review packet](../reviews/full-run-20261010-g07-review-packet.md) owns provenance, unresolved dependencies and queued work; [tactics](tactics.md) owns the broader local-coordination lens.

## Definition and boundaries

For this entry, **tactical RPG** means a game in which local decisions about differentiated characters or units—position, timing, action allocation, interaction and objectives—are coupled to persistent character/party capabilities or identity. The coupling is the useful research boundary: a choice during an encounter can change who remains available, what they can do, whom they support or which situations the party faces later; prior development can change the local alternatives. This is an analytical convention, not a formal genre standard or approved taxonomy revision. [C-01]

Persistent identity need not mean an experience-level number. Equipment access, learned abilities, relationships, obligations or altered bodies could carry it. Nor must all of these exist together. However, merely putting names and portraits on interchangeable pieces does not by itself demonstrate that persistent identity materially changes play. Conversely, players may care about a named piece without a simulation of relationships. Classification requires examining the actual decision structure rather than deciding whose attachment is legitimate. [C-01, C-10]

The overlap with **tactics** is intentional. Tactics can organize a self-contained local problem; a tactical RPG makes the character/party continuity question additionally important. A strategic layer concerns which resources and situations become available across encounters, but that layer alone does not establish RPG identity. A narrative-heavy tactics game can belong to both entries. Platform, single-player/cooperative mode, tone, grid representation and turn structure remain facets, not automatic new genre files. [C-01, C-02]

Neither permanent death nor a branching plot is a definitional requirement. Nintendo's Engage interview distinguishes Casual and Classic and presents attachment as a developer belief, not a measured universal. Tactical Breach Wizards documents level-based ability perks and a largely nonbranching story. It is therefore **not** a valid example of “tactics without levels,” even though it is a useful boundary case. [C-03, C-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-G07-001; GDR-SRC-20261010-G07-008]

## Custom research instructions

The decision question is: **when does persistence make local choices worth carrying forward, and when does it merely conceal the tactical task or make continuation depend on a vanished prerequisite?** Research the following relationships, not a checklist of familiar genre features. These are proposed instructions for future bounded work, not claims about every audience. [C-02, C-10, C-11, C-16]

1. Follow a character through preparation, deployment, consequences and the next relevant scenario. Record which state persists and which resets. Separate an injury's effect on available actions from its effect on narrative identity. Seek actual cases where defeat continues, closes a branch or intentionally ends the campaign.
2. Inspect growth and class rules at both ends of the capability range. Ask whether a level, equipment upgrade or class change introduces a new positional decision, substitutes raw power for one, or merely shifts a hidden numerical gate. Treat “horizontal” and “vertical” as descriptions to verify, not quality labels.
3. Sample deterministic and stochastic resolution; grid and free-space movement; alternating, initiative-based, simultaneous-order and real-time/hybrid execution; fixed casts and replaceable rosters; authored, branching and run-based campaigns. The present evidence does not fill every cell.
4. For a feature claim, capture platform, numbered build when available, scenario/save state, options, reproduction steps or recording timestamp. Until that work exists, keep the claim at the document level used here. For an effect claim, inspect the original study or actual test record; creator intention and successful products cannot substitute.
5. Challenge “permadeath creates attachment,” “grinding fixes difficulty,” “undo destroys stakes,” “every decision needs a branch,” and “no level number means no RPG.” Recruit and distinguish intended player goals rather than treating those propositions as audience facts.
6. Obtain reviewed G06 RPG and relevant progression, economy, combat, simulation and narrative-interface dependencies before generalizing their systems. Do not adopt a running batch's conclusions or mint its candidate IDs from its position in the queue.

## Player promises and varied audiences

Three **candidate promises** are useful to ask about: “my party becomes recognizably mine,” “my development choices change my tactical options,” and “what happens in an encounter matters afterward.” They can conflict. Protecting a favorite companion can dominate efficient mission play; optimizing a disposable force can conflict with a story that assumes durable interpersonal bonds. These are hypotheses for a game's intended audience, not measured expectations of everyone who uses the label. [C-10, C-13]

A continuity-focused player may prefer lasting scars with a viable next chapter. A player selecting terminal-loss rules may prefer the possibility that the campaign ends. A build experimenter may value cheap reconfiguration, while another may value a committed identity. Access preferences do not identify which stakes someone wants: needing selectable inputs or readable forecasts does not imply wanting reversible character death. Ask these questions independently. [C-12–C-15]

## Characteristic loops and structures

The proposed decomposition has three interacting horizons: **encounter** (choose and execute a plan), **party development** (change capabilities and commitments), and **campaign** (choose, unlock or endure the next situation). These are analytical horizons, not prescribed session lengths. A one-evening campaign can contain all three; a long game may repeat one narrow tactical decision despite extensive growth. [C-02]

| Design axis | Conditional tactical-RPG adaptation | Conflict or limiting case |
|---|---|---|
| Deterministic versus stochastic resolution | Separate known costs, hit/outcome uncertainty and information that has not been acquired. Describe which persistent loss can follow each kind. | An exact result preview can remove an intended gamble; a probability display cannot explain an undisclosed immunity. |
| Grid versus free space | For grids, inspect adjacency, height and reach boundaries; for free space, inspect paths, facing, clearance and exposure during execution. | A destination can be reachable yet the route unsafe. A grid is not proof that the rules are comprehensible. |
| Turn, initiative, simultaneous orders or real-time/hybrid | Define when commitment occurs and whether new events can invalidate an order. Connect the execution demand to character capability rather than assuming it is purely intellectual. | A pause may preserve deliberation but remove a selected timing challenge. An initiative change can alter rescue feasibility. |
| Fixed cast versus recruitable roster | Audit the consequences of absence for combat roles, authored scenes, relationships and entry requirements separately. | A replacement can restore a needed action without replacing a person or a unique relationship. |
| Authored, branching, procedural or run-based campaign | Define what is promised to remain reachable after a setback and what is intentionally lost. | Restoring all routes can negate commitment; restoring none can turn an accidental prerequisite gap into a misleading continuation state. |

These are design inferences, not observed effects of the named variants. The inspected cases below demonstrate some available combinations; omitted combinations remain gaps. [C-02, C-12]

### Persistence, meaning and roster traps

The proposed mechanism has four links. A local choice changes persistent state; that state changes later feasible actions; the player understands or can discover the relationship; and the continuing consequence matters to their goal. Documents below support the existence of the first kind of coupling. Feasibility follows only from the particular rules. Understanding and experienced meaning remain untested links. An elaborate injury system can fail the last two links, and a simple fixed cast can still matter to a player. [C-10, C-13]

A **roster trap** here is not merely losing a preferred build or facing a difficult encounter. It is a continuation state that lacks a reachable prerequisite for the kind of continuation the design has promised. Examine unavailable roles, resources, recruitment, geography, deadlines, alliances and scenario entry together. Healing health does not restore a lost social permission; a powerful recruit does not reopen a route whose contact is dead. An explicitly disclosed terminal ending is a different design, not automatically a defect. [C-10]

When continuity is an intended promise, consider the [Consequence-preserving roster recovery](../patterns/consequence-preserving-roster-recovery.md) candidate. Its distinctive proposal is to audit and restore a **route to sufficient capabilities**, not necessarily the original character, equipment, relationship, victory or ending. No source demonstrates the complete recipe's comparative benefit. It should not be the default for terminal-loss play. [C-13]

### Progression versus positional reasoning

Increasing damage can make a poor position survivable without improving the player's model of line of sight, range or turn order. Conversely, opening a new movement option can make an old positional solution obsolete. Therefore assess comprehension and capability separately. Neither a faster victory nor a higher-level party alone measures tactical learning. This is a logical limitation of the measurement, while the proposal that particular growth designs obscure learning is a hypothesis. [C-11]

A reversible design process is to identify the target relation, compare representative states at documented power levels, inspect whether there remain meaningful alternatives, then decide whether the chosen growth promise should preserve that relation. Do not preserve a tactical obstacle merely because it was once important: becoming powerful enough to bypass it may be an intended reward. Do not impose a level cap as a universal answer. [C-11, C-16]

### Classes, builds and role consequences

A class or build choice matters tactically when it changes the feasible actions and combinations, not merely the menu label. Nintendo's interview documents skill inheritance through Emblem bonds and weapon proficiencies opening previously unavailable classes; it also describes a story without multiple branches. This supplies a narrow example of party-development choice without requiring a branching campaign. The reported appeal remains developer interpretation, not demonstrated player benefit. [C-03; source 001]

For a proposed design, trace each important build from acquisition to deployment: what prerequisite unlocks it, which local option it adds or removes, what another party member must now supply, and whether the next scenario still admits a viable plan. Distinguish changing a class, redistributing investments, changing equipment and replacing a person. They can have different costs and narrative consequences. A cheap respec can support experimentation but undermine a chosen commitment; a costly respec can preserve identity but trap someone who misunderstood a rule. Neither conclusion follows from the genre label. [C-10, C-12, C-13]

An especially important counterexample is a transformation intended to make an old obstacle irrelevant. Do not label that successful growth a learning failure. Instead ask whether the next scenario introduces a new coordination problem or intentionally provides relief. Exact class availability, cost, legal party combinations and scenario assumptions remain game-specific evidence requirements; this draft supplies no universal class triangle, party size or balance formula. [C-11, C-16]

### Forecast, commitment and recovery are separate choices

A useful forecast distinguishes action validity, resource cost, known consequences, bounded uncertainty and protected information. A path preview need not disclose the contents of an unobserved room; a risk label need not reveal a hidden story. A preview is also not a promise of success unless its semantics actually say so. Maintain the same entitlement to information across text, icons, audio and optional detail. [C-12, C-14]

Separate cancelling a selection, undoing an uncommitted move, rewinding a revealed outcome, retrying a battle, restoring a campaign checkpoint and respecializing a character. Each restores different state; none makes learned information unknown. An ironman-style promise can constrain deliberate rollback without justifying accidental irreversible activation or an unusable stop/save route. The dependency on shared multiplayer state must be resolved before importing a solo rewind policy. [C-12, C-14]

### Illustrative worked scenario: the absent envoy

A hypothetical party must escort supplies through a floodplain. An envoy can obtain ferry access; an engineer can reopen a bridge; a scout can guide an overland route. In an earlier encounter the envoy withdrew injured. The tactical decision now is not simply “heal the lowest health bar.” Ferry access depends on the envoy's presence, bridge access on tools and engineering, and the overland route on time and navigation. These are invented rules for illustrating the audit. [C-10]

First record the actual continuation promise: reaching the next settlement by some route, not preserving every treaty or rescue. Then check whether bridge tools remain obtainable without ferry access and whether the party can afford the overland delay. If both alternatives depend on the absent envoy, a health-only rest option may be a circular repair. A disclosed delay until recovery, a reachable temporary role substitute, or an explicit changed ending are different fixes with different consequences. Keep the envoy's absence and any missed negotiation in the story rather than silently substituting another character's relationship. [C-10, C-13]

The strongest counterexample is a campaign explicitly about the irreparable cost of failing that escort. Making another route appear may invalidate its central choice. In that case, communicate the terminal branch honestly and repair accidental activation/access problems without rewriting the selected stakes. [C-12, C-13]

## Accessibility and production implications

The proposed access audit covers the entire planning-to-consequence sequence: map navigation, inspection without precision hovering, order editing, confirmation, stat comparisons, probability language and re-entry after interruption. Distinguish a coordinate, a path and a facing. Offer usable text/symbol alternatives for critical color-coded facts and keep the uncertainty unchanged. No pixel size, contrast ratio, animation time or reaction window is prescribed by this genre entry; reviewed P01 numerical guidance remains scoped to its original standards/platform context. [C-14, C-15]

Localize effects, not only labels. “Downed,” “withdrawn,” “dead,” “retired,” “unavailable this mission” and “unavailable this campaign” should not collapse into one ambiguous translated status. Define the reference frame of “behind” or “left,” and test probability, obligation and negation in the target language. These are proposed checks, not claims that a particular translation is deficient. [C-15]

Injury, prosthetics, sacrifice and disposability also carry representation risks. Separate a specific fictional rule from a general claim about disabled people; do not require participants to disclose diagnoses to select an access route. Assess whose losses the fiction recognizes and whether a purchasable or time-consuming remedy pressures continuation. No clinical or legal conclusion is asserted. [C-15]

Production costs include combinations of absent characters, scenario prerequisites, save versions, alternate routes, changed equipment and translated state descriptions. A smaller fixed roster or fewer supported aftermath states may be a more honest scope than a claim that every story survives every loss. Restoration and rollback specifications require system-owner review before implementation; source-feature descriptions do not supply an engine architecture. [C-15]

## Research questions and validation

**PROPOSED — NOT PERFORMED.** No prototype, player test, recruited participant, game execution or telemetry collection occurred. Sampling, practical benefit/harm margins and analysis are decisions for a later approved study, not numerical defaults supplied by this entry. Maintain each participant's accommodations in all comparisons. [C-16]

**Persistence test.** Compare a selected continuity-preserving recovery route with the current setback policy and a simpler explicit retry/ending alternative. Keep encounter rules and information constant where possible; document unavoidable changes in available story or capabilities. Recruit intended continuity-focused and terminal-stakes players separately rather than averaging their goals together. Measure viable next actions, explanation of what persisted, attachment/replacement judgments, repeated solved activity, voluntary continuation and refusal. Reject the claimed advantage if it restores nominal progress but produces an unaffordable or confusing recovery loop, erases valued loss, or fails to reduce the target trap.

**Progression discrimination test.** Separate power from understanding using matched rule-prediction tasks and representative encounter states with documented capabilities. Change surface layout while retaining the underlying relation for a transfer check. Use a distinct task that changes the rule only to test adaptation/discrimination; do not report it as transfer of the original rule. Record correct prediction, actual execution, selected goals and access burden separately. A win produced solely by greater damage cannot establish better positional reasoning.

**Forecast and commitment test.** Compare concise entitled-information previews with on-demand detail and the existing presentation. Include legal-but-risky, illegal, hidden-information and accidental-selection cases. Ask what is known, what is uncertain, what will be spent and what can be undone before action. Falsifiers include false certainty, protected-information leakage, inaccessible order editing and unacceptable planning burden. A slower decision is neither automatically deeper nor automatically worse.
