# Cozy game

**Research candidate · version 0.1.0 · coverage open.** This file helps a designer decide what a promise of comfort actually commits the game to, while retaining chosen mastery, care and expression. It does not certify a game as cozy, accessible, therapeutic or suitable for everyone. No game build, participant session or prototype was run for G20.

## Definition and boundaries

The working usage is an **experience-oriented and market-facing label**, not a mechanically exclusive genre: a game invites some combination of perceived safety, manageable demands, care, personal expression and self-directed pacing. Whether those invitations are realized is a question about particular players, situations and rules. This is an analytical definition for this candidate, not an approved universal classification. A cozy experience can be a whole-game promise, an optional mode or a temporary refuge; those have different obligations. [COZ-C01]

The practitioner vocabulary in Project Horseshoe separates safety, abundance and softness, and explicitly distinguishes cozy from merely cute, domestic or childlike. That is useful question-framing, not a demonstrated recipe or a validated account of human needs. [COZ-C02; GDR-SRC-20261010-G20-001]

Keep **platform, multiplayer, tone, visual style and session model as facets**. A farming loop is neither necessary nor sufficient. A caring narrative can include emotionally difficult subjects; an orderly optimization puzzle can invite relaxation for some players and obligation for others. Do not resolve a disagreement by silently excluding the disagreeing player from the audience. Keep the requested roster entry because this label creates a distinctive design problem: aligning an advertised emotional promise with actual demands. [COZ-C01, COZ-C05, COZ-C06]

## Custom research instructions

Research **the promise–demand mismatch**, not a checklist of comforting assets. Record what can be lost, what time means, who depends on the player, how a task is refused, and whether optional challenge gates valued expression. Separate low motor demand from low reasoning demand, slow accumulation from slow interaction, fictional safety from data/progress safety, and solitary play from relationships with fictional characters. [COZ-C07]

For subsequent work, sample contrasting structures: finite authored experiences, open-ended expression, strategic puzzles, persistent care simulations and community-mediated play. Seek people who reject the cozy label as well as people recruited from cozy communities. Prioritize original audience work, creator explanations with explicit production context, and build-specific observations. Study the timing of discomfort rather than asking only whether someone liked the entire game. Challenge claims that soft visuals establish comfort, that all challenge is incompatible, that care must require daily attendance, or that apparent friendliness removes moderation needs. These are research directions, not completed coverage. [COZ-C07, COZ-C13]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Daneels and Maes surveyed 277 adults recruited through cozy-game communities; ages were 18–67 and 78% identified as female. Their results describe multiple motives, including agency, narrative and performance. This cross-sectional convenience sample does not estimate all cozy players or establish wellbeing effects. A favorite-game concentration and subjective genre labels limit transfer. Personality measures are not grounds for calling an audience morally better. [COZ-C03; GDR-SRC-20261010-G20-002]

Use candidate audience promises as **questions to negotiate**, not demographic prescriptions. A player may want a dependable place to return, another may want uninterrupted arrangement, and another may want an exacting puzzle without permanent loss. The same person may choose different demands on different days. This last variability is a hypothesis to examine locally; the inspected sources do not supply a reliable automatic personalization rule. [COZ-C07]

Ask what counts as a satisfactory stopping point. Completion, deliberate incompletion, aesthetic satisfaction, a solved challenge and time with a character are distinct possible endpoints. Avoid treating a shorter session or a decision not to return as an experience failure without asking what the person intended. [COZ-C08; reviewed F07-TOP-02]

## Characteristic loops and structures

These are **proposed analytical structures**, not a census of shipped games. A small loop may be notice → choose → care/create/solve → see a comprehensible change. A session may arrange a space, complete a bounded request, explore a story segment or improve a layout. A long horizon may accumulate expression, deepen relationships, explore variations or finish a finite work. Each structure should offer a reason to act that survives the removal of arbitrary attendance pressure. That last condition is a proposed design priority requiring local approval. [COZ-C07, COZ-C08]

A return loop deserves its own inspection. A familiar place can still contain an unexplained backlog, expired opportunity or needy task queue. Conversely, a completely frozen world might undermine a desired sense of autonomous life. Compare persistence with no decay, changes that wait for acknowledgement, and genuine world progression with clearly bounded consequences. None is universally cozy. [COZ-C08]

## Mechanisms, practical choices and evidence-to-application bridges

The strongest recommendation is to **make demands and their consequences negotiable before adding more soothing presentation**. Its whole effect on comfort is an unvalidated hypothesis. The following links expose what would have to be true. [COZ-C07]

| Design decision | Proposed mechanism and conditional application | Evidence limit, cost and opposing case |
|---|---|---|
| Perceived safety versus actual loss | Make the consequences of failure, absence and irreversible editing understandable; preserve a recovery route where the promise requires one. | Horseshoe supplies a heuristic vocabulary, not causal validation. Guaranteed recovery can weaken a deliberately valued commitment. |
| Pace versus sluggish operation | Let a person choose when to start a demanding task; keep routine navigation responsive. Consider pausing or postponing independent of animation speed. | Reviewed challenge/access distinctions support separating demands. More settings create authoring and QA costs; a well-chosen fixed mode may be better. |
| Care versus compulsory maintenance | Make help meaningful through its effect or interpretation, rather than making absence automatically destroy a relationship. | This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion about real caregiving. Removing all dependencies can make care feel inconsequential. |
| Mastery versus punitive consequences | Preserve spatial, strategic or expressive depth while separately choosing time pressure, loss and recovery. | A creator-described mode range supplies an example, not proof that every audience benefits from optional challenge. Multiple reward economies may be expensive. |
| Expression versus irreversible mistakes | Compare undo, previews, saved arrangements and inexpensive experiments where investment in a creation is central. | Hypothesized reduction of avoidable regret; automatic reversibility can flatten commitment and multiply save-state complexity. |
| Optional challenge versus indirect compulsion | Inspect what a person must forgo by declining: story, decoration, belonging, access or only a record of that challenge. | A clearly labeled option may remain practically costly to decline. Reward equality is not a universal rule; document and test the actual tradeoff. |

The final decision should name which demand is being changed and which meaningful activity remains. “Make it easier” is insufficient when the barrier is unreadable information, fear of losing an arrangement or unwanted attendance obligations rather than the puzzle itself. [COZ-C07, COZ-C09]

## Pattern selection and hybrid conflicts

**Use conditionally:** `GDR-CAND-20261010-F07-PAT-02`, Legible commitment and safe exit, when a session, event or relationship mechanic imposes a commitment. Adapt its refusal/return checks to the actual stakes; do not add confirmation screens to every reversible decoration action. [COZ-C09]

**Use only when world state changes during absence:** [Away-state reconciliation](../patterns/away-state-reconciliation.md), `GDR-CAND-20261010-G20-PAT-01`. Show decision-relevant changes without converting the return into a missed-opportunity sales pitch. A stable, easily understood scene may need no recap. [COZ-C10]

A **cozy–educational hybrid** can lower embarrassment and timing demands without removing the target reasoning. An answer-revealing assist may be welcome for play while changing what an assessment can claim. A **cozy–idle hybrid** must reconcile automation with freedom to ignore the game; low input frequency does not establish low felt obligation. A **cozy–strategy hybrid** may preserve demanding planning and offer different consequence policies, but an optional mode that withholds desired expression creates a visible tradeoff. Shared-world play also requires consent, ownership and moderation rather than an assumption of universal niceness. [COZ-C10; see [educational game](educational-game.md) and [idle/incremental](idle-incremental.md)]

## Representative cases and negative cases

**Spiritfarer — documentary counterexample.** The 2019 creator announcement explicitly combines a cozy management label with caring for spirits and saying goodbye. It challenges a definition that excludes every sad subject; it does not demonstrate that bereavement content feels safe to a particular person. No trailer or executable build was used as gameplay observation. [COZ-C05; GDR-SRC-20261010-G20-003]

**Dorfromantik — documentary contrast.** The creator presskit describes strategic high-score play alongside creative, hard and custom modes. It is a counterexample to equating a relaxation-oriented pitch with an absence of elective challenge, not a controlled comparison of those modes. [COZ-C06; GDR-SRC-20261010-G20-004]

**Illustrative worked example, not a shipped game or test.** Consider a neighborhood repair workshop. A visitor asks for a restored lantern. In the proposed baseline, the player chooses its shape and finish, experiments with arrangements, and may postpone the delivery without inventory decay. An optional exhibition asks for a constrained arrangement; submitting is separate from decorating the workshop. The intended remaining challenge is expression under chosen constraints, not attendance. [COZ-C11]

Trace a failure: an apparently optional exhibition exclusively unlocks the only finish needed for the player's preferred design. Merely adding a skip button has not made the reward tradeoff disappear. Compare allowing an alternate acquisition route, making the exclusivity explicit, or removing the exhibition entirely. Each changes motivation and content cost. Another failure: a no-deadline request becomes a permanent highlighted backlog. Test a quiet archive and a small active selection rather than assuming reminders are helpful. A deliberate negative case is a player who values scarce materials and dislikes undo; preserve that preference as a different contract instead of declaring it an invalid cozy response. [COZ-C11]

## Accessibility, ethics, localization and production

Treat this section as proposed safeguards and investigation questions. Check that calm scenes remain legible, low-volume cues are not the sole information route, arrangement tasks do not require unadvertised precision, and required settings are reachable before the barrier. Avoid making repeated clicking the access price of a gentle interaction. No numeric text size, sound level, timing window or sensory setting is prescribed here. [COZ-C12; reviewed P01-TOP-03]

Test descriptions of safety, home, food, family and care with the intended languages and contexts rather than translating a presumed universal comfort palette. Do not use familiarity to erase conflicting experiences. Where community content is present, the production plan needs permissions, reporting, ownership, moderation and recovery; a warm art direction is not a substitute. These are risk questions, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice or guarantees of safety. [COZ-C12]

Cost alternatives before promising indefinite novelty: finite completion, reusable creative tools, a smaller authored cast, or optional expansion. Persisted arrangements, alternate challenge rewards, save compatibility, localization of notices and moderation each require maintained behavior, not just initial assets. Price the promise of reliable return as seriously as new content. [COZ-C12; reviewed F07-TOP-02]

## Research questions and validation

**Proposed, not performed.** Compare the workshop variants with people who seek expression, mastery or low obligation, including relevant sensory, motor and reading access needs. Do not recruit by gender as a proxy for those preferences. Vary consequence policy while holding task content and presentation reasonably stable; a second comparison can vary presentation without changing rules. Record the unavoidable confounds. [COZ-C13]

Ask participants to predict absence/failure consequences, undertake a self-chosen task, stop when they choose, and return under an agreed scenario. Measure perceived safety, agency, meaningful challenge, boredom and obligation separately; observe mistaken commitments, recovery attempts, settings access and reasons for stopping. No physiological signal, logged duration or completion rate is treated as a comfort or therapy measure. [COZ-C13]

Before testing, the local owner should define acceptable comprehension and burden, the intended population and what uncertainty would warrant another iteration. Reject the broad recommendation if making demands more negotiable fails to improve the targeted problem, removes the valued activity, or adds access friction. Prefer a simpler fixed contract when options create more confusion than useful control. No universal threshold or default sample size is supplied. [COZ-C13]
