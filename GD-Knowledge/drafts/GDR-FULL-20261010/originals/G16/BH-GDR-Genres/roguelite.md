# Roguelite

**Complete bounded author draft; coverage remains open.** This file examines how repeated attempts interact with retained power, opportunities, narrative and knowledge. It does not prescribe permanent stat growth, characterize the genre as easier, or assert one official definition. No game execution, player test, economy simulation or telemetry analysis occurred in G16.

## Definition and boundaries

For this research, **roguelite is a contested label for run-oriented designs that selectively combine roguelike traditions with other structures**. The main decision focus here is what persists between attempts and why; this is not a claim that every use of the label requires account upgrades. Cellar Door explicitly relates its rogue-LITE vocabulary to persistent upgrades and heirs. Supergiant describes Hades as rogue-like while also describing permanent progression. Treat that difference as evidence of non-uniform usage, not a dispute that this file can settle by choosing a winner. [C-01, C-02]

The companion [roguelike](roguelike.md) entry focuses on systemic exploration, procedural situations and run-bounded planning. This file focuses on the **relationship between one attempt and the larger progression structure**. A game can require both entries. Conversely, a checkpointed action game is not automatically a useful roguelite case simply because some resources survive death. State the recognizable attempt boundary, the variation on replay, and the decisions changed by persistence. These are working research criteria rather than membership requirements. [C-01, C-03]

Separate the game’s public label, a critic’s classification, and the actual inspected mechanics. Avoid using “lite” as a proxy for casual audiences, accessibility, reduced ambition or a smaller skill ceiling. Those are questions requiring their own evidence. A demanding execution game with upgrades and a turn-based game with no numerical power carryover can both require careful analysis of retained opportunities; the mechanics, not the suffix, decide which guidance applies. [C-01, C-03, C-09]

## Custom research instructions

Begin with a **persistence inventory**, not a progression tree. For each retained item, ask whether it changes the next attempt’s numbers, possible actions, available content, starting information, narrative state, challenge settings or merely presentation. Record unlock eligibility, costs, refund/reset behavior and whether rewards are granted on success, failure, voluntary exit or a particular accomplishment. Inspect actual versioned rules before inferring these details from a product page. This draft has only the narrower documentary examples recorded below. [C-03, C-05]

Research four competing explanations for wanting another attempt: improved capability, a new tactical opportunity, a changed story/world, and applying something learned. Also retain “the player simply wants another try” and “the player stops” as legitimate outcomes. Do not collapse them into a single retention metric or assume progress is necessary for replay. Ask whether the structure rewards unwanted repetition more reliably than the activity it supposedly encourages. [C-05–C-08, C-10]

Compare first-time and returning players at declared unlock states, not just account ages. A high-attempt account may have greater power, a broader content pool, more knowledge and survivor-selection bias simultaneously. For an assistance feature, inspect what triggers it and what the player controls. A failure count may include deliberate experimentation, narrative-seeking or technical faults; it is not a health, ability or motivation diagnosis. [C-04, C-05, C-09]

Source priorities for follow-up are actual progression tables and save/mode rules, creator postmortems that report unsuccessful iterations, consent-based longitudinal observation, and independently inspectable economy/pool data. Include designs with opportunity-only progression, no permanent numerical growth, finite narratives, cooperative runs and explicit assistance. Do not fill missing cases with remembered mechanics from familiar games. Original Hades, its sequel, platform ports and different Rogue Legacy releases must remain separate observations. [C-01, C-05, C-11]

## Player promises and varied audiences

Candidate promises include **something useful can survive an unsuccessful attempt**, **a new approach can become possible**, **the larger story can continue**, or **the player can choose a longer path toward an obstacle**. The creator sources demonstrate that such promises are advertised; they do not demonstrate that every player values them or that the promised benefit occurs. Some players may specifically want a clean slate and comparable starts. Others may want visible accumulation, a finite narrative or a manageable return after absence. These differences should be tested rather than assigned to fixed player types. [C-02–C-06, C-15]

An eventual win is not the only possible objective. A player might enjoy assembling a strange build, finishing a narrative thread or accumulating a persistent collection. A design that removes progression to isolate mastery may damage those goals even if it produces a cleaner skill measure. Equally, a player who seeks a self-contained tactical challenge may experience mandatory pre-run accumulation as unrelated work. Neither audience is inherently the more legitimate one. [C-05, C-07, C-15]

## Characteristic loops, mechanisms and alternatives

### The persistence vector

Use the following categories as a design analysis, not an exhaustive scientific taxonomy. A single reward can affect several rows; record those interactions rather than pretending it is purely horizontal or vertical. [C-03]

| Retained change | Intended opportunity | Possible failure / simpler alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical capability | Reduce a specified resource or execution demand in later attempts. | Conceals a misunderstood rule; creates mandatory early farming; moves the target through escalating enemies. Compare a manual demand setting or shorter repeated segment. |
| Options/content pool | Enable new approaches or discoveries. | Adds incompatible or unwanted offers, makes relevant combinations harder to find. Compare curated pools, selectable starts or optional modules. |
| Narrative/world record | Let attempts contribute to relationships or a finite arc. | Requires deliberate failure, repeats stale dialogue, or contradicts reset events. Compare accomplishment-based or optional narrative access. |
| Knowledge records | Support re-entry and application of discovered rules. | Spoils desired discovery or preserves obsolete information. Compare opt-in notes or version-aware summaries. |
| Challenge access | Offer harder or differently constrained future attempts. | Treats unlock tier as a validated skill score or hides desired modes behind unrelated labor. Compare direct selection where appropriate. |
| Ordinary settings/accommodations | Preserve usable interaction across sessions and characters. | Becomes an earned reward or is silently reset. Keep separate from gameplay reward progression. |

Hades provides a documentary example of permanent progression and optional death-conditioned assistance; GDC’s Slay the Spire presentation shows a different kind of unlock, access to additional challenge levels. These are examples of distinct retained changes, not controlled evidence that any particular row improves retention or mastery. [C-03, C-04]

### The evidence-to-application bridge

A permanent numerical increase changes a formal encounter only if it affects a binding constraint. More health may matter for damage tolerance yet do nothing for a missed rule, inaccessible cue or an instantly failing objective. A new option can matter only when offered, understood, usable and relevant to a later situation. A narrative continuation can matter only when reached and valued, not merely written. These are conditional mechanisms; the sources do not validate the complete benefit chains. [C-05–C-09]

Consequently, name the intended barrier before choosing a reward. If the barrier is repeated execution, compare retained power with explicit assistance or practice. If it is a lack of viable approaches, compare new options with better availability of existing ones. If it is re-entry after absence, more currency may be less relevant than an understandable current state. When the barrier has not been established, keep the progression choice reversible in the research specification rather than presenting accumulation as necessary. [C-05–C-09]

### Variation and compensation are different decisions

A run can vary because of starting tools, opponent arrangements, objectives, route offers or combinations of familiar rules. A reward that changes only a number may compensate for setbacks without adding a new decision; that may still be the intended pleasure. Conversely, a broader pool may generate more nominal combinations while reducing the probability of an understandable synergy. Do not evaluate both structures by asking only whether there are more unlocks or more attempts. [C-06, C-07, C-15]

The strongest negative case is a progression system in which repeated early failure is the predictable fastest route to purchasing the capability required by the next section. This can be a deliberate resource-collection game, a pacing error, or a mismatch with the advertised promise. The incentive follows from the local rules; player dissatisfaction or coercion does not follow without evidence. Preserve the possibility that people knowingly enjoy such collection, while testing whether those seeking the declared activity are diverted into unwanted labor. [C-10, C-15]

## Practical application process

1. **Declare the purpose of each retained change.** Record the intended audience goal and the specific later decision or demand affected. “Adds progression” is not a sufficient mechanism. [C-03, C-06–C-08]
2. **Model the attempt boundary.** For defeat, success, abandon, suspend and mode change, specify retained rewards, losses and eligibility. Separate account settings from character data, and claims about state from technical implementation. [C-03, C-09]
3. **Audit incentives and alternatives.** Compare effort/reward for trying the intended challenge, repeating safe content, deliberately failing, and stopping. Treat these as design alternatives, not accusations about players. [C-10, C-12]
4. **Inspect the opportunity pool.** Identify what unlocks replace, dilute or enable; track prerequisites and the state in which an offer becomes useful. A new item with no reachable use is not automatically a meaningful reward. [C-07]
5. **Design a narrow comparison.** Keep content, power, assistance and prior familiarity fixed when isolating one effect; where that is impossible, state the bundled contrast honestly. Include voluntary stopping and disliked unlocks. [C-05, C-12, C-13]
6. **Choose retain, revise, make optional or remove.** Keep a structure because it meets the declared goal with acceptable burden, not because a successful game includes something similar. Do not treat a cleaner research comparison as a mandate to remove an enjoyed reward. [C-05, C-15]

## Pattern selection and hybrid conflicts

[Explicit run-state boundary — GDR-CAND-20261010-G16-PAT-01](../patterns/explicit-run-state-boundary.md) applies when defeat and other transitions treat different state categories differently. Its distinguishing work is the mixed-state transition and its player-facing explanation; the existing safe-exit pattern continues to own general commitment ethics. Use a minimal statement instead of the full pattern when the distinction is already obvious. [C-03, C-09]

For a roguelite/deckbuilder hybrid, permanent card unlocks change the acquisition environment even when the current deck resets. Test whether a new pool provides useful choices or makes a previous build less reachable; do not describe every unlock as strictly beneficial. [Cycle-aware deck acquisition — GDR-CAND-20261010-G16-PAT-02](../patterns/cycle-aware-deck-acquisition.md) concerns the within-attempt composition choice, not the global account economy. [C-07, C-11]

In a persistent-world hybrid, changes remembered by characters may conflict with regenerated physical state. Choose what the fiction acknowledges and budget reachable combinations, rather than allowing arbitrary resets to produce contradictions. In competitive or cooperative play, account advantages can alter comparability or contribution. Possible responses include declared separate categories, shared eligibility, account-independent modes or explicit asymmetry; none is a universal fairness solution. The relevant shared multiplayer/economy reviewed bytes are missing. [C-08, C-11]

## Representative cases and counterexamples

**Rogue Legacy / Rogue Legacy 2:** the inspected creator descriptions connect the label with successors, inheritance and retained growth. This establishes a concrete promise, not its effectiveness or an official definition. **Hades:** the FAQ’s different label and documented progression/assistance challenge a rigid suffix-based boundary. **Slay the Spire’s GDC presentation:** unlocking greater challenge is a counterexample to interpreting all retained progression as lower difficulty. Actual game builds were not inspected. [C-02–C-04]

A **synthetic contrary design** keeps no numerical capability between attempts but offers direct access to alternate starts, a persistent journal and a finite narrative. It can address variety or continuity without making the character stronger. Another synthetic design uses visible accumulated power as its primary reward and deliberately accepts repeated collection. Neither is proved superior; they isolate distinct objectives that a generic “metaprogression improves replayability” claim would hide. [C-03, C-06–C-08, C-15]

### Illustrative worked example: successor rewards without conflation

A fictional expedition grants a retained research token for completing a newly discovered objective. The token can unlock a tool family for future starting loadouts; the chosen tool still replaces an existing slot. A separate optional assistance setting changes a declared damage tolerance. The run’s carried resources reset on defeat; the discovered objective, token balance and access configuration persist. These are invented rules, not a proposed game adoption or observed source mechanics. [C-03, C-09, C-12]

First audit a failed alternative: every death grants a token, and the next obstacle can only be attempted comfortably after buying a required upgrade. Repeated intentional early deaths then become a predictable acquisition route. Whether that is desirable depends on the intended activity, not the label. Compare rewarding a discovered objective, directly offering the assistance, or reducing already-understood traversal; do not automatically solve the problem by making the death reward smaller. [C-06, C-10, C-12]

For the tool unlock, ask what happens to the next run’s choices. If all unlocked tools enter a random offer pool, the new family could crowd out a desired starting option. If the player chooses a family directly, it may preserve agency but reduce surprise. If it only adds higher numbers, the research question is capability, not variety. A local test should separate those conditions rather than making every unlock stronger, more available and better explained at once. [C-05, C-07, C-12]

## Accessibility, ethics, localization and production

Do not make a usable interface contingent on accumulated currency, prior deaths or finding a rare character trait. Keep ordinary accommodation settings stable while explicitly documenting any gameplay-mode consequences. This does not mean every challenge modification must preserve identical scores or competitive categories; it means the distinction must be deliberate and evaluated, not inferred from “lite.” No universal font, timing or contrast values are supplied here. [C-09]

The original Rogue Legacy description uses medical/disability-related traits in its successor framing. That is a prompt for representation review, not proof of harm or permission to repeat stereotypes. Ask affected people about the proposed portrayal, distinguish an access option from an in-fiction impairment, and examine alternatives that vary play without equating disability with an amusing defect. No such consultation occurred in this batch. [C-14]

A multi-run narrative can produce pressure when desired content is reachable only through repeated losses or calendar attendance. This is a design risk hypothesis, not a finding about the inspected games. Evaluate whether people understand the route, can stop, and actually value the intervening play. Localized inheritance, failure and upgrade language should state mechanics neutrally and avoid shaming a chosen assist. [C-08–C-10]

Persistent systems also create production obligations: migration when content changes, clearly identified reward states, handling duplicate grant/retry events, and reachable narrative combinations. Those are conceptual verification needs, not an engine or cloud-save prescription. A small finite set of transparent unlocks may be cheaper to maintain than a broad dynamic progression tree; choose based on the intended benefit and actual authoring capacity. [C-03, C-07, C-08, C-12]

## Research questions and proposed validation

**Not performed.** Every proposed comparison requires separate approval, a justified sample and predeclared local decision criteria. Recruit across prior familiarity, unlock state, access routes and time budgets, including participants who decline another attempt. [C-05, C-12]

| Question / contrast | Procedure and measures | Main confound and disconfirmation |
|---|---|---|
| Does retained power address the intended obstacle? | Compare matched starting accounts with and without the proposed power change, then test a separate same-rule reasoning probe. Measure task failures, interpretation, experience, wanted repetition and agency separately. | Practice, seed and assistance can explain success. Reject the proposed mechanism if only numbers change while the targeted misunderstanding/access barrier remains. This does not reject power as a preferred reward. |
| Do new options create valued approaches? | Compare controlled pools at equivalent resource state; log what was actually offered, legal and selected, plus declined offers and reasons. | Raw pick rate hides availability and experience. Reject “more variety” if added options produce repeated nonfunctional offers without locally valued alternatives. |
| Does cross-run continuity help rather than compel? | Compare a finite narrative/objective route with a repeat-loss-gated route in a noncommercial prototype. Allow stopping without real loss; ask what players believe is required and what they want to do. | Writing quality, novelty and reward attractiveness differ unless controlled. Reject mandatory repetition if it produces unwanted obligation without the intended experience. |
| Can players explain defeat versus suspend? | Use matched scenario cards with both retained and reset states; separate rule prediction from preference and preserve accommodations. | A blanket answer can look correct in one state. Include contrasting cases and a boundary transition; revise the contract if misunderstanding persists. |

The strongest opposing case is **accumulation as the chosen pleasure**. A study that only measures unaided skill transfer could wrongly conclude that a satisfying upgrade loop is defective. Include the declared collection/progression goal, while retaining stopping burden and understanding as separate constraints. Cuerdo’s reset-location study is useful as a warning against simple punishment rankings, not as evidence for or against account upgrades. [C-05, C-12, C-13, C-15]
