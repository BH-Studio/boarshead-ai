## Bounded framing and evidence strategy

The encounter question is whether escalation, alternatives, exits and recovery remain meaningful across supported arrival histories. Competing explanations include encounter count, unresolved pressure, resource availability, task mix and the player’s familiarity. The interface question is how to specify meaningful occurrence, attribution, repetition, expiry and return without dictating runtime architecture. The theme question is how rules, narrative and play can support compatible or conflicting readings without treating creator intent as an answer key.

The evidence route prioritized original development accounts, official documentation and patch reports, original theoretical/critical work, creator descriptions, and a scoped original survey. Contrary cases were sought explicitly: authored alternatives instead of assumed emergence; intentional costly refusal; valid repetition and purposeful unreliable accounts; deliberate contradiction rather than universal alignment; and critique of rules-only meaning analysis. Public originals were re-inspected before transferring their claims. Search snippets and abstracts were leads, not substantive support. The sole mirror-backed chapter is identified below rather than disguised as publisher-verified delivery.

The resulting recommendations have explicit evidence-to-application bridges. None of the complete W04 recipes was validated by an inspected controlled experiment. A primary source can substantiate its own narrow account without establishing a universal recommendation.
