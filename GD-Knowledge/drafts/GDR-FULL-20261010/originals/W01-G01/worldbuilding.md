# Worldbuilding

**Research candidate — not released guidance.** This file is complete for bounded W01 drafting, not a claim of exhaustive coverage or accepted evidence. Human research authorization does not approve this entry’s conclusions, game adoption, implementation, publication or permanent identity. No prototype, game execution, participant recruitment or player test was performed.

## Decision and scope

**Decision:** Which world relationships must be established, which may remain contested or unknown, and how will players obtain enough evidence for the intended experience? Worldbuilding here means authoring and maintaining a setting’s relevant rules, histories, cultures, institutions, economies and spaces. It does not mean producing a complete fictional encyclopedia, prescribing actual economics, asserting historical facts about real communities, or importing a particular game’s canon. [C-01; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

A world can be a room, an organization, a competition circuit, a settlement, an abstract network or a continent. Scale is a production and interpretive choice. W01 proposes depth according to the decisions and pleasures being supported, not land area, lore word count or the apparent realism of the presentation. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

**Coverage limit:** This bounded search found inspectable theory and creator/practitioner accounts, but no controlled comparative study demonstrating that the proposed coherence record improves player experience or production efficiency. The method below is therefore a hypothesis. It is not validated worldbuilding doctrine. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

## Core explanation and mechanisms

### Construction is not reconstruction

Ekman and Taylor distinguish the author’s construction of a world, the audience’s reconstruction and a critic’s interpretation. Their discussion also treats worlds as incomplete and relational. W01 transfers that distinction to design: a richly documented setting can still leave a player unable to explain the one local rule needed for an action. Conversely, a sparse setting can support the intended interpretation without a fully specified history. The transfer is an inference, not their tested game-design result. [C-01; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

**Proposed mechanism:** A bounded relation model identifies what a decision depends on. An evidence route makes some of those dependencies available. Players form a working model and use it to act or interpret. A later outcome can confirm, complicate or contradict that model. Each link needs separate evaluation: author consistency does not prove player comprehension, and comprehension does not prove pleasure. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06] [C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14] [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

### Coherence is not omniscience

Use the following working distinctions in authoring and review. They are proposed record categories, not mandatory player-facing labels. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09]

| Category | What is being recorded | Review question |
|---|---|---|
| Established operational rule | A constraint on which the authored situation currently depends. | Do relevant consequences and exceptions respect its stated scope? |
| Observed evidence | A trace, document, behavior or event available to a character/player. | What does it actually support, and what remains inference? |
| Attributed account | A claim made by a person, group or institution. | Whose perspective is this; what could they know or have reason to conceal? |
| Contested interpretation | Competing explanations of the same available evidence. | Is disagreement intentional and usable, or an accidental authoring mismatch? |
| Deliberately unresolved detail | A question the work does not settle, possibly even for its authors. | Can it remain open without breaking a decision the work promises is informed? |
| Unspecified detail | Something not yet needed or authored. | Is it harmless incompleteness or a dependency that must be settled before use? |

Jayanth’s *80 Days* writing account describes an Artificers’ Guild developed through differing NPC perspectives without one definitive world-bible truth. That is a concrete counterexample to a compulsory omniscient canon model, not proof that ambiguity always works. A record can preserve intentionally incompatible accounts instead of secretly resolving them. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09]

An institution may claim an absolute rule while its members observe exceptions. Those statements are not automatically a world error. The error appears when the work unintentionally treats the claim as reliable operational knowledge, then penalizes the player for using it. A setting with impossible physics can remain coherent if the operative limits are sufficiently established for its intended actions; a realistic-looking setting can be incoherent about its own permissions. [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14]

## Design choices and alternatives

### Coverage decisions by world dimension

This is a **proposed authoring matrix**, not an empirical account of how all societies or environments work. Its purpose is to expose dependencies and omissions before a setting fact carries gameplay or interpretive weight. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-08; hypothesis] [C-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

| Dimension | Characteristic decisions | Useful connections and alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Rules | What is possible, prohibited, costly or uncertain? Is a rule physical, social, customary, narrated or only believed? | Establish exceptions when they matter. A symbolic or dreamlike rule can replace naturalistic explanation; the intended uncertainty must still be deliberate. |
| History | Which earlier events explain present conditions? Who preserves, edits, disputes or forgets the record? | Author traces and consequences rather than only dates. A short shared memory may be enough; an unresolved cause can remain open. |
| Cultures | Which practices, values, languages, identities and disagreements matter to this encounter or place? | Avoid making every member interchangeable. A work may focus on a single relationship without claiming to characterize an entire people. |
| Institutions | Who can recognize membership, authorize action, distribute resources, enforce decisions or hear dissent? | Separate official authority, practical capacity and legitimacy. Informal networks, reciprocal duties or contested jurisdiction are alternatives to a single ruler. |
| Economies | What work, resources, services or obligations sustain the activities shown? Who has access; what changes scarcity? | Model only relevant flows. Gifts, common provision, rationing, status and reciprocity can matter without a universal cash market. Balance and exploit prevention belong to Foundations. |
| Geography | What is adjacent, reachable, sheltered, exposed, connected or seasonally different? Which distances are intentionally compressed? | A route graph or a single room can be sufficient. Do not infer culture mechanically from climate or require real travel times for an abstract map. |
| Internal coherence | Which relations must remain compatible for the intended action or interpretation? | Record allowed contradictions of testimony, scoped exceptions and intentional resets. Coherence does not require exhaustive explanation or universal agreement. |

**Example of a connected question:** A city says its flood gates protect every resident. Who maintains the gates, who can authorize opening them, which neighborhoods have access to evacuation routes, and whose records report past failures? These questions connect institution, resources, geography and history without determining in advance whether the city is benevolent or corrupt. The example is invented; it is not a claim about actual cities. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-08; hypothesis]

### Authoring approaches

**Constraint-first.** Begin with a few relationships that must support action: a crossing closes under stated conditions, a permit grants access, or a shared resource cannot meet simultaneous demands. Derive only the history and institutions needed to make those constraints meaningful. This can bound scope, but risks making every culture or relationship an explanation for mechanics. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06] [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

**Encounter-first.** Begin with a place, person, activity or contrasting accounts that carry the intended meaning. Add connecting rules when scenes need them. This can preserve specific perspectives, but leaves continuity debt when an early evocative detail later becomes operational. Mark that transition instead of silently treating all prose as authoritative fact. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06] [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09]

**Relationship-first.** Define dependencies among groups, routes, resources and memories, then select situations that expose them. This offers a useful alternative to a timeline-first bible, but can become an abstract diagram detached from player experience. Require an evidence route only for the relations that need to be understood. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06] [C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14]

**Discovery or interpretive openness.** Leave parts of the world unsettled, making evidence and interpretation the experience rather than a route to a single hidden answer. Do not use this as an excuse for an accidentally impossible task. Where the player must select a valid action, distinguish uncertainty of meaning from uncertainty about permitted interaction. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09] [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

**Intentional abundance or minimalism.** Extra world detail can be an end in itself; not every invented fact must unlock a task. Equally, a short challenge may need almost no history or institutions. The proposed rule is to declare the benefit sought and the production cost, not require either minimalism or encyclopedic completeness. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

## Application process

1. **State the local use.** Specify whether the player needs prediction, orientation, attachment, interpretation, exploration pleasure or some combination. Name the consequence of misunderstanding. “Rich world” alone is not an evaluable requirement. [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]
2. **Bound the model.** Select the places, actors and relationships needed for a representative situation. Mark excluded or intentionally unspecified regions. Treat newly discovered dependencies as revision requests rather than filling every gap with improvised canon. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]
3. **Classify claims.** Distinguish the world’s operative rule from an official statement and from a person’s interpretation. Record whose knowledge is relevant and which issues remain unresolved. An authoring record may intentionally contain multiple incompatible accounts. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09]
4. **Connect dimensions.** For each consequential assertion, inspect at least the relationships it actually depends on: authority without resources, a route without access, a resource without replenishment, or history without a surviving trace may be an intentional abstraction or an omission. Decide which, and document that choice as proposed until locally approved. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-08; hypothesis] [C-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]
5. **Specify evidence and exceptions.** Identify an appropriate encounter, action, trace or optional record. Test whether exceptional cases invalidate a plan the work otherwise presents as reliable. Do not insert a late exception only to force a preferred outcome. [C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14]
6. **Review perspective and access.** Ask who is absent from the telling, who is reduced to a function and which claims concern recognizable living communities. Arrange relevant cultural and language participation before claiming representational adequacy. Provide translators with contested terminology and the degree of uncertainty intended. [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-12] [C-10; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-15; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14]
7. **Evaluate, prune or deepen.** Use the proposed tests below. Remove documentation that serves no identified need, or deepen a relationship when its absence obstructs the desired experience. Optional richness is itself a possible need; pruning is not compulsory. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06] [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

No data schema, persistence service, simulation algorithm or runtime owner is prescribed. Mechanical feasibility, resource behavior and available actions require Foundations/design-harness agreement; only the player-facing consequences belong in this entry.

## Examples and counterexamples

### Illustrative worked example: the Reed Basin gate dispute

**Invented setting and procedure; not historical research, an observed game or a performed prototype.** Reed Basin contains an elevated workshop, a low storehouse and a footbridge. For this scenario, a gate diverts water between two channels; it cannot make the incoming water disappear. The work’s map deliberately compresses travel between these three places. These are stipulated fictional constraints, not hydrological advice. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

The council’s notice says opening the gate is a council privilege. A maintenance crew says it has long handled emergencies without waiting for a vote. An old monument credits the council for surviving a flood, while a work log credits an unnamed crew. The author records these as institutional claims and surviving evidence. The original political bargain is deliberately unresolved. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09] [C-08; hypothesis]

A player wishing to move supplies needs to understand present access and the consequences of diverting water. They do not need to memorize the entire succession of councils. A short inspection shows which channel leads toward the storehouse; a conversation reveals who currently has the gate key. Neither observation establishes who deserves historical credit. [C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14]

A supporting character can choose to act on customary duty rather than official authorization. That makes institutional legitimacy and material capability distinct without requiring an invisible simulation of an entire government. The author should not label the crew culturally disobedient or the council inherently malicious merely because of their roles. [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-12] [C-08; hypothesis]

**Proposed coherence record:**

| Assertion | Evidence status | Consequential dependency | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening the gate redirects water. | Established local operation. | Route and storehouse exposure must respect the stipulated direction. | Unmodeled engineering details. |
| Only the council may order it opened. | Official institutional claim. | People may comply, contest or refuse; the notice alone does not settle enforcement. | Historical origin and perceived legitimacy. |
| The crew saved the basin before. | Work-log account. | Characters may cite it as a reason for trust. | Identity, motive and full accuracy of the old account. |
| The footbridge is the normal shortcut. | Observable local geography. | Closure changes which shown route is usable. | Exact region-wide travel scale. |

**Counterexample:** Later, the player opens the gate after inspecting the route, and the story floods an unrelated elevated workshop simply because that would produce a dramatic confrontation. Unless an additional relevant mechanism has been established, this is an unmotivated change in the local rule—not valuable historical ambiguity. Revise the consequence or establish the uncertainty before the decision. [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14]

**Stronger counterexample to the method itself:** An installation-like experience presents mutually incompatible spaces and never promises a physically predictable map. Forcing all passages into a single geography could destroy its purpose. The audit should record that intended discontinuity, not “fix” it into realism. [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

### Creator account: participation is contextual, not a universal stamp

The official *Never Alone* page describes Iñupiat collaboration and Iñupiaq narration. This is a concrete production account worth distinguishing from a studio merely borrowing surface imagery. It is not independent proof of accuracy, harm avoidance or approval by every community member. W01 infers the need to identify relevant participants and the scope of their authority rather than certify representation from a credit list. [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-12]

No participant consultation, cultural review or direct game observation occurred in W01. The proposed workflow must not be presented as approval by the people a future setting might depict.

## Failure modes and constraints

**False coherence.** A document may appear consistent only because every dissenting account was overwritten. Preserve contradictions that belong to the world; correct contradictory operational commitments that the design unintentionally asks the player to rely on. The distinction must survive summaries and handoffs. [C-03; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09] [C-05; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

**Cultural compression.** A single practice, ancestry, climate or institution can become a total explanation of everyone’s behavior. The proposed alternative is to locate the particular speaker, interests and context. This is an ethical authoring question, not a quantitative model of cultures. Source-informed consultation should include a way to record disagreement rather than demand one approving spokesperson. [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-12]

**Economy as decorative accounting.** A fictional scarcity may cease to matter because unlimited supplies appear whenever the story needs them. Conversely, fully modeling labor and distribution may waste effort in a setting that never asks about them. Declare the abstraction boundary and maintain only the resource commitments that carry meaning. Actual quantities, balance and exploit controls belong to Foundations. [C-04; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07] [C-08; hypothesis]

**Geographic determinism or accidental scale shifts.** The same route can be a short walk in one scene and an impossible journey in another without an intentional explanation. Review the relation being used rather than chasing cartographic realism. Do not turn a plausible travel constraint into a rule about the character or culture of people living there. [C-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07]

**Access and localization.** Names, honorifics, offices, kinship terms and historical labels may imply different relations across languages. Provide intended meaning, speaker perspective and alternatives; a glossary is not permission to erase contested meaning. An optional journal should report what is known or claimed, not silently promote an unreliable account to fact. [C-10; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-15; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14]

**Production and freshness.** A new action, revised map or removed character can invalidate several setting relationships. The hypothesis is that a bounded dependency record helps locate these changes; that benefit has not been measured. Treat real-world research as a separate task whenever authenticity or factual accuracy is claimed. This file supplies no factual history, law or ethnography for a particular setting. [C-02; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06] [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-12]

## Playtest and validation

**Status: proposed; not performed.** No prototype, world simulation, cultural consultation, recruited participant or observed play session exists from this batch.

**Question:** Does a selected amount of relational evidence support the intended action or interpretation without unnecessary exposition or loss of desired ambiguity? Compare a concise connected presentation with a similarly sized collection of disconnected facts, or compare two different evidence routes. Match length and access effort where possible; otherwise identify those differences as confounds. Do not compare a polished version against an intentionally bad one and attribute the result solely to coherence. [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

Before an outcome, ask participants to explain which rule or account they are using and what they expect. Afterward, ask what changed and why. Include a case whose correct response is uncertainty, so the evaluation does not reward unjustified confidence. For exploration-focused detail, ask about voluntary interest and interpretation rather than requiring task utility. [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

Recruit relevant language, familiarity and access groups. Where the work draws on living communities, research participation and cultural authority are different roles: a playtester does not automatically approve representation. Collect disagreement and discomfort alongside comprehension. Control or report prior genre/literary knowledge, reading burden and assumptions imported from the visual style. [C-06; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-12] [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

**Proposed decision rule:** Revise when a consequential action repeatedly depends on information participants cannot access, or when the work presents an attributed claim as operational certainty without justification. Preserve divergent thematic readings when they are intended and do not conceal a broken interaction promise. Reject or narrow the relation-record method if it adds maintenance without improving the locally chosen outcomes. Quantitative thresholds and sample planning must be specified for a later authorized study; none is asserted here. [C-11; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06]

### C-01 — inference
World construction, audience reconstruction and critical interpretation are different activities; an author’s complete intended model should not be assumed to be the player’s model.
**Support / contradiction:** The source supplies a critical distinction; the player-model application is a transfer from literary scholarship, not an empirical game result.
**Sources / exact locators:** GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07: printed pp. 8–13; Three Kinds of World-Building.
**Applicability:** Interpreted worlds of any representational scale, including sparse settings.
**Confidence:** moderate — supported conceptual distinction; interactive applicability requires context.
**Unresolved:** How much mismatch is desirable ambiguity versus an obstacle to informed action.

### C-02 — hypothesis
For consequential decisions, a bounded record of relationships, evidence status and allowed exceptions is a candidate alternative to an exhaustive world bible.
**Support / contradiction:** Relational/incomplete-world theories motivate the proposal; no inspected study compares this authoring method with a large world bible.
**Sources / exact locators:** GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07: printed pp. 9–13, incompleteness and relations; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06: Embedded Narratives.
**Applicability:** Teams needing continuity while leaving optional or unresolved detail open.
**Confidence:** low — practical synthesis, not measured production or player benefit.
**Unresolved:** Whether the documentation burden is justified for a given team and whether it overconstrains interpretation.

### C-03 — inference
A world fact, a character’s belief, a public institutional claim and a deliberately unresolved question should not automatically be treated as the same kind of truth.
**Support / contradiction:** The distinctions are W01 working categories. Jayanth’s creator account counters the assumption that every setting question needs a definitive author-only answer.
**Sources / exact locators:** GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07: Three Kinds of World-Building and relational interpretation; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-09: Artificers’ Guild account.

**Confidence:** moderate — strong logical distinction and an explicit contrary creator account, not an outcome study.
**Unresolved:** Where action fairness requires a determinate rule despite ambiguity about historical meaning.

### C-04 — hypothesis
Rules, history, culture, institutions, economy and geography should be connected only as far as needed for the intended decisions, interpretation or world-exploration pleasure.
**Support / contradiction:** The proposed coverage questions avoid both isolated trivia and a rule that every detail must serve the plot. No universal optimal depth is established.

**Applicability:** Selecting worldbuilding scope across small and large experiences.
**Confidence:** low — the allocation of detail remains a local aesthetic/production choice.

### C-05 — inference
Internal coherence can be evaluated as compatibility with a setting’s declared rules and evidence status, rather than resemblance to real-world physics, a unified culture or one historical account.
**Support / contradiction:** The source supports relational interpretation; W01 proposes this operational distinction, including the separation of contradiction from incompleteness.
**Sources / exact locators:** GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-07: printed pp. 9–13.

Culture and institution design should leave room for internal disagreement and identifiable participation rather than treating one fictional spokesperson as an entire culture.

**Confidence:** low — ethical authoring proposal requiring contextual review and participation.
**Unresolved:** Which stakeholders are relevant, who has decision authority, and how disagreements will remain visible.

### C-07 — hypothesis
Every important rule or claim used for an action should have an appropriate player evidence route, but not every world detail needs explicit exposition.
**Support / contradiction:** A causal bridge is proposed: accessible evidence may improve usable understanding; it can also remove intended uncertainty. No fixed number of clues or exposition channels is justified.
**Sources / exact locators:** GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-06: Embedded Narratives; GDR-SRC-20261010-W01-14: task-record and narrative-review guidance.
**Applicability:** Actions whose consequences depend on understanding setting constraints.
**Confidence:** low — source-informed hypothesis, not demonstrated player prediction improvement.
**Unresolved:** What evidence is sufficient for the intended audience without converting uncertainty into certainty.

### C-08 — hypothesis
An institution’s narrated authority, material capacity and legitimacy can be modeled separately, and a fictional economy need not be reduced to cash prices.
**Support / contradiction:** Original coverage heuristic: distinguish claims, resources, access, duties and contestation. This is not an empirical political/economic law.

**Confidence:** low — useful analytical proposal, no comparative validation here.
