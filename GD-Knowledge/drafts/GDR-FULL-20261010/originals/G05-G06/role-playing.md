# Role-playing

> **Draft research candidate, not released guidance.** Research authorization does not approve this definition, any causal claim, a game implementation or publication.

## Definition and boundaries

**Working usage, not an approved taxonomy:** role-playing names a family of games in which taking, developing or interpreting a character/party role organizes a substantial part of the decisions and the world's responses. A useful design description says what the player can author, what the character can do, who resolves an attempted action, and what persists. This is a comparison framework, not a necessary-and-sufficient feature test or a claim about everyone who uses the market label. [C-01]

Do not require leveling, combat, a linear story, numerical statistics, a fixed class or a single predefined avatar. Nor does adding an experience bar establish role-playing membership by itself. The inspected cases expose different combinations; they do not supply a universal boundary. A fixed protagonist can still permit interpretation of a role, while a configurable character may permit little consequential authorship. Whether either arrangement fulfils a particular project's promise remains a design question. [C-01; C-07]

| Dimension to record | Distinctions that matter | Misleading shortcut to challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Role authorship | Invented identity, selected background, interpreted protagonist, distributed party authorship | More character-creation fields necessarily mean more expressive play |
| Character change | Changed relationships/commitments, exchanged capabilities, expanded repertoire, increased power | Every change is a level or upgrade |
| Resolution authority | Negotiated fictional action, explicit game rule, authored digital affordance, a combination | A digital interface supports every action a tabletop group could adjudicate |
| Recognition | Immediate acknowledgement, available methods, remembered aftermath, altered opportunity | Only a different ending makes a choice meaningful |
| Development and execution | Deliberation, tactical turns, continuous action, hybrid time | One timing model determines the entire genre |

These are G06 analytic distinctions. They are not prevalence findings or standardized scales. Use the [action RPG](action-rpg.md) entry when continuous execution and character configuration interact; use [dungeon crawler](dungeon-crawler.md) when interdependent spaces and expeditions make preparation and return central. Tactical RPG and soulslike remain separate roster entries, not researched substitutes in this batch. Platform, tone and multiplayer mode are facets. [C-01; C-15]

## Custom research instructions

Start with the promised kind of role-taking, not a checklist copied from a combat RPG. Inspect a character's available actions, a consequential resolution and a later state in the same specified ruleset. Record whether a claim concerns player expression, character competence, adjudicator recognition, material reward or remembered history. A numerical check measures the game's resolution rule; it does not directly measure the player's social ability or attachment. [C-02; C-07]

For tabletop sources, identify the rule text separately from a group's negotiated variation and inspect how authority is distributed. For digital cases, distinguish developer intent from the interaction actually implemented in a named build. Compare an intended action, a permitted method, the resolution and later recognition; do not infer an unimplemented branch from evocative dialogue. Include a nonviolent case, a character-change mechanism that is not merely power accumulation, and a case whose combat is easily misdescribed. Sources 001–005 provide bounded examples of these contrasts, not representative samples. [C-01–C-06]

Challenge four recurring comparisons: a large skill tree against an unrelated narrative game; tabletop improvisation against a digital menu without accounting for adjudication; more outcomes against stronger acknowledgement without matching the player's promise; and irreversible choices against reversible ones without matching information and costs. Future empirical work must disclose participants, task, exposure, uncertainty and what was actually manipulated. Source 015 remains an abstract-only lead and supplies no accepted result here. [C-07–C-10; C-14]

## Player promises and varied audiences

**Candidate promises to test, not universal player expectations:** expressing a stance, becoming capable in a chosen role, understanding a scenario well enough to act, contributing through a party, or seeing relevant actions recognized later. The same person may seek different combinations between sessions. No audience proportions or preference ranking were established by this research. [C-07; C-14]

Define a promise in terms a later test can challenge. “Choices matter” is underspecified. A narrower promise might be: *the town's mediator remembers whether an agreement was purchased, negotiated or imposed, even when all routes restore access*. Another might be: *a specialist can contribute without mastering the same execution demand as a companion*. Both are illustrative hypotheses. Neither promises unlimited narrative freedom or identical rewards for incompatible methods. [C-07; C-08]

There is a legitimate alternative: a deliberately constrained role, fixed premise or tragic commitment. Its quality cannot be judged solely by counting refusals. The test is whether the intended scope of authorship is understood and acceptable, not whether the player can rewrite every premise. In cooperative play, one player's expressive freedom may also constrain another's. Explicitly assign which decisions are individual, shared or already part of the premise. [C-10; C-13]

## Characteristic loops and structures

Analyze a **role-resolution loop**: encounter a situation → form an intention → choose or negotiate a supported action → resolve it → interpret the response → carry relevant change forward. This is a G06 abstraction; not all games expose each stage separately. D&D's inspected rhythm-of-play rules describe scene presentation, player action and resolution in one tabletop tradition. That does not make a Dungeon Master or a die roll a genre requirement. [C-02; C-07]

A development loop can change what the character attempts, how a player interprets a relationship, or which future situations are available. Fate's distinction between equivalent exchanges and additions is particularly useful against the claim that character development always means more power. Its minor-milestone text also ties changes to fictional events; that does not imply all advancement must follow Fate's conventions. [C-03]

A campaign can accumulate consequences across episodes, while a short scenario can concentrate role expression without a long power curve. The relevant question is what persistence contributes. If an unfinished conversation is remembered, identify who remembers it and with what certainty. If a party role changes, determine whether world relationships, equipment and action permissions change with it. Respecializing a capability need not erase a promise made in the fiction. These are proposed design distinctions, not a mandated persistence architecture. [C-07; C-09; C-10]

### Meaningful alternatives versus dominant upgrades

Compare alternatives against **declared objectives and contexts**, rather than against a single generic score. A capability is not meaningfully different merely because its name and effect color differ. Conversely, equivalent material outcomes can differ in role expression, risk, dependencies, effort or social meaning. A build that is worse on every relevant dimension at the same cost is dominated *under those specified dimensions*; changing goals, access conditions or encounter distribution can change that judgment. This is conditional reasoning, not proof that every upgrade should be removed. [C-08]

Straight upgrades can serve a clear power-growth promise. Do not fabricate tradeoffs simply to make every decision look strategic. Where the aim is specialization, record the situation in which each alternative is useful and whether the player can actually reach that situation. A theoretically valuable specialist who never encounters their enabling circumstance is not evidence that the player chose badly. Distinguish eligibility, availability, comprehension, preference and performance before tuning rewards or statistics. [C-08; C-11]

### Reversibility versus commitment

Separate correcting an accidental input, exploring a capability, changing an established role, and revising a past outcome. These are not one respec setting. Early reversible trials can support a proposed learning goal; informed lasting commitments can support a chosen identity or campaign premise. Either can fail: a free reset may turn every encounter into compulsory reconfiguration, while a costly reset may punish information the player could not reasonably obtain. No universal price, refund interval or mandatory free-reset policy is established. [C-09]

The conditional recommendation is to state *what* may change, *when*, *at what cost*, and *what does not change*. A promise to refund skills should not imply permission to undo another character's consent or a completed narrative act. The optional [Dependency-complete loadout switch](../patterns/dependency-complete-loadout-switch.md) addresses the narrower problem of interdependent configurations, not the moral authority to rewrite history. [C-09; C-10]

## Pattern selection and adaptations

Use the inspected `GDR-CAND-20261010-W02-PAT-02`, **Multi-route objective contract**, when a scenario deliberately supports different methods. Preserve its reviewed distinction between achieving a world result and satisfying a method-sensitive contract. It does not require all routes to exist, to pay equally or to be morally interchangeable. The G06 illustrative example below adapts that distinction without reissuing the W02 recipe. [C-10; C-11]

Use `GDR-CAND-20261010-F01-PAT-01`, **Supported attempt and transfer check**, for a defined capability relation. Its corrected probes change the correct answer, distinguish permission from preference and preserve access supports. In an RPG, a player choosing an expensive expressive option after correctly predicting the cost has not necessarily failed a lesson. [C-08; C-11; C-14]

The new G06 loadout pattern applies only when interdependent configurations can be changed under the game's declared policy. Omit it for independent cosmetic selections or a fixed-character scenario where such switching is not offered. For hybrid role-playing/action/crawler designs, balance capability freedom against expedition preparation and remembered role obligations; no label mechanically wins. [C-09; C-15]

### Illustrative worked scenario: the contested crossing

A fictional party must restore a public crossing. The ferryman will accept either a repaired cable or a temporary ferry arrangement, but a separate civic oath prohibits diverting water from a downstream settlement. One character is a capable negotiator, another knows the machinery, and a third has a relationship with the settlement. These are invented roles and state conditions, not an implementation proposal. [C-07; C-10]

First define the actual objective: usable crossing, preservation of downstream access, or merely satisfying the ferryman? The player can ask about the constraint without accepting the oath. Each offered route states its known costs; hidden motives remain uncertain. Repair uses a component, negotiation creates a later obligation, and unauthorized diversion restores crossing access while breaching the stated covenant. The world outcome, actor response and reward may therefore disagree coherently. [C-10]

Next test the role contribution. Let the mechanically skilled character inspect the fault through an accessible interaction; do not require the player to solve an unrelated timing challenge solely to exercise that role. Let a relationship change the available negotiation, but do not pretend a higher statistic reproduces every possible persuasive argument. An alternate descriptive response route can test intention without demanding performed eloquence. These are hypotheses to evaluate, not proven accessibility equivalences. [C-07; C-12]

Finally compare consequences. The repair and negotiation routes can reach the same destination while preserving different debts or affiliations. A deliberately linear version may instead ask the player only how the protagonist treats the ferryman. That is a valid counterproposal if the narrower authorship promise is explicit. Reject the multi-route expansion when it adds unmaintainable states without an intended, testable difference. [C-10; C-13]

## Accessibility and production implications

Character competence and player access are different layers. A player may understand a negotiation while reading slowly, need a nonverbal response route, or prefer planning without sustained input. Test the action and information routes, not a stereotype about which build such players should choose. Do not gate the only usable control configuration behind weaker equipment or stigmatizing role text. These are proposed safeguards, not a claim of achieved inclusion. [C-12]

Localization review should inspect commitment verbs, register, pronouns, role descriptions and uncertainty: a translated inquiry must not become a threat or promise by accident. Preserve the difference between what a character believes and what the library or game asserts. Do not use unfamiliar accents, cultures or disabilities as automatic evidence of dishonesty or competence. These are ethical/production hypotheses requiring appropriate human review, not findings about a particular culture. [C-12; C-13]

Bound the recognition promise to what can be authored and tested. Track the consequence that matters, the circumstances in which it is acknowledged, and interactions with party membership or an interrupted session. More branches increase the set of states needing consideration by definition, but no cost multiplier or universal maximum is known here. A smaller expressive scene with honest scope may be preferable to poorly maintained apparent freedom. No runtime schema, canon decision, voice-production contract or engine change is authorized. [C-10; C-13]

## Research questions and validation

**All proposed; none performed.** Obtain separate approval for recruitment, data collection and exposure. Use relevant languages, role-playing familiarity, party arrangements and access routes; do not require an inaccessible baseline. Determine sample and stopping criteria from the question and uncertainty, not a library-wide default. [C-14]

| Question / comparison | Observations and confounds | Disconfirming disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Does a role-specific response fulfil the promised scope of authorship? Compare matched recognition with/without the relevant remembered consequence. | Before/after predictions, action chosen, perceived recognition and unwanted commitment; control wording, outcome desirability and prior genre assumptions. | Reject the claimed benefit if recognition is unnoticed or creates expectations of unavailable control; narrow the promise. |
| Are alternatives understood or merely different labels? Present changed contexts where different methods are permitted or useful. | Separate correct rule prediction from preferred role expression; track access and comprehension support. | Revise when one answer strategy succeeds in all probes without understanding, or one option is dominated under every declared objective. |
| Does reversible practice help informed commitment? Compare reversible practice with equally informative direct explanation. | Retained understanding, unwanted commitments and later chosen stability; confounds include extra exposure and time. | Prefer the simpler explanation if practice adds burden without a worthwhile learning distinction. |
| Can a returning party resume without false history? Compare factual recap with no recap where safe. | Distinguish remembered facts, inferred motives and chosen intent; record spoiler complaints and coordination cost. | Remove or revise the aid if it selects the interpretation or conceals a legitimate uncertainty. |

Coverage completed here is bounded treatment of role enactment, character/party change, consequential choice, statistics, scenario reactions and tabletop/digital contrasts. Unresolved: representative player evidence; full nonviolent-game rules; culturally diverse longitudinal role enactment; empirical comparison of commitment policies; final-build scenario observations; and pending shared-topic integrations. Next discriminating evidence should establish what players were promised and understood before comparing global enjoyment ratings. [C-14; C-15]
