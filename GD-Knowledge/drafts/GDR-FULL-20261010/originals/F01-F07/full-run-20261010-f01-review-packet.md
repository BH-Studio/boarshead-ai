### Strongest recommendation challenged

The strongest practical proposal is to diagnose a specific learning need, use a supported attempt and a changed-case check, then reduce only unnecessary instructional prompting while retaining access and a route to help.

**Strong opposing case:** this can become a burdensome instructional apparatus that spoils discovery, mistakes compliance for understanding, treats a changed task as a validated measurement instrument and silently classifies access needs as inexperience. Relevant supporting studies concern different outcomes and contexts. The game-tutorial paper measures engagement rather than learning; the retrieval experiment concerns vocabulary; expertise reversal is a framework here, not an independently rechecked experiment set; XAG is guidance, not an efficacy trial. These do not jointly prove the full recipe.

**Response retained in the candidates:** whole-pattern confidence is low; the causal links have separate support/limits; a player-selected help route is an alternative to adaptation; direct instruction and no solution tutorial remain alternatives; a single success is not durable mastery; feedback-mediated misconception repair and game re-entry benefits are explicit hypotheses; accommodation removal is outside the recipe; the technique exits when the secret itself is the experience.

**Falsifying result:** the simpler explanation/reference route produces comparable later rule use with less interruption, or the pattern improves only prompted completion, reduces desired discovery, or creates access loss.
