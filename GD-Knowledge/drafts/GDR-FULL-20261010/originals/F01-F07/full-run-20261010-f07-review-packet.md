## Research framing and source strategy

Two questions governed the search: **(1)** What evidence can distinguish a genuine balance problem from measurement, comprehension, access or sampling problems? **(2)** How can incentive, representation and production choices be evaluated without equating engagement with wellbeing or a checklist with ethics?

The search followed public primary routes: original methodological papers; author/publisher research articles and corrections; firsthand developer presentations and postmortems; original survey reports; government ethical principles; and official technical documentation. Contrary routes included limitations of familiar playtests, denominator/early-stopping pitfalls, null versus beneficial wellbeing findings, uncertainty of perceived monetization mechanisms, voluntary challenge, and the external costs of unrestricted exit. Search results and inaccessible pages were leads, not support for unread details.

This was a bounded purposive search, **not a systematic review or exhaustive search through October 2026**. Source dates range from foundational historical material to a documentation update in March 2026. Older sources were used for their inspected content, not presumed to describe current product policies. No formal legal, clinical, lifecycle-carbon or validated cross-language measurement review was attempted.
