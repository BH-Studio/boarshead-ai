# Proposed research taxonomy

Status: proposed for Doug's approval. This is a coverage map, not a claim that all subjects have been researched or that this partition is definitive. Listed topics are backlog candidates, not published entries or reserved IDs. Split or merge after the pilot when boundaries become clearer.

## Foundations and systems

Owner: BH-GDR-Foundations-and-Systems.

1. Player experience and motivation: intended experience, needs, agency, mastery, curiosity, social play, interpretation and cultural context.
2. Core loops and interaction structure: verbs, rules, goals, feedback loops, short/session/long horizons and systemic emergence.
3. Decision design: information, uncertainty, risk and reward, meaningful choice, commitment, reversibility and opportunity cost.
4. Learning and onboarding: discovery, tutorials, scaffolding, mental models, transfer, feedback and re-entry after absence.
5. Challenge and difficulty: skill demands, difficulty adjustment, failure, recovery, fairness, frustration and assistance.
6. Progression and rewards: unlocks, mastery, character growth, pacing, intrinsic/extrinsic incentives and reward fatigue.
7. Economies and resources: sources/sinks, scarcity, currencies, crafting, trading, inflation and incentive exploits.
8. Combat and conflict systems: offense/defense, timing, positioning, readability, encounters, enemy roles and nonviolent alternatives.
9. Movement and traversal: locomotion, navigation abilities, momentum, vehicles, precision and traversal costs.
10. Simulation and systemic behavior: rule interactions, ecology, physical simulation, agent behavior, predictability and emergence.
11. Multiplayer and social systems: cooperation, competition, asymmetry, matchmaking experience, communication, moderation and griefing resistance.
12. Session structure and retention: saves, checkpoints, interruption, re-entry, session goals, repeat play and respectful engagement.
13. Procedural and authored content: generative constraints, validation, variation, repetition, controllability and authoring cost.
14. Evaluation and balancing: prototypes, playtests, telemetry, qualitative methods, experiment design and interpretation limits.
15. Ethical and sustainable design: coercive incentives, monetization experience, player wellbeing, representation and production tradeoffs.

## Worlds and characters

Owner: BH-GDR-Worlds-and-Characters.

1. Narrative structures: linear, branching, systemic and emergent narrative; agency and consequence.
2. Worldbuilding: rules, history, cultures, institutions, economies, geography and internal coherence.
3. Character design: goals, contradictions, capabilities, relationships, change and player interpretation.
4. Dialogue and voice: conversational structure, choices, subtext, barks, performance and localization.
5. Quests and objectives: motivations, dependencies, discovery, alternate solutions, tracking and resolution.
6. Environmental storytelling: spatial evidence, props, traces, inference, unreliable evidence and pacing.
7. Lore delivery and continuity: discoverability, optional depth, codex design, approved canon and contradiction management.
8. Level and spatial design: topology, sightlines, landmarks, paths, gating, flow and spatial literacy.
9. Encounter and scenario composition: escalation, contrast, resource pressure, alternatives, exits and recovery.
10. World structure and exploration: hubs, regions, open worlds, density, discovery and backtracking.
11. Narrative systems interfaces: conceptual states, reactions, persistence needs and authoring/debug requirements.
12. Theme and meaning: mechanics/narrative alignment, tone, symbolism, representation and interpretive openness.

Systems mechanics belong to Foundations; their spatial and narrative orchestration belongs here. Runtime architecture remains the harness/design system's responsibility.

## Presentation and interaction

Owner: BH-GDR-Presentation-and-Interaction.

1. Audio design: ambience, cues, sonic identity, spatial audio, mixing, occlusion, dynamic range and audio accessibility.
2. Music design: adaptive structure, motifs, transitions, rhythm, silence and emotional interpretation.
3. Visual communication: hierarchy, contrast, affordances, silhouettes, readability and aesthetic coherence.
4. Art direction: style systems, material language, environment/character cohesion and production constraints.
5. Animation and performance: anticipation, timing, posing, transitions, responsiveness and expressive behavior.
6. Camera and framing: control, composition, visibility, occlusion, motion, comfort and cinematic transitions.
7. Controls and input: mapping, remapping, responsiveness, buffering, device parity and alternative inputs.
8. Interface and information: HUDs, menus, inventories, maps, notifications, settings and discoverability.
9. Feedback and game feel: audiovisual/haptic response, impact, state clarity, latency and sensory load.
10. Accessibility and inclusive interaction: sensory, motor and cognitive access, assist options and representative evaluation.
11. Multimodal communication: redundant cues, subtitles/captions, haptics and competing attention demands.
12. Localization and presentation: language expansion, reading direction, cultural interpretation and voice constraints.
13. Platform and display context: small screens, couch play, VR/AR, touch, controllers and environmental noise.

Accessibility, ethics and localization also apply across all domains; owning a reference topic does not remove other domains' review obligations.

## Genre library

Owner: BH-GDR-Genres. One eventual Markdown file per genre, under `genres/`, with an explicit scope and custom guidance. Candidate roster:

- Action adventure; platformer; precision platformer; metroidvania; shooter; tactical shooter; fighting; beat-em-up.
- Role-playing; action RPG; tactical RPG; dungeon crawler; soulslike.
- Strategy; real-time strategy; turn-based strategy; 4X; tactics; tower defense; auto-battler.
- Puzzle; adventure; point-and-click adventure; interactive fiction; visual novel; detective and mystery.
- Simulation; management; city builder; colony simulation; life simulation; immersive simulation.
- Survival; survival crafting; survival horror; psychological horror; stealth.
- Roguelike; roguelite; deckbuilder; collectible card game; board-game adaptation.
- Racing; sports; rhythm; party; social deduction; sandbox; cozy game; educational game.
- MMO; MOBA; battle royale; extraction game; idle/incremental.

These labels mix mechanics, audience promises and market vocabulary. Do not pretend they are mutually exclusive or equally precise. Each genre file must define its usage and contested boundaries. Tags such as single-player, cooperative, PvP, open-world, VR, mobile, narrative-led and live-service are orthogonal facets; create separate genre entries only when justified by useful, distinct design guidance. A parent genre and subgenre may each have a file if the latter contains meaningful conditional differences rather than duplicate summaries.

## Cross-domain facets

Index by player goal, experience (tension, wonder, comfort, mastery), problem, mechanic, modality, platform, input, audience/access need, session model, production constraint and evidence strength. Facets are tags, not a second competing folder hierarchy.

## Coordination and initial sequencing

BH-GDR-Library-Coordination owns IDs, vocabulary, conflicts, source registry, review, releases and retrieval checks. It does not replace domain expertise.

Proposed sequence: approve this map → audio pilot → revise templates and retrieval → research a small, approved batch across domains → expand from demonstrated design needs. No automatic commitment to research every listed subject. Ask Doug to approve priority batches and any material scope expansion.
