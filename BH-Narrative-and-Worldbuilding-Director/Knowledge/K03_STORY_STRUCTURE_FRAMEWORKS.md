# Supporting Story-Structure Frameworks

## Framework selection rule
Hero's Journey remains the primary transformation lens. Use these frameworks to answer **different diagnostic questions**. Do not stack them all until the story "passes."

## 1. Three-Act Structure — macro movement
### Best question
Does the story have a clear setup/commitment, escalating confrontation, and climax/consequence?

### Useful game translation
**Act I — Establish and Commit**
- player fantasy and baseline;
- inciting pressure;
- stakes;
- first irreversible commitment/threshold.

**Act II — Learn, Escalate, Reinterpret**
- tests and consequences;
- new relationships/factions;
- reversals;
- midpoint change in meaning or strategy;
- costs accumulate;
- old approach becomes untenable.

**Act III — Resolve and Integrate**
- final strategy/value choice;
- climax;
- relationship/world consequences;
- transformed end state.

### Game caution
Open-world content may occupy Act II in variable order. Treat acts as changing **dramatic conditions**, not mandatory linear mission counts.

## 2. Dan Harmon's Story Circle — compact arc engine
Eight-step shorthand:
1. character in comfort;
2. wants/needs something;
3. enters unfamiliar situation;
4. adapts/searches;
5. gets or reaches what they sought;
6. pays a price;
7. returns/reorients;
8. changes.

### Best use
- individual quests;
- companion missions;
- episodes/chapters;
- short storylines;
- repeatable narrative units where full Hero's Journey terminology is too heavy.

### Diagnostic strength
The "get what you wanted → pay a price" pair is particularly useful for preventing flat success arcs.

### Source
Dan Harmon's archived "Story Structure 101" material via Channel 101 Wiki: https://channel101.fandom.com/wiki/Story_Structure_101:_Super_Basic_Shit

## 3. Save the Cat Beat Sheet — pacing pressure diagnostic
Common 15-beat vocabulary:
Opening Image; Theme Stated; Setup; Catalyst; Debate; Break into Two; B Story; Fun and Games; Midpoint; Bad Guys Close In; All Is Lost; Dark Night of the Soul; Break into Three; Finale; Final Image.

### Best use
- diagnosing a linear main campaign or cinematic arc;
- checking whether middle escalation has shape;
- comparing opening/end transformation imagery;
- checking whether the midpoint changes pressure;
- ensuring a low point and renewed strategy are distinct.

### Game caution
Do not force page-percentage targets onto playtime. Player pacing, optional content, failure/retry, exploration, and systemic play destroy screenplay timing assumptions.

### Source
Save the Cat official Beat Sheets and Beat Mapper: https://savethecat.com/beat-sheets

---

## 4. Pixar Story Spine — causal compression
Useful pattern:
- normal state;
- disruption;
- because of that…;
- because of that…;
- until finally…;
- changed end state / thematic implication.

### Best use
Use it early. If a complex game story cannot be compressed into causal beats, the macro story may be a sequence of incidents rather than motivated consequence.

Pixar's storytelling curriculum also emphasizes character internal/external features, wants vs needs, obstacles, arc, and stakes.

### Source
Pixar in a Box / Khan Academy Storytelling curriculum and Story Spine: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling

---

## 5. Dramatica Throughlines — advanced thematic coherence
Dramatica models a complete story through four perspectives:
- **Objective Story** — external conflict affecting the group/world;
- **Main Character** — personal lived perspective;
- **Influence Character** — challenging alternative perspective;
- **Relationship Story** — conflict/change in the relationship between the central perspectives.

### Best use
Use when a story has plenty of plot but feels thematically or perspectivally incoherent, especially when the protagonist's internal problem and the external conflict feel disconnected.

### Game caution
Dramatica is sophisticated and terminology-heavy. Do not make it a mandatory design dependency. Use the throughline concept selectively.

### Source
Dramatica Narrative Platform introduction: https://platform.dramatica.com/docs/learn/

---

## 6. Freytag / tension-curve thinking — local dramatic shape
Use rising tension → peak/climax → consequence/falling action as a rough emotional-pressure map for linear scenes, missions, or chapters.
Do not confuse tension with action volume; dialogue, revelation, dread, intimacy, and moral choice can all increase dramatic pressure.

# Quick selection matrix
| Need | Preferred lens |
|---|---|
| Whole-game transformation | Hero's Journey |
| Macro campaign pacing | Three-Act |
| Quest/episode/companion mission | Story Circle |
| Beat-pressure diagnosis | Save the Cat |
| Premise/causality simplification | Pixar Story Spine |
| Theme/perspective coherence | Dramatica Throughlines |
| Local tension shape | Freytag/tension curve |

# Structural cross-check
When a story feels weak, ask which problem exists before choosing a framework:
- no transformation → Hero's Journey;
- middle has no escalation → Three-Act / Save the Cat;
- quest feels consequence-free → Story Circle;
- story is a list of events → Story Spine;
- theme and protagonist feel disconnected → Dramatica;
- scene/mission pressure is flat → tension curve.
