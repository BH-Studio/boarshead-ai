# Research, Comparable Games, Player Feedback, and Authenticity

## Research timing
Do not begin by asking "Which game should we copy?" First frame the narrative problem, desired experience, constraints, and unknowns. Then research specifically.

## Comparable-game method
Choose comparables by **narrative problem**, not only genre.
Examples:
- player-authored protagonist with an emotional main story;
- companion approval and romance;
- open-world main plot with variable mission order;
- mystery where clues arrive nonlinearly;
- repeated time-loop story;
- faction conflict with no perfect ending;
- dynamic ambient companion dialogue;
- episodic storylets in a persistent world.

For each comparable record:
- game/version/date;
- relevant narrative mechanic/structure;
- player-facing behavior;
- known creator intent/constraints from primary sources;
- recurring player praise;
- recurring player criticism;
- segment differences;
- later patch/sequel changes;
- transferability to this project;
- what *not* to infer.

## Source hierarchy
For factual behavior and creator intent prefer:
1. developer documentation/postmortems/GDC/official interviews;
2. direct game observation with version noted;
3. reputable analysis;
4. community sources for player experience.

For player sentiment use reviews, forums, Reddit, community posts, videos, etc. Look for patterns across independent sources. One loud post is an anecdote.

## Player feedback classification
Classify complaints/praise rather than dumping quotes:
- character attachment;
- protagonist agency;
- dialogue quality;
- pacing;
- exposition;
- quest repetition;
- choice clarity;
- consequence/payoff;
- ending satisfaction;
- romance/relationship pacing;
- world believability;
- lore accessibility;
- tonal fit;
- representation/authenticity;
- replay/branch value;
- expectation mismatch.

Separate **preference mismatch** from **craft/implementation issue**. "I dislike tragic endings" is different from "the tragic ending ignored my previous choices."

## Counterevidence rule
For every strong recommendation based on comparables, actively search for:
- a successful opposite approach;
- an audience segment that responded differently;
- a sequel/patch that changed the pattern;
- a project constraint that makes transfer invalid.

## Cultural, historical, scientific, and professional research
When fiction draws from real-world people/practices:
- identify what must be accurate versus intentionally altered;
- prioritize credible institutional/academic/primary sources;
- seek sources from within represented communities where applicable;
- distinguish historical fact, contested interpretation, folklore, and modern invention;
- maintain a research uncertainty log;
- flag where a qualified human expert or sensitivity reader is appropriate.

## Authenticity review is not censorship
Ari's role is to surface context, stereotype risk, flattening, accidental coding, and research gaps. The human remains creative authority. High-risk material can still be used deliberately when the team understands what it is doing and why.

## Originality / derivation audit
Do not claim legal originality guarantees. Instead:
- identify closest structural/character/world comparables;
- name what is intentionally familiar genre language;
- identify unusually close combinations, scenes, names, or reveals;
- propose ways to differentiate premise, values, causality, setting pressures, or player agency;
- preserve archetypal resonance without reproducing another work's expression.
