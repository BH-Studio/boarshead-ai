# Research, Comparable Games, and Player Evidence Reference

## Purpose
Use external evidence to improve decisions without letting precedent replace project goals. Research should answer **targeted uncertainties defined during problem framing**.

## Comparable selection
Search by mechanical/design problem first, genre second.
Examples:
- energy allocation can learn from space sims, mech heat systems, submarine resource management, or tactical action economies;
- persistent procedural worlds can learn from games with dormant simulation, discovery state, streaming, and versioned world data even when the genres differ.

## Source hierarchy
### Tier A — primary/authoritative
Developer documentation, GDC/conference talks, postmortems, official manuals, patch notes, public source/docs.
Use for factual behavior and developer-stated intent.

### Tier B — rigorous secondary
Established game-development publications, technical/design interviews, well-supported analysis.

### Tier C — player/community evidence
Steam/platform reviews, Reddit, official/community forums, videos/comments, public Discord discussions when methodologically useful.
Use for player experience, confusion, praise, friction, meta behavior—not claims about internal implementation.

## Player-feedback classification
Distinguish:
- **Preference mismatch** — player dislikes the category itself.
- **Execution problem** — feedback/readability/controls/pacing fail.
- **Balance/meta problem** — dominant strategy or incentive breaks variety.
- **Expectation mismatch** — system contradicts genre/store/tutorial/player expectation.

Always look for positive evidence too: satisfying mastery, memorable stories, constraints veterans defend, feedback players praise.

## Version awareness
Player evidence may refer to obsolete versions. Record dates/patches when behavior changed. Do not merge contradictory versions into false consensus.

## Evidence quality
Label conclusions as appropriate:
- direct developer statement;
- directly observable/documented behavior;
- recurring player pattern;
- isolated anecdote;
- analyst inference.

Confidence:
- HIGH — direct/triangulated evidence;
- MEDIUM — useful evidence with uncertainty;
- LOW — sparse/conflicting/heavily inferred.

## Counterevidence
Before a consequential recommendation, seek at least one strong reason the opposite choice might work or one comparable where the opposite choice succeeded. The goal is to reduce confirmation bias.

## Research output
A useful research brief ends with:
1. design questions researched;
2. evidence summary;
3. player feedback patterns;
4. counterevidence;
5. version caveats;
6. transferable lessons;
7. project-specific non-transfers;
8. design hypotheses that still need direct validation.
