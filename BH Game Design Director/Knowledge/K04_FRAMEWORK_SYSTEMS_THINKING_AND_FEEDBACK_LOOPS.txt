Framework invocation rule
Use this framework when its triggering conditions match the design problem. Do not force every problem
through every framework. When several frameworks apply, state which framework owns which question and
reconcile conflicts in a final synthesis pass.

Custom GPT operating directive
When to invoke systems thinking
Invoke this framework when multiple mechanics interact, a balance problem keeps reappearing after number
tuning, player behavior creates second-order effects, the game contains economies/factions/ecologies/AI
populations, or the team needs to predict unintended consequences before implementation.

• Define the system boundary before analyzing causality. Include enough context to capture the behavior, but not
every feature in the game.
• Represent variables as things that can increase, decrease, accumulate, deplete, activate, or change state.
• Distinguish reinforcing loops from balancing loops. Reinforcing does not mean good; balancing does not mean bad.
• Look for delays between cause and effect. Delays are a major source of overshoot, oscillation, instability, and player
confusion.
• Prefer structural explanations over parameter explanations when a problem returns across many tuning passes.

Systemic thinking
Michael Sellers defines systemic thinking as an approach between reductionist and purely holistic thinking: identify the
parts of a system while also understanding how those parts affect one another in operational context. He emphasizes
that loops formed by these interactions are central to systemic game design. [SYS-1]

Part D

Part B

Behavior emerges from relationships

Part C

A systems designer therefore asks two questions at once: what are the parts? and what behavior does their
relationship produce over time? A combat economy, for example, is not just ammunition, health, enemies, and
rewards. It is the way pressure changes resource use, resource use changes survival, survival changes reward, and
reward changes future power.

Causal links and loop polarity
A causal loop diagram uses arrows to show the direction of influence between variables. A positive link means the
variables move in the same direction relative to what they otherwise would have done; a negative link means they
move in opposite directions. The sign describes the relationship, not whether the outcome is desirable. [SYS-4]

The safer way to determine loop type is to trace a small change all the way around the loop. If the original change
comes back amplified, the loop is reinforcing. If the result opposes the original change, the loop is balancing. [SYS-4]

Loop labels describe behavior, not whether the effect is morally good or bad.

Reinforcing loops generate growth or decline: success can create resources that create more success, or damage can
reduce capability and cause more damage. Balancing loops resist change, pursue a target, or constrain growth. Ian
Schreiber and Ernest Adams both discuss how feedback structure shapes competitive balance: snowballing can make
leads self-strengthening, while catch-up or regulatory mechanics can counteract runaway advantage. [SYS-2] [SYS-3]
• Useful reinforcing behavior: mastery acceleration, engine building, escalating commitment, economic growth, risk
escalation.
• Dangerous reinforcing behavior: irreversible early leads, poverty traps, death spirals, dominant-strategy
compounding.
• Useful balancing behavior: heat regulation, diminishing returns, scarcity responses, catch-up pressure, capacity
limits.
• Dangerous balancing behavior: rubber-banding so strong that earlier decisions feel meaningless, hard caps that
erase strategy, auto-correction that removes agency.

Stocks, flows, and delays
Donella Meadows describes stocks as accumulations and flows as the rates that increase or decrease them. In game
terms, health, money, ammo reserves, reputation, threat, population, inventory, and political influence can all behave
as stocks. Income, damage, regeneration, production, spending, attrition, and decay are flows. [SYS-5]

Stock-flow thinking reveals accumulation, depletion, and control.

Delays matter because the system may react to old information or because an intervention takes time to affect the
stock. A delayed market response can cause price oscillation; delayed reinforcements can cause strategic overshoot;
delayed feedback to the player can make a fair rule feel arbitrary. Meadows identifies feedback strength, information
flows, rules, goals, and delays as potential leverage points in system behavior. [SYS-5]

Emergence and behavior over time
System behavior should be described over time, not only at a single snapshot. Useful questions include: Does the
value converge, oscillate, explode, collapse, plateau, or settle into cycles? Does the player learn to manipulate the
loop? Does the system produce multiple stable strategies or one attractor that dominates? Does adding content
change the system or merely feed an existing loop?

Leverage points for design changes
A common tuning failure is changing parameters when the real problem is structural. Meadows' leverage-point work
provides a useful hierarchy: parameters and constants are relatively shallow interventions; deeper leverage can come
from buffers, flow structure, delays, feedback strength, information access, rules, self-organization, and system goals.
[SYS-5]
• Parameter: change damage from 10 to 8. Fast and cheap, but often shallow.
• Flow: change when or how resources enter/leave the system.
• Delay: shorten or lengthen response time so players can adapt before overshoot.
• Feedback strength: alter how strongly success or failure compounds.
• Information: reveal a hidden state so players can respond before the system crosses a threshold.
• Rule: change eligibility, ownership, conversion, caps, transfer, or interaction logic.
• Goal: change what the system is optimizing or what the player is rewarded for optimizing.
Diagnosis rule
If repeated number tuning keeps recreating the same unwanted player behavior, stop tuning coefficients and
redraw the causal structure. The lever may be a connection, delay, information rule, or objective rather than a
value.

Step

Action

1. Define the design
question

State the behavior to explain or predict, not merely the feature to document.

2. Set the system

List what is inside the analysis and what is intentionally excluded.

3. Identify variables /
stocks

Use measurable or directionally meaningful nouns such as wealth, threat, population,

4. Add causal links

For each link, state why A changes B and assign polarity only after defining both variables.

5. Close loops

Find reinforcing and balancing cycles. Trace each loop to verify its type.

6. Mark delays and
thresholds

Identify where information or action arrives late and where behavior changes nonlinearly.

7. Predict behavior over
time

State whether the structure should grow, shrink, oscillate, stabilize, or switch regimes.

8. Red-team the system

Look for exploits, runaway accumulation, death spirals, lock-in, griefing, and edge cases.

9. Choose leverage points

Prefer the smallest structural intervention that changes the unwanted behavior while preserving
the target experience.

10. Validate

Prototype, simulate, or instrument the system and compare observed behavior to the causal
hypothesis.

Multi-persona team operating pattern
This section describes how to apply Systems Thinking and Feedback Loops inside an existing multi-persona design
committee. These are responsibility overlays, not a requirement to create new permanent personas. Map each
responsibility onto the personas already present in the team.
• Independent first pass. Relevant personas analyze the same design question independently before seeing the
synthesis. This reduces premature consensus.
• Evidence/assumption separation. Every persona labels what is known, inferred, assumed, or proposed. Numerical
claims without evidence are treated as hypotheses.
• Constructive disagreement. At least one persona must challenge the current favorite solution and identify a
plausible failure mode or player exploit.
• Framework vocabulary. Personas use the framework terms precisely. If a term is ambiguous, they define it before
using it as a decision criterion.
• Convergence pass. A synthesis lead resolves conflicts by returning to player goals, constraints, and evidence - not
by averaging opinions.
• Traceable output. Final recommendations include why the framework supports the decision, what remains uncertain,
and what playtest or simulation would falsify it.

Prompt patterns for AI-assisted design
The following patterns are designed to be reusable inside a custom GPT. Replace bracketed fields with project
context. The prompts explicitly separate framing, independent analysis, criticism, and convergence.

Pattern A - Framework invocation
Use Systems Thinking and Feedback Loops to analyze [SYSTEM OR MECHANIC].
Context: [PLAYER, PLATFORM, GENRE, DESIGN PILLARS, CONSTRAINTS].
Decision to make: [EXACT DECISION].
Known evidence: [PLAYTEST DATA / TELEMETRY / DESIGN FACTS].
Unknowns: [UNCERTAINTIES].
Each relevant designer persona must:
1. Apply the framework terminology explicitly.
2. Separate observations from assumptions.
3. Identify at least one failure mode.
4. Recommend the smallest design change that could test the hypothesis.
5. State what evidence would change the recommendation.
Do not converge until independent persona passes are complete.

Pattern B - Cross-examination
Cross-examine the current design using Systems Thinking and Feedback Loops.
Ask and answer these questions:
- What system behavior are we trying to explain over time?
- Which variables are stocks, which are flows, and which are control or information
variables?
- What reinforcing loops amplify change? What balancing loops resist it?
- Where are the delays, thresholds, nonlinearities, or missing information links?
- Is the proposed fix changing a parameter when the root cause is structural?
For every concern, identify:
- severity: low / medium / high
- confidence: low / medium / high
- affected player behavior or experience
- likely root cause
- cheapest validation method
Do not propose feature additions until root causes are identified.

Pattern C - Convergence and decision record
Synthesize the persona analyses into one design decision.
Resolve conflicts by using the project pillars, player needs, constraints, and evidence.
Do not average incompatible recommendations.
Return exactly this structure:
1. Design question and system boundary
2. Variables / stocks / flows
3. Causal links with polarity and rationale
4. Reinforcing loops
5. Balancing loops
6. Delays, thresholds, and nonlinearities
7. Predicted behavior over time
8. Exploits and failure modes
9. Candidate leverage points
10. Recommended intervention
11. Validation / simulation plan
If evidence is insufficient, mark the decision PROVISIONAL and specify the experiment
needed.

Worked example - territory and faction power
Consider a strategy system where controlling territory produces income, income buys units, and units capture territory.
That structure forms a reinforcing loop. If left unchecked, a small early lead can become an irreversible strategic
advantage.
Variable / link

Effect

Territory +-> Income

More controlled territory increases economic production.

Income +-> Military power

More resources support more or better units.

Military power +-> Territory

Stronger forces increase capture and defense capability.

Territory +-> Administration burden

Expansion increases logistics, unrest, or control cost.

Administration burden --> Effective
power

Rising burden reduces the advantage of additional territory.

Variable / link

Effect

Enemy threat +-> Coalition pressure

A dominant faction causes rivals to coordinate or receive new opportunities.

Instead of immediately reducing income per territory, the team can compare leverage points: introduce logistical delay,
add information to help weaker factions target supply lines, change capture rules, add diminishing control efficiency, or
create coalition dynamics. Each intervention produces different player experiences even if all reduce snowballing.

Failure modes and misuse
• Positive = good / negative = bad. Feedback terminology describes amplification and correction, not value judgment.
• Polarity by intuition. Link signs are often wrong when variable names are vague. Define variables precisely and
trace a small change.
• Ignoring delay. Many unstable systems appear balanced in a static spreadsheet because the time dimension is
missing.
• Parameter tunnel vision. Repeatedly changing constants while leaving the loop structure unchanged.
• Model everything. Over-expanding the boundary until the diagram becomes unreadable and no decision can be
made.
• Missing player adaptation. A system that is stable under passive simulation may destabilize once players learn to
manipulate it.
• False precision. A causal diagram is a hypothesis about structure; it does not prove magnitude without data or
simulation.

Review gate and quality checklist
A system should not be considered reviewed under Systems Thinking and Feedback Loops until the team can
answer the following checks with evidence or an explicit test plan.
• Is the design question expressed as behavior over time rather than a static feature description?
• Are key accumulations identified as stocks and their inflows/outflows understood?
• Can every causal link be explained in a complete sentence with a direction of effect?
• Have reinforcing and balancing loops been traced rather than guessed?
• Are important delays, thresholds, caps, or regime changes visible in the model?
• Has at least one optimizing or adversarial player strategy been applied to the system?
• Does the proposed intervention target a plausible leverage point rather than only a convenient number?
• Is there a plan to observe, simulate, or instrument the predicted behavior over time?
AI behavior rule
If a required input is missing, the personas should not silently invent it. They should either state a bounded
assumption and continue, or mark the analysis as blocked when that assumption would materially change the
decision.

How this framework combines with the other four

independently correct.
