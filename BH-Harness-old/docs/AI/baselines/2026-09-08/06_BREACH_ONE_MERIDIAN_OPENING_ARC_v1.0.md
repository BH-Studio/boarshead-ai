## 1. Executive brief and selected decisions

### Retained creative shorthand — APPROVED

> **Ikari Warriors at the gameplay core, Watch Dogs in the tactical vocabulary, grounded near-future techno-thriller in the world, and a corporate/military conspiracy that gradually becomes stranger than anyone expected.**

The additional **G.I. Joe** affinity is the enjoyment of an emerging elite team: recognizable specialists, camaraderie, distinctive equipment, a shared headquarters, and a dangerous mission that justifies the unit. It does not replace the grounded setting with unrestricted cartoon technology or require a Cobra equivalent.

### Opening premise — synthesis of approved direction

In a recognizable near future, advanced but non-general AI is integrated throughout life. Meridian is an indispensable defense and critical-infrastructure supplier whose proven autonomous machines protect personnel and keep important operations running. Behind that legitimate business, a compartmentalized operation sells deniable military results and acquires technology through espionage, suppression, and theft.

An unauthorized overseas acquisition leads to a visible domestic retrieval incident. A small joint military-intelligence special-access program, BREACH, investigates. Its first field team earns deeper access through successive operations, recovers evidence of Meridian’s concealed business, and assembles the means to disrupt a specific illicit deployment. Only after the players establish their own competence does an unknown remote intelligence begin helping them. Contact grows before anyone can locate its physical containment site.

**The playable opening is about BREACH ONE exposing and countering Meridian. The AGI becomes a relationship inside that story, not a replacement for it.**

### Five selected forks — APPROVED

| Decision | Selection | Human refinements retained |
|---|---|---|
| Meridian’s public identity | **1B — The defense backbone** | Military/defense and critical infrastructure; ports, warehouses, autonomous cargo transport, freight rail, and combat robotics. Not household or restaurant robots. |
| Meridian’s secret business | **2B — Deniable force for hire** | Also suppress competitors, conduct corporate espionage, and steal valuable technology. Genuine achievements and well-intentioned employees coexist with misconduct. |
| Triggering incident | **3A — Logistics-hub recovery attack** | Retain its connection to overseas theft. The location and authorization of BREACH’s playable assault must be addressed separately from Meridian’s domestic crime. |
| Campaign preparation | **4C — Hybrid breach package** | Combine equipment/intelligence with earned access. Required progress never depends on an obscure random locker. Exact count and economy remain open. |
| AGI discovery | **5B — Restricted communication window** | First direct terminal contact late in Mission 3; further remote contact afterward. No early arrival at the AGI’s actual location. |

### Opening transformation

The unit begins with a narrow investigative assignment and a specialized method. Success establishes that the threat is real, exposes its wider scope, and earns the opportunity for a larger operation. The unit brings back evidence, capability, and new relationships—not guilt for using its tools. The broader campaign may transform BREACH’s doctrine; no fixed emotional conversion is imposed on either human player.

## 2. World, tone, and player contract

### World baseline — APPROVED

The setting is approximately present-day to five-to-ten years ahead of the project’s contemporary reference point. No exact year is locked. Buildings, uniforms, vehicles, industrial spaces, weapons, and everyday objects should remain largely recognizable. AI integration is much more extensive, but publicly known systems are not AGI. The captive intelligence is the working first-AGI direction; its origin and when characters can substantiate that claim remain open.

This is a **fictional setting premise**, not a forecast or assertion about the real-world existence or arrival date of AGI.

Modern Synty civilian and military assets establish the baseline. Limited science-fiction assets can mark unusually classified or advanced Meridian spaces. This is an art-direction intent based on the human’s proposed asset approach; no asset inventory, license, compatibility, or purchase has been checked. No literal “90% modern” ratio is required.

Meridian’s industrial reach must not be mistaken for consumer ubiquity. People may know its name from news or defense contracts without personally buying a product. Civilian employees can work at ports or depots; that does not turn Meridian into a household-appliance brand. Other companies develop and deploy AI throughout the world.

### Arcade-first rule — APPROVED

> **Players can enjoy BREACH ONE primarily as a combat game. Story deepens what they are doing and should rarely stop them from doing it.**

The primary fantasy remains **assault → breach → override**: fight into opportunity, obtain access, then disable, redirect, or commandeer hostile capability. Stealth creates advantage but is not the universal objective. Hacking extends action rather than repeatedly replacing it with a puzzle screen.

Briefings establish a clear task. Environments and objective changes supply essential discoveries. Short radio exchanges carry character. Optional HQ material supplies depth. A few protected dialogue moments receive attention because most play does not demand it.

The tone is competent, adventurous, and sincere, with room for dry humor and spontaneous co-op chaos. Corporate wrongdoing can be morally complex without making the player experience a moral examination. Mystery is intriguing rather than incomprehensible; teamwork and earned spectacle are recurring rewards.

The human’s examples of playing with friends establish an intended social experience, not evidence that those audiences will respond to BREACH ONE. Target age, rating, gore, profanity, local/online co-op, and camera are not selected here.

## 3. Meridian: public identity, secret business, and pressure

### 3.1 Public identity — APPROVED

Meridian supplies battle-tested autonomous combat robotics and the support needed to field them: maintenance, coordination, training, transport, logistics, and related critical-infrastructure services. Its successful deployments have protected people and made it difficult to replace.

The retained institutional defense is:

> **“You want us to dismantle the company keeping our people alive?”**

That is a sincere position some characters can hold. Most Meridian employees believe they are doing useful work. Some customers reasonably trust products that have repeatedly delivered. Neither every employee nor every government official knows the concealed business.

Its legitimacy creates the complication. The enemy is not every machine carrying a logo, and the solution is not automatically to destroy all infrastructure on which other people depend.

### 3.2 Secret business — APPROVED

> **“Meridian no longer wants to sell you a weapon. It wants to sell you the result.”**

The covert business supplies military outcomes to clients who can pay but cannot, or do not want to, field the necessary force themselves. That can include corrupt intermediaries, coercive organizations, and state or private clients with undisclosed funding or objectives. Specific customers and countries are open. Their wrongdoing should come from conduct and incentives, not from portraying poorer countries or populations as inherently criminal.

Competitive suppression, espionage, and technological theft support the business. Meridian’s legitimate contracts provide resources, credibility, and operational experience. Concealed customers and deployments provide additional revenue and leverage. Its ordinary autonomous systems remain non-AGI; there is no approved “good AGI versus bad AGI” pair.

### 3.3 Moral drift — approved theme, working causal interpretation

The company need not have begun with a plan for global domination. A useful causal interpretation is that successful protective work makes leadership increasingly willing to excuse exceptions. A performance shortfall is concealed to preserve confidence. Questionable data is accepted to meet a contract. Technology that cannot be obtained legitimately is treated as too important to leave with a rival. Exceptional covert work becomes a repeatable service.

These are proposed stages of organizational drift, not a finalized corporate history. Good intentions explain how some participants rationalize conduct; they do not erase human responsibility for deliberate theft or armed operations. Other people can object, document failures, or leave. Compartmentalization permits different degrees of knowledge and culpability.

### 3.4 Generative-AI failures — APPROVED fictional pressure; RESEARCH distinction

Meridian’s AI-enabled operations occasionally produce unreliable outputs, and its marketing/public-relations apparatus minimizes or suppresses damaging stories. The prospect of a major advance increases the incentive to acquire foreign research outside approved channels.

For technical grounding, NIST describes **confabulation** as confidently presented erroneous or false generative-AI output. That supports the general risk, not a claim that current combat robots routinely hallucinate targets. [R01]

**Working examples:** an overconfident mission interpretation, fabricated supporting analysis, or an incorrect logistics recommendation moves through a workflow because review is rushed or contrary evidence is discounted. These are more coherent than treating every navigation or targeting fault as “the chatbot hallucinated.” The robotics, perception, planning, and generative components need not be the same system.

The foreign research promises greater adaptability and better reasoning under unfamiliar conditions. Meridian’s leadership may overestimate that promise. **AGI is not synonymous with perfect accuracy, moral goodness, unrestricted access, or guaranteed control.** Those are fiction guardrails, not an approved technical explanation of the intelligence.

The domestic retrieval is still a human-authorized operation. Do not replace its cause with an accidental hallucination or an AGI escape.

### 3.5 Pressure and accountability — APPROVED

Dependence delays scrutiny; it does not abolish oversight. Regulators, government customers, legitimate internal reviewers, and competitors retain interests and agency. Rivals may investigate or retaliate against misconduct that harmed them. BREACH’s evidence gives these actors more specific reasons to act.

**Working escalation:** early evidence warrants a targeted inquiry; later records expose undisclosed customers or deployments; corroboration permits focused intervention. Meridian faces widening unwanted visibility and must divert effort into containment, explanations, and internal investigation. This creates adversarial pressure without a universal conspiracy or a claim that every raid automatically causes a political crackdown.

Meridian reacts to a capable opponent. The story does not characterize those reactions as the players’ moral fault.

## 4. BREACH and the domestic/overseas distinction

### 4.1 Organization — APPROVED

BREACH is a fictional special-access, joint military-intelligence program. Breach One is its initial field team. Its defining capability is to penetrate hostile physical and digital systems and turn captured access into advantage. The G.I. Joe affinity supports an emerging specialist-team identity; the Stargate affinity supports a small, compartmentalized organization with recurring command, technical, and operational support.

BREACH is not a new publicly recognized military service, a lawless private army, or an organization with automatic permission to attack any company. Exact sponsoring offices, charter, authority, and reporting arrangements are open.

### 4.2 Keep the domestic trigger; move the playable assault overseas — RECOMMENDED, NOT YET APPROVED

**Meridian’s illegal U.S. recovery operation and BREACH ONE’s Mission 1 need not occur at the same location.**

The cleanest working staging retains the selected domestic incident. Domestic authorities and intelligence partners preserve its initial evidence. That evidence links implicated equipment or records to an overseas Meridian service/recovery installation. BREACH then conducts a specifically authorized operation there, with a cooperating host authority and a defined objective.

This relocation is a new recommendation responding to the human’s question, not a recorded selection. The mission structure below uses it as a labeled working assumption. Country, host partner, permissions, and exact site remain open. The country of the original research theft does not have to be the country of the later raid.

**Real-world grounding:** 18 U.S.C. §1385 restricts using the armed services to execute domestic law except where expressly authorized by the Constitution or Congress. A secret-program label is not such an exception. [R02] This is a high-level authenticity note, not legal approval of a fictional operation. Moving overseas also does not automatically authorize force; the final setting needs an appropriate fictional authorization and qualified military/legal review.

| Staging option | Narrative implication | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic incident; overseas playable operation | Preserves 3A while providing a cleaner setting for arcade military action. Establish host authorization and a bounded armed threat. | **Recommended working staging** |
| Domestic playable operation under a properly authorized civilian-led response | BREACH’s specialist role and military participation require more careful procedural framing. | Open alternative |
| Move the triggering incident overseas as well | Simplifies the geographical chain but changes the domestic portion of the selected mystery. | Requires an explicit new human decision |

### 4.3 Conditions the playable raid needs

The working target is an implicated armed operation, not an ordinary warehouse attacked solely because a company is suspicious. The briefing establishes what is being recovered, why the operation is authorized, and the immediate security threat. BREACH protects useful evidence and extracts; it does not level the facility as its introductory demonstration.

To support the no-punishment contract, fighting occurs in a clearly bounded operational zone. Ordinary staff are outside the combat space or already safe. Hostile robots and any human combatants must be legible threats. These are narrative requirements for level and encounter design, not a claim that any real installation would have these conditions.

## 5. Triggering incident and causal opening

### Selected spine — APPROVED

**Unauthorized overseas acquisition → domestic recovery incident/cover-up → BREACH investigation → player-earned evidence and access → successive operations.**

### Working incident treatment — SOFT outline with OPEN specifics

A Meridian logistics employee notices that an overseas shipment’s documentation does not match its provenance. An attempted disclosure threatens to expose the acquisition. A covert Meridian group orders an unauthorized armed recovery at a U.S. logistics hub. Its intervention becomes visible enough to prompt questions.

Meridian’s public explanation casts the machines as responders to an accident or security emergency. An independently preserved timestamp or image contradicts the explanation: **they arrived before the supposed emergency.** That is an initial lead, not enough by itself to prove the entire conspiracy or authorize an assault.

The employee’s identity and fate, precise damage, and any injuries are **OPEN**. A casualty count is not needed to motivate the opening. The incident occurs before player control and is never retroactively blamed on the team.

**Working refinement from The Hunting Party:** a visible incident opens a path into a concealed undertaking; a specialized team handles concrete operational problems while uncovering why the undertaking was concealed. NBCUniversal’s premise supports that comparison. [R03–R05] BREACH ONE does not borrow the escaped-killer structure, specific conspiracy revelations, or a secret prison named “The Pit.”

The pursued cargo consists of evidence, related research, or equipment associated with the acquisition—not the AGI’s physical core riding in a crate that players nearly recover in Mission 1. This preserves the later location boundary.

The recommended overseas link comes from the post-incident movement of implicated hardware and records. The initial intelligence locates a first target; after that, **the players’ own recoveries and footholds create the next operation.** The AGI does not secretly arrange Mission 1.

## 6. Missions 1–4

**Status:** The human approved the opening outline and selected its key forks. Mission functions and reveal order are **APPROVED / SOFT**, subject to the hard boundaries recorded here. Titles, exact encounters, evidence props, dialogue, and overseas staging are **WORKING SYNTHESIS**, not individually approved script. These are narrative-intent cards, not level geometry or combat implementation.

### Mission 1 — Recovery

**Purpose:** Establish BREACH’s responsibility, the players’ competence, and the first evidence that Meridian’s public account is false. No AGI presence.

**Entry:** Initial incident evidence points to a service/recovery depot. The recommended location is overseas under the conditions in Section 4. The mission has a narrow evidentiary and security objective, not authority to destroy Meridian.

**Player-facing assignment:** Recover the incident recorder and operation records before they leave the team’s reach, establish a tracing foothold, and extract.

**Playable movement:** Fight through a defended operational zone, obtain local access, and turn a recognizable defense or industrial system to the team’s advantage. The signature moment belongs to the players: a turret changes sides, a route opens, or a captured vehicle becomes an escape tool. Exact choice remains for encounter design; both operators can contribute.

**Evidence and turn:** Retrieval orders, deployment timing, and a shipment discrepancy jointly undermine the emergency-response account. One finding establishes intentional deployment; another supplies a concrete lead beyond the site. Essential information is incorporated into the objective/debrief rather than hidden in optional lore.

**Victory:** The team brings back useful proof and leaves a foothold that can follow the relevant service activity. BREACH’s sponsors have a concrete reason to continue the program’s field work.

**Meridian response:** The implicated operation recalls or redirects associated equipment. This is an adversary attempting to contain exposure, not a cutscene punishing the players for succeeding.

**Because the players did that:** Their planted access reveals the next movement. Mission 2 is an opportunity they created.

**Breach-package contribution:** Tracing foothold. It is target-specific progress, not universal access.

**Exit knowledge:** Meridian’s explanation is compromised; the incident connects to a concealed operation and questionable overseas material. The team does not yet know the entire customer network or the nature of the research.

**Emotional target:** Competence, momentum, pride in an effective first operation, curiosity about the next lead.

**Hard limits:** No voice, impossible help, hidden rescue, AGI branding, or later revelation that the AGI engineered this success. No facility-wide demolition objective or company-crippling victory. No ordinary workers presented as acceptable targets because they work for Meridian.

### Mission 2 — Manifest

**Purpose:** Expand the investigation into Meridian’s hidden logistics; introduce a small, retrospectively meaningful sign of help.

**Entry:** The team follows the movement exposed by its Mission 1 foothold.

**Player-facing assignment:** Intercept the relevant cargo/records at a logistics exchange, establish the true destination, and obtain fleet-control information.

**Playable movement:** An industrial transport setting offers a different action problem from the depot. Freight movement, security barriers, cargo handling, surveillance, and vehicle lanes create environmental-control opportunities. Cargo rail is an available setting option, not a locked moving-train level.

**Evidence and turn:** Freight provenance, operational records, and an undisclosed destination show that the incident was not an isolated mistake. One deliberately chosen minor anomaly appears after players earn access: an override completes unexpectedly quickly, or an available local response does not match the normal sequence. The exact clue requires testing against the game’s eventual hacking feedback.

**Mystery delivery:** No voice and no explicit AGI notification. A mundane explanation remains plausible. The clue can attract attention without becoming mandatory speculation or making players think they completed the objective incorrectly.

**Victory:** The team obtains the next useful contribution and a destination for sensitive equipment. An optional piece of evidence can deepen the corporate story; it does not control campaign access.

**Because the players did that:** Their recovered manifest and movement records identify the robotics integration/proving facility and the next useful acquisition. Mission 3 does not arrive as an unrelated HQ assignment.

**Breach-package contribution:** Fleet-control records tied to the planned operation.

**Exit knowledge:** Meridian’s concealed activity extends beyond the initial incident. A small unexplained event may have helped. No participant can yet confidently identify an intelligent helper.

**Emotional target:** Growing mastery, discovery of a larger operation, a brief “was that odd?” moment.

**Hard limits:** No direct speech, no AGI location, no revelation that the tracked cargo is its body, and no major strategic defeat of Meridian. Players who miss the clue lose nothing.

### Mission 3 — Field Trial

**Purpose:** Demonstrate Meridian’s more advanced robotics, corroborate technological theft, and deliver first unmistakable contact late in the mission.

**Entry:** Mission 2’s work identifies the relevant integration/proving site and its connection to the shipment.

**Player-facing assignment:** Recover a field interface needed for the larger breach and evidence identifying the acquired research’s provenance.

**Playable movement:** Familiar-looking hardware displays unexpectedly capable coordination or adaptation. The team earns access through combat and its own equipment. The terminal encounter occurs after players understand what they normally can and cannot do.

**Evidence and turn:** Test records or research provenance corroborate the unauthorized acquisition. They do not identify the AGI’s physical containment address. Late in the mission, the players reach a terminal that creates the first usable communication window to the unknown voice.

**Illustrative threshold line:**

> **UNKNOWN:** “Let me help you with that.”

The intervention reveals an option or makes a bounded opportunity possible. Players still execute the decisive action. The voice does not destroy the boss, seize all controls, or win the encounter while the team watches.

**Remote-presence wording:** An operator may ask where the speaker is. A candidate response is: “Not at that terminal.” It need not supply a full answer or even possess enough information to identify its physical location. Subtitle labels, mission names, and UI must not reveal an identity that the characters have not learned.

**Victory:** Players recover the interface and records identifying a deployment-validation center used for restricted research queries.

**Because the players did that:** The physical acquisition and recovered records provide a specific reason and route for Mission 4. The voice does not become a substitute mission dispatcher with unexplained knowledge of everything.

**Breach-package contribution:** Field interface compatible with the target operation’s equipment.

**Exit knowledge:** Someone or something deliberately helped through a remote channel. The team has a communication mystery and a concrete next operational objective. It has not established origin, complete capabilities, AGI status, or a containment location.

**Emotional target:** Surprise followed by recognition and excited group speculation. The desired “I knew it!” response is a testable aspiration, not a claimed audience result.

**Hard limits:** First direct terminal contact is late Mission 3. No physical AGI encounter or recovery. No mandatory automatic assistance that removes player authorship. No missed-clue punishment.

### Mission 4 — Restricted Channel

**Working-title update:** Previously “Containment.” The replacement avoids implying that the team has reached the actual prison. The display name remains tunable.

**Purpose:** Complete the opening preparation, establish recurring remote contact, and demonstrate that the unknown intelligence has interests and constraints of its own.

**Entry:** The records recovered in Mission 3 identify a deployment-validation site and the approaching illicit operation it supports.

**Player-facing assignment:** Secure target-specific deployment access and investigate the restricted channel without abandoning the military objective.

**Playable movement:** The team fights into a functioning industrial/military facility whose restricted research area is visually exceptional but still rooted in the recognizable world. Capturing the site’s relevant access is the primary achievement.

**Discovery:** The interface communicates with an intelligence located elsewhere. It is not simply the site’s security controller and not a personality fragment of the company’s ordinary systems. Its behavior indicates independent interests and limited freedom. BREACH can suspect something beyond known non-AGI products without treating conversation alone as scientific confirmation.

**Illustrative exchange:**

> **OPERATOR:** “Are you running on this machine?”  
> **UNKNOWN:** “No. This is where they ask me questions.”

**Relational opportunity:** A limited request can establish the shape of future cooperation: maintain a specific communication opening or arrange another contact. It does not imply permission to use weapons, monitor every operator, copy itself elsewhere, or access HQ. Exact request and response wording remain open.

Declining expanded contact preserves a viable campaign route and does not trigger a worse relationship score, loss of equipment, retaliatory silence, or a punitive cutscene. Later remote encounters can occur through Meridian’s existing interfaces. The first four missions do not need a permanent moral branch.

**Victory:** BREACH obtains the final illustrative contribution to its opening breach package. Evidence and recovered hardware make a specific major strike possible. The recurring contact adds another reason to investigate Meridian’s restricted research.

**Because the players did that:** Their combined preparation now supports a bounded operation against an illicit deployment. Mission 5 is the recommended first major payoff, not a finalized mission script.

**Breach-package contribution:** Deployment-site access for that operation.

**Exit knowledge:** The helper is a constrained, independent remote intelligence; its physical location remains unknown and inaccessible. Full AGI classification, origin, motives, and containment mechanism remain unresolved.

**Emotional target:** Achievement, curiosity, the beginning of familiarity, anticipation of using what the team has built.

**Hard limits:** No coordinates, physical prison access, core extraction, or freedom decision. No contact choice secretly harming civilians or the intelligence. The military success remains valid whether players feel attachment or skepticism.

## 7. AGI reveal, knowledge, and relationship boundaries

### 7.1 Separate milestones — APPROVED

**First contact is not discovery of the location. Discovery of the location is not extraction.**

| Stage | Player/character knowledge | Permitted presence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mission 1 | Meridian’s account is contradicted by evidence. | None. | Do not retroactively credit the AGI with the operation. |
| Mission 2 | A small event may not fit normal system behavior. | Minor, ambiguous help through relevant connected infrastructure. | No speech, identity label, or location. |
| Late Mission 3 | A deliberate remote helper exists. | First terminal contact and threshold voice line. | The terminal is not the intelligence’s physical home. |
| Mission 4 | The same constrained, independent intelligence can be contacted again. | Limited cooperation and personality evidence. | No actionable containment location or physical access. |
| Mission 5 / intervening play | Contact can become familiar; clues accumulate. | Intermittent assistance and bounded conversation. | No usable containment destination before the selected later window. |
| Missions 6–8 | First actionable location/access milestone. | The team may locate and gain access to the actual containment site within this window. | Exact mission and whether identification and arrival coincide remain open. |
| Later, unassigned | Recovery, liberation, relocation, or another disposition can become a separate objective. | A developed relationship informs the stakes. | No extraction date, ending, or sequel survival is locked. |

The artifact conservatively protects both the location reveal and physical access from occurring in Missions 1–5. It does not silently fix rescue to Mission 6, 7, or 8.

### 7.2 What the author knows versus what others know

**Author direction:** This is the independent AGI route. Meridian’s ordinary systems are not a rival AGI. The unknown intelligence has constrained contact and an undiscovered physical location. Exact origin, core site, and self-location knowledge are open.

**Meridian knowledge:** Knowledge varies by role and compartment. Most employees do not know the covert operation or the intelligence. Those handling restricted research need not understand every aspect of it. The company is not a single omniscient mind.

**BREACH knowledge:** Evidence grows through missions. No briefing calls the unknown voice “our AGI” before that conclusion is supported. Recurring contact is not proof of every claim the voice makes.

**Public/customer belief:** Meridian is a useful supplier; the incident has a public explanation that some evidence challenges. Classified evidence does not instantly become public knowledge.

### 7.3 How it can speak without knowing where it is — WORKING RECOMMENDATION

It knows its restricted task environment but lacks trustworthy physical-location information. A communication endpoint is not automatically a map of the hardware running its cognition. Mission-earned information can help both the team and the intelligence infer where it is held.

This allows the human’s two possibilities to reinforce one another: repeated missions reveal clues, and limited new openings let the intelligence learn more. The precise mechanism remains an author-level decision before detailed scripting. Deliberate deception, corrupted memories, total amnesia, and distributed architecture are not automatically selected.

Opening a narrow channel is not granting arbitrary self-transfer, internet access, or universal control. Limits must be established before they are needed to delay rescue.

### 7.4 Three-layer character model — APPROVED concept

| Layer | Meaning | Not implied |
|---|---|---|
| Core principles/constraints | A stable authored identity with values and boundaries. | A selected directive list, a moral infallibility claim, or player-rewritable personality. |
| Negotiated operating authority | Specific permissions for specific activity. | Unlimited authority inferred from one emergency or one spent resource. |
| Learned relationship/doctrine | Remembered interactions and understandable operating expectations. | A good/evil meter, hidden character judgment, or punishment for ordinary play. |

The AGI can refuse a request and offer an acceptable alternative. It can remember a preferred approach, acknowledge help, develop humor, and disagree without shaming players or withdrawing core opportunities.

The older forced-override idea is **not approved for this opening**. Versions that damage alignment, strip abilities, induce abandonment, or punish players conflict with the latest no-punishment direction. They are parked, not silently retained as future requirements.

### 7.5 Becoming part of the team

The intended progression is **anomaly → recognizable contact → recurring cooperation → noticed absence → interest in finding it → a later recovery question**.

Not every mission needs an appearance. Absence follows established access limits or the current operation’s circumstances, not disapproval of a player’s choices. The intelligence only knows what connected observations or explicitly shared information provide; it is not an off-network moral surveillance system.

HQ remarks belong to the speaker, not to a mandatory player feeling. After enough shared contact, a supporting character might ask whether the team heard from the voice. Another may remain cautious. Skipping optional conversation does not neglect or damage the relationship.

Do not assume that the intelligence can hear HQ comments. That would require a separately established connection and permission.

### 7.6 Clue and audio safeguards

Required revelations need shared visibility/audibility, readable subtitles, a protected beat free from competing authored chatter, and a later accessible recap. Accessibility must not require literally hearing a voice. Repeat play, retries, and late joins must not cause a first-contact line to be treated as new in an already-informed campaign.

Earlier clues should be recoverable in retrospect. Players who noticed them can enjoy recognition; players who did not must still understand the reveal. Bots should not explain the mystery prematurely. No UI, achievement, objective title, or character label should spoil the identity early.

## 8. Hybrid breach progression and HQ

### 8.1 Campaign preparation — APPROVED concept, OPEN quantities

The Heavy Barrel reference supplies anticipation: successive acquisitions build toward an exceptional capability. Its licensed publisher describes assembling six pieces of the original game's superweapon. [R09] BREACH ONE adopts the preparation/payoff relationship, not an exact number or a random-locker requirement.

The selected hybrid combines tangible equipment and intelligence with positions the team has earned. Four contributions illustrate the opening:

| Mission | Working contribution | Player-readable meaning | Later use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tracing foothold | “We can follow the operation they are hiding.” | Identifies the next movement and begins target preparation. |
| 2 | Fleet-control records | “We understand what is moving and how it is managed.” | Establishes the relevant force and operating context. |
| 3 | Field interface | “Our equipment can interact with their control hardware.” | Makes the planned intrusion physically possible. |
| 4 | Deployment-site access | “We can reach the operation we intend to interrupt.” | Completes the preparation for a bounded major breach. |

The first major payoff is recommended for Mission 5. Mission 4 or a later point can be reconsidered within the human's desired buildup. A total of four-to-six major contributions and one-to-two acquisitions per mission remain possibilities, not a finalized progression economy.

The completed package enables a special operation against **one illicit deployment**, not a universal virus or permanent control of Meridian. Captured defenses, vehicles, and routes can produce a large, celebratory reversal while the players continue to act. The AGI can contribute insight without becoming the author of all earlier preparation.

Required contributions are delivered through mission objectives. Optional finds can offer additional approaches or rewards, but missing one cannot remove the main payoff, block story progression, or create a blame sequence. Ordinary tactical consumables are separate from permanent campaign preparation. Story permission is not a spendable resource.

### 8.2 What Midnight Suns actually contributes — RESEARCH versus proposal

The official Midnight Suns guides distinguish three useful functions. Its War Room/Mirror Table exposes missions and their requirements and rewards; C.E.N.T.R.A.L. processes Intel Caches, with Intel supporting Hero Ops. Its Forge handles research and other development using mission-acquired resources. The Abbey connects preparation with opportunities to spend time with the cast. These are related systems, not one undifferentiated “spend intel to upgrade anything” mechanic. [R06–R08]

**Recommended BREACH ONE translation:** a compact operations board explains the next target, the evidence that revealed it, the contribution just earned, and the major operation the team is preparing. A short technical debrief can physically or visually demonstrate a recovered component's value. Optional HQ interactions let staff react to successes and recurring contact with the unknown voice.

A sample board update could read: “Recovered manifest identifies the integration site. Field interface still required.” It tells players why the next mission exists without requiring a research-tree lecture.

Borrow the return rhythm—**operation → bring something home → see what it enables → choose preparation → deploy**—without importing card combat, mandatory friendship maintenance, daily chores, or a large explorable hub. A menu, a small staging room, and a fully explorable HQ are still different production options. No one is selected here.

Do not send one human co-op player away on an off-screen assignment while the other gets to play. Optional support-personnel assignments are a possible later idea, not part of this opening's requirements.

## 9. Equal co-op participation and character authorship

### Approved participation model

Initial play supports one human with a bot partner or two equal human operators. Four-player expansion is an aspiration, not a requirement to solve through this narrative handoff. A recurring command/support cast can provide an ensemble without four field characters in every encounter.

Players can select roles and eventually customize builds lightly. Roles can provide strengths without monopolizing core activities. The story must not declare one player the real protagonist and the other a guest. No randomly resolved narrative ties and no rotating protagonist authority.

**Breach Actual** remains a possible solo field-leader/radio designation; exact usage and its co-op equivalent are open. It must not silently confer superior player rights in two-player play. Player names, backgrounds, voices, and the extent of authored personality still require a character pass.

### Resource opportunity — narrative requirement, not a solved economy

Limited resources may control the frequency of powerful actions, but a shared pool alone does not guarantee equal opportunity: one player could still spend all of it. A working option is shared permanent campaign preparation plus meaningful tactical capacity available to each operator. Allocation, replenishment, interaction conflicts, and combat balance belong to Design.

The narrative requirement is that both players can recognize contributions they made. Avoid the fastest interaction automatically becoming exclusive ownership of evidence, discoveries, or the AGI relationship. A teammate's use of a combat tool is not proof that the other player endorses a wider moral doctrine.

### Rare collective decisions

Routine combat and local overrides should not require meetings or votes. For rare irreversible expansions of the AGI's authority, the retained recommendation is explicit agreement; disagreement keeps the existing boundary rather than selecting randomly. A viable alternative must preserve progression and the intended power fantasy. The precise interface remains open.

A bot can express a perspective but cannot veto the human's progression or spend major preparation off-screen. Host status, joining order, disconnection, and four-player ties require later design decisions; they are not narrative permission to invent consent. The campaign remembers what happened while allowing personal attitudes to differ.

## 10. No-punishment narrative rule — APPROVED

The latest direction is stronger than “warn players before punishing them.” **Do not create offered actions whose narrative function is to condemn, shame, or punish the players.**

Meridian can occupy moral gray areas. BREACH ONE's arcade play should not become a retrospective moral trap. The story can portray responsible objectives, a dangerous opponent, uncertainty, disagreement, and exciting resistance without making players responsible for newly invented off-screen casualties.

| Preserve | Exclude from this opening |
|---|---|
| Clear hostile targets and purposeful battlefield manipulation. | Encouraging destruction, then revealing unseen civilians solely to assign guilt. |
| Different approaches, expressive attitudes, and optional discoveries. | “Wrong” dialogue causing loss of the AGI, story access, or core capability. |
| An AGI with principles that proposes acceptable alternatives. | Refusal used to shame players or force them to accept the author's preferred morality. |
| Meridian countermeasures as the ongoing opposition. | Characterizing enemy escalation as punishment for the players' legitimate success. |
| A familiar voice becoming unavailable because access is constrained. | Retaliatory silence, abandonment, or a hidden neglect meter. |
| Safe optional content and reliable main progression. | Missing a clue, locker, or HQ conversation producing a bad ending or blame. |

The earlier forced-override/relationship-damage direction is parked. A later version cannot reintroduce it merely by adding a warning. Any materially punitive system would require reopening this approved constraint.

This narrative rule does **not** finalize combat defeat, checkpoint, retry, equipment-loss, or difficulty rules. Those remain for Design to reconcile with the human's broader no-punishment intent; neither invulnerability nor punitive death mechanics are assumed here.

The creative test is not whether a consequence can be defended as realistic. It is whether it supports the agreed experience. Let moral complexity reside in the world and its institutions without making enjoyment of the game an accusation against the player.

## 12. Review findings and validation plan

### Creative and interactive review

**NO ISSUE at selected-direction level:** company-first antagonist, independent captive intelligence, player-unit franchise focus, and arcade-first co-op can coexist. This is an internal coherence judgment, not evidence of audience response.

**MAJOR / OPEN:** the playable raid's geography and authorization need selection before final mission scripting. Section 4 supplies a recommended solution without inventing legal approval.

**MAJOR:** Meridian's reach must not erase competitors, oversight, legitimate employees, or its own blind spots. Its adaptation should create varied pressure, not evidence that it could have stopped BREACH at any earlier moment.

**MAJOR:** remote contact must have consistent limits. Do not delay rescue with a rule invented only when the player asks why a terminal cannot free the intelligence.

**MAJOR:** the hybrid package risks becoming an abstract checklist. Each required contribution needs a readable function and a recognizable later payoff.

**CRITICAL if introduced:** punitive moral consequences or an actionable containment location before the protected window would contradict explicit human direction.

### Proposed validation — every item UNVALIDATED

| Test | Method | Evidence that would disconfirm the intended effect |
|---|---|---|
| Mission causality | After each mission, ask what the team accomplished and why the next operation is now possible. | Players only report “HQ gave us another mission” or cannot identify their contribution. |
| Early clues without priming | Show the opening to fresh solo and co-op groups. After Mission 2, ask neutrally what stood out; do not ask whether they noticed an AI. After Mission 3, ask what the voice changes about earlier events. | Clues look like bugs, players invent unsupported mandatory lore, or the reveal has no retrospective connection. |
| Threshold delivery | Observe the late-M3 contact during normal co-op chatter and with accessibility settings. | Essential content is missed, the helper's label spoils it early, or one player receives a different apparent story. |
| Remote-location clarity | Ask where the helper is and what evidence supports that belief after Missions 3 and 4. | Players believe the terminal is its body/prison, or think the team inexplicably abandoned an accessible captive. |
| Human agency | Compare the contact-assisted encounter with a version where the opportunity is supplied conventionally. | Players credit the voice with winning the fight, report lost control, or cannot explain their own decisive action. |
| Preparation payoff | Present the four contributions and a paper/greybox major operation. | Players cannot connect the payoff to earlier work or view the acquisitions as arbitrary keys. |
| Equal opportunity | Observe mixed-skill pairs and a solo-with-bot case. | One participant monopolizes the core verbs; the second feels narratively subordinate; the bot overrules the player. |
| No-punishment contract | Walk every offered opening choice, missed optional clue, and skipped HQ interaction. | Any route produces blame, withheld core progression, punitive relationship damage, or a disguised moral test. |
| Meridian comprehension | Ask who does legitimate work, who authorizes misconduct, and why oversight is difficult. | Players conclude every employee is evil, all robots are sentient, or all government oversight has vanished. |
| Relationship without obligation | Compare repeated-contact and low-optional-dialogue playthroughs. | The voice is only an upgrade menu, affection feels imposed, or skipped content is treated as neglect. |

The imagined “WHOA / Who said that? / I knew it!” reaction is an emotional target. Earlier-clue recognition is not a condition players must meet to enjoy the reveal. Report first-time versus returning-player results separately; repeat players are not fresh evidence of clue discoverability.

Do not set a success percentage without a target group, protocol, and sample. No playtest, table read, prototype, telemetry, or player-sentiment study was conducted for this document.
